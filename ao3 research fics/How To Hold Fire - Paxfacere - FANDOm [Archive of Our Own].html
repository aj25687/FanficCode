How To Hold Fire

Paxfacere

Summary:

Jet's gaze drops to the peach resting in his hand, his thumb brushing absently over the soft skin.

He is still here.

In the Fire Nation.

A thought that still has the peculiar power to bring everything to a standstill.

And apparently he's staying.

That may be the strangest thought of all.

He could leave. Technically. So why doesn't he?

Jet frowns down at the peach in his hand.

The answer is embarrassingly simple.

Zuko is here.

And so is Jet.

Oh, did Jet mention how much he hates this place?

Yeah...

[ Caldera, Port, Week five ]

 

The air is rich with salt, carried inland on a soft, wandering breeze that drifts across the harbor. The early afternoon sun is bright overhead, spilling its warmth across the water, scattering a trail of gold on the waves as ships rock gently on them beside the docks.

Wooden cargo ships lie docked at the nearer berths, their weathered sails darkened by years of salt and sun. Alongside them, boats carrying travelers from distant parts of the world, their decks and holds laden with unfamiliar goods and the remnants of long journeys past.

And scattered between: countless ships with their unmistakable dark metal hulls— vessels whose sight on the horizon would once have sent anyone running— crowded through the harbor in every single direction.

Above the forest of masts hangs a vast sea of flags. Reds, deep browns, muted greens stirring lazily in the breeze, snapping now and then before settling back into their gentle flutter.

Some bear familiar emblems. Others display symbols that speak of faraway nations and distant shores. Together, they form a shifting canopy of color above the harbor, each flag a quiet reminder of how many different roads— and seas— have converged upon this single place.

The port itself hums with life.

Workers call to one another across the docks. 

Sailors and soldiers alike weave between the vessels with the practiced ease of people who have spent half their lives walking sun-warmed decks.

Nearby, another cargo ship is in the final stages of docking. Thick ropes are already thrown ashore and pulled taut, straining against the ship's weight as workers hurry across the deck to secure her properly. Orders pass from one man to another, answered by shouted calls from the dock below. The vessel creaks softly as it settles against the harbor, surrendering inch by inch to the hands guiding her into place.

At the edge of the deck, officials have only just begun their inspection. Papers are unfolded, names checked, seals examined, and questions exchanged with the weary patience of men who have performed the same ritual a thousand times before.

 

Jet tosses a peach into the air absentmindedly and catches it again.

Up here, from his perch on a rooftop high above the harbor, he has an excellent view of the port below and everything that's happening there.

He leans back against a low chimney, one leg stretched out and the other bent, sending the peach lazily skyward once more.

Smellerbee sits beside him. 

Longshot has claimed the other side of her. 

And between them rests the basket of the peaches they picked up at the market on their way here, already looking lighter than when they arrived.

The late-summer sun warmly kisses their skin and the stone of the roof beneath them is pleasantly warm. 

The breeze is soft.

The view is good.

And there is nowhere they need to be right now.

There is no reason to move at all.

Jet's already started losing track of time, letting the afternoon drift past with nothing to mark it but the changing light on the waves and the occasional peach disappearing from the basket.

"The Earth Kingdom one," Smellerbee says.

She lifts a hand, pointing toward a wooden ship farther out in the harbor. Even at this distance, its colors are easy enough to make out: unmistakably green canvas stretched across the mast, shifting gently with the breeze. "That’s from Gaoling."

Jet follows her gaze, studying the distant vessel for a moment before his brow furrows. "Why Gaoling?" He glances sideways at her. 

Smellerbee only shrugs, entirely unbothered by the question. "Looks like Gaoling."

Jet turns his attention back to the ship. "That’s not an answer."

"It is if I’m right."

He snorts, shaking his head as he tosses the peach once more. This time, he catches it and finally takes a bite, the skin giving easily beneath his teeth. Sweet juice fills his mouth, the flesh soft and perfectly ripe—exactly as it should be.

He already knew a fresh shipment of peaches would arrive early that morning. And there is something satisfying about being proven right, even when the only thing he has to show for it is a good peach.

"And the next one?" Smellerbee points toward the broad cargo vessel beside her supposed Gaoling one. "What’s it delivering?"

Jet wipes his chin with the sleeve of his shirt before the juice has a chance to drip onto his clothes. He glances down to make sure he hasn't already stained himself, because the last thing he wants is to have to change his clothes for later just because he couldn't eat a peach without making a mess. Not that he particularly cares. 

He still can make an effort though.

"Weapons," Jet says then. "Obviously."

Smellerbee turns to him with a very particular look, the kind that makes it clear she already knew exactly what he was going to say. "It’s always weapons with you."

Jet grins at her. "Sure." He gestures lazily toward that one ship they have just watched through its docking process, its crew already getting off board. "But it’s all empty cargo anyway. There haven’t been that many delivery runs lately," he adds.

"That’s cheating," Smellerbee says immediately.

Jet raises an eyebrow. "How?"

"You looked at the ship."

"That's generally how this game works."

Though game might be overstating what they are doing.

There isn't really a score and there certainly aren't any rules that either of them can agree on for more than five minutes at a time. 

Mostly, it is just something they do to pass the afternoon: guessing where the ships have come from, where they are headed, what they are carrying, or whether the flags flying above them actually mean what they are supposed to mean.

Sometimes they get it right.

Most of the time they don't even have a way to check.

And every so often, one of them finds a way to make the other concede the point.

Longshot, naturally, is much better at it than either of them.

Smellerbee gives him a deeply unimpressed glare.

Jet still grins as he tosses his peach up in the air again, the fruit flashing in the sunlight.

This time, however, Smellerbee's elbow catches him at the exact wrong moment.

His hand closes on nothing, the peach slipping past his fingers. The half-eaten fruit lands unceremoniously between his legs before somehow finding the perfect angle to roll down the slope of the roof.

Jet watches, utterly taken off guard, as it vanishes over the edge.

"Seriously?" he mutters.

Beside him, Smellerbee sticks out her tongue, entirely unapologetic. 

She reaches into the little basket between them and plucks out a fresh peach as though she has absolutely no remorse about the previous one’s demise.

Longshot, uncaring about Jet's poor peach, finally contributes something, by pointing his hand.

Jet still takes another moment to glare at Bee, then he follows the line of his hand.

Another ship is making its way through the harbor, cutting steadily between the other ships— smaller than the cargo vessels but unmistakably in design. 

Something tightens low in his stomach.

Jet narrows his eyes. "That’s military again."

He tilts his head, studying the vessel for another moment. "Hm," he says. "Definitely Hu Xin colonies," he declares with all the solemn confidence he has.

Smellerbee snorts immediately.

Jet only glances at her. "What?"

"Definitely not."

His brow furrows. "How would you know?"

"Because the Earth Kingdom one isn’t from Gaoling either."

Jet opens his mouth, already reaching for a retort—

—but Longshot beside her shakes his head.

Jet stops.

For a moment, Jet just looks at him, the beginnings of his response still caught somewhere behind his teeth.

Longshot doesn't explain himself, only points again, his gaze fixed on something beyond them.

Smellerbee and Jet both turn their attention back to the place Longshot is indicating.

At first, there seems to be nothing new to see. It is still the same military ship lingering at the far edge of the harbor, its dark hull sitting low against the water. From this distance, the details blur together— the lines of its construction, the insignia painted along its sides, all of it reduced to little more than shadow and shape beneath the hazy light.

For a moment, neither of them says a word.

Smellerbee hums under her breath, thoughtful, while Jet squints at the distant vessel, searching for whatever Longshot has noticed.

Then he sees it.

Tiny flecks of blue moving across the deck.

Jet's expression shifts. "Ah."

He leans forward slightly, eyes narrowing as he follows the movement. "Fire Nation ship," he says. "But that's the Water Tribe."

His gaze flicks toward Longshot. "You weren't asking us to guess."

Longshot gives a small nod.

Jet's mouth curls into a grin. "Good catch."

Smellerbee's eyes remain fixed on the distant ship. The amusement slowly drains from her face as recognition settles in, replaced by something quieter. "They seriously never gave it back," she murmurs.

Jet glances at her. "I'd say good for them. Spoils of war and all."

She shifts, leaning farther back and bracing herself on her hands, her eyes never leaving the ship. "Hm," she hums like she doesn't disagree. 

A quiet moment passes. Then—

"Remember when Zuko and Sokka spent two whole days yelling at each other over the maps?" she goes on.

"Yeah?"

Smellerbee nods. "Only for it all to be for nothing?" She sighs dramatically. "That whole trip on that thing was so unnecessarily awful."

Jet gives her an unimpressed look but she isn't looking at Jet. After a moment, he turns his attention back to the water too.

The ship sits distant and dark against the water, but the sight of it is enough to drag up the miserable memory of those weeks after Ba Sing Se fell— weeks he has no desire to revisit.

But that's well in the past.

And Jet just has to never set another foot on any ship ever again. 

Though...

On the other hand, that would mean he's stuck in the heart of the Fire Nation forever.

His stomach tightens involuntarily at the thought.

Jet breathes sharply through his nose.

"Eh," he still finds himself saying. "Could have been worse."

Smellerbee lets out a short, disbelieving snort. “You hated every damn minute on that thing.”

Jet makes a face, but he isn't in the mood to argue.

The moment stretches, comfortable enough in its silence that neither of them seems particularly interested in filling it.

After a while, Bee glances sideways at him. "Should we go greet Bato and the others?"

Jet leans forward, resting his forearms against his knees, and looks toward the harbor again.

"It’s been a while," she adds.

He considers the prospect. Then his gaze drifts upward, following the cloudless expanse of sky overhead. The weather is too fine for that. Far too fine to abandon a perfectly good roof just to climb down and exchange pleasantries with a handful of acquaintances.

"We’ll see them later at the banquet anyway."

Smellerbee doesn't argue. Apparently, she arrives at the same conclusion. She merely shrugs and settles back against the warm slope of the roof.

Longshot does the same.

Jet reaches into the basket and takes another peach.

And for a while, none of them speak.

Below, more soldiers make their way through the harbor, moving between ships and along the busy docks. The breeze carries the scent of the salty sea upward, mingling with the sun-warmed smell of timber and rope. The sun hangs comfortably overhead, bathing the roof in warmth and for another quiet moment, there is nowhere he needs to be. 

No one waiting for him. 

Nothing that demands his attention.

So they stay.

 

 

 

 

[ Caldera Palace, Banquet (don't forget to clean your boots!) ]

 

The grand hall is already crowded, filled with people who have come from every corner of the world. Voices mingle beneath the high— and unnecessary golden painted ceiling, rising and falling into a constant hum of conversation.

Along one side of the room, a long table is full with drinks and refreshments, glasses catching the warm light as people drift past to help themselves.

Nearby, a small group of musicians waits quietly for their cue. A pipa rests across one musician's lap, an erhu leans within another's hands and a bamboo flute is held loosely between slender fingers, all of them silent for now.

The room is a wash of rich colors— greens, reds, deep blues and every shade in between— as people mingle in small clusters. And despite the Fire Nation officials speaking with Earth Kingdom representatives and Water Tribe masters, there is far less animosity in the room than Jet had expected. 

No sharp words, no obvious hostility beneath the polite exchanges. 

Whatever tension obviously exists is muted, tucked beneath measured voices, carefully courteous smiles and finer clothes— embroidered fabrics and arranged layers marking this unique historical occasion as something worth dressing up for.

For tonight, at least, everyone has made an effort.

Jet rubs absently at his shoulders, where his protectors used to sit. His hook swords are missing too. He made sure of that.

Even his boots are clean.

Smellerbee, dressed in a deep teal fitted tunic, gives him one long look before shaking her head and disappearing from sight.

Jet sighs, already exhausted and finds himself a place toward the rear of the hall, where he can lean comfortably against one of the great columns and watch without being expected to participate in every little exchange.

The hum of conversation gradually falls away when the Fire Lord steps into view at the front of the hall, the Avatar at his side. 

Two Kyoshi Warriors stand a little behind them, flanking the pair with the quiet, unmistakable presence of guards who have no intention of being ornamental.

As one, the room bows.

Jet crosses his arms and watches the proceedings.

A pointed little cough comes from beside him.

Jet turns his head.

The man standing there is dressed in an impressive amount of red and gold, topped off with a ridiculous hat that somehow manages to make him look even more self-important. His disapproving stare lingers on Jet for a moment, apparently waiting for something.

Jet raises an eyebrow.

The minister clears his throat. "You should show the Fire Lord proper respect."

Jet looks at Zuko, who has just started into a speech Jet has heard often enough over the past few days that he could probably stand up there himself and recite it word for word.

Zuko’s voice carries steadily across the gathered crowd, careful and measured, each word chosen with deliberate precision. There is something almost painfully formal about the way he speaks when he is Fire Lord— every syllable controlled, every pause perfectly placed, as though he has rehearsed not only the speech itself but every breath between its sentences.

He certainly looks the part.

Zuko is dressed in the full ceremonial regalia of the Fire Lord, swathed in deep, dark red robes draped over his shoulders, gold catching along the embroidered edges whenever he moves, glinting beneath the light. 

His hair has been pulled back into an absurdly complicated looking topknot and tucked neatly among it the crown piece— polished until it gleams brilliantly in the light.

The whole thing should be ridiculous.

And, objectively, Jet still thinks it is.

Yet the sight of Zuko manages to sends an entirely different sensation curling through his chest— something so warm and unexpectedly pleasant, entirely at odds with how ridiculous Jet thinks the robes are. The feeling still catches Jet by surprise because Zuko—

Well.

Zuko looks way too good for his own good.

Annoyingly so.

For a moment, all Jet can do is stare at him.

Then he notices it.

The way Zuko is standing.

His posture is almost painfully straight, shoulders held just a fraction too stiff beneath the heavy layers of his robes. His hands are composed, his expression carefully neutral, his chin lifted with all the practiced dignity expected of a Fire Lord.

But Jet knows him.

He can see the tension in the set of his shoulders.

Zuko is nervous.

Really nervous.

Jet smiles faintly.

Then he leans slightly toward the man standing beside him, lowering his voice so that only he can hear. "If he ever makes me bow to him," he whispers. "I'll punch him in the throat."

The guy stares at Jet in open horror.

He mutters something that might pass for an excuse, then quickly scruffs away before Jet can make things any worse for him.

Jet huffs, amused and turns his attention back to the crowd while Zuko goes on about an new area of peace and what not.

Jet's eyes move over the gathering, taking stock of the familiar faces scattered among all the ceremony and finery.

He spots Bato first, standing with Hakoda and the rest of the Water Tribe men near the exit. They look as out of place in all this pomp and ceremony as Jet feels, though at least they have the good sense not to pretend otherwise.

Next to them Jet notices one of the guards by the door, a face that’s been starting to look familiar lately. One of these days, he should probably get around to asking the guy his name.

He moves on.

Katara stands farther toward the front in a nice formal dress, her attention fixed entirely on Aang. Next to her Zuko's uncle— and Jet can't see it from here, but undeniable beaming with pride.

Jet's gaze keeps moving.

A little farther in, the Earth King stands among a cluster of Earth Kingdom dignitaries. He looks less clueless than the last time Jet saw him and somehow manages to fit into the gathering better than he had any right to. The other officials have flocked around him, talking amongst themselves in low voices.

There are more Fire Nation ministers scattered throughout the room, all dressed in varying degrees of excessive formality.

He finally spots Longshot as well, off in one corner, arms crossed over his chest, watching the proceedings with his usual unreadable expression. 

Then Aang finishes.

The crowd breaks into applause, polite at first before quickly swelling into something louder. Somewhere toward the back, one particularly enthusiastic voice lets out a loud, embarrassingly earnest cheer.

Jet winces.

His eyes land on Sokka immediately.

She looks completely at home beside him, as though she belongs among all the silk and gold and polished floors.

Aang, at least, seems to have mastered the art of escaping. Jet catches sight of him skillfully evading another group of people before moving toward the balcony doors with Katara.

Jet raises an eyebrow when he notices they're holding hands now.

He still has to duck around another pair of guests.

Jet sighs and settles against another pillar instead.

Across the room, Jet sees Smellerbee and Toph at the refreshments, both of them having a go at the food and laughing together about something.

At least these two are having fun.

Then Jet notice the advisor he had spoken to earlier stands in a small group of other officials near him. They're talking quietly amongst themselves.

But one of them glances over at Jet.

Another follows.

The conversation continues, but their eyes keep finding him.

Jet's mouth tightens.

He folds his arms as he looks toward the front, set on ignoring them.

At least from here, he has a clear line of sight.

Zuko is talking to an Earth Kingdom delegation now, listening with that same careful, diplomatic expression he seems to have perfected in the last month.

Jet watches him for a moment.

Still looks stupid in his robes.

Still far too handsome.

There is something almost unfair about it, the way Zuko can stand there looking every inch the Fire Lord and still somehow be so unmistakably himself beneath all of it. The whole performance should make him seem distant, untouchable.

Jet lets his gaze linger.

But at least there is one comforting thought. Later this evening, Jet will get to peel all of this Fire Lord nonsense away. Piece by piece until there is nothing left of it.

And then, finally, Zuko will be his again. 

Jet just has to get through the—

"You’re staring at him," a voice from beside him says.

Jet doesn't have to look to know who it belongs to.

The noblewoman has drifted over to the pillar beside his, close enough that he can feel her presence without having to turn his head.

"At whom?"

"You are staring at Zuko."

Jet's mouth instantly tightens at her overfamiliar use of the Fire Lord's name. 

But he keeps his eyes fixed on the room, watching the movement of people between them and said Fire Lord.

"I’m checking whether he’s about to do something stupid."

There is a small, thoughtful hum beside him. "That’s usually why I stared at him too."

That makes Jet look.

Up close, she is even more striking.

There is something almost regal about her face, every feature composed into effortless elegance. Not a single dark strand of hair has fallen out of place, not a crease on the immaculate lines of her crimson dress. She carries herself with the quiet confidence of someone who has never once had to wonder what impression she is making.

The perfect picture of a Fire Nation noblewoman— polished, beautiful, poised, and entirely aware of the effect she has on everyone around her.

Jet finds it deeply irritating.

He knows the type.

His stomach twists unpleasantly, old resentment rising sharp and familiar beneath his ribs.

Still, Jet is nothing if not practiced.

The charming smile appears on instinct, smooth and effortless, settling over his face as he leans back against the pillar.

"Sorry," he lets the corner of his mouth curl a little higher. "I don't think I've had the pleasure of making your acquaintance yet."

She only scoffs.

Not loudly, but with enough disdain to make it feel like Jet has somehow committed a personal offense simply by speaking to her.

Her eyes travel over him, slow and dismissive, as though she's inspecting something unpleasant that has somehow found its way stuck on her fancy footwear.

"Ugh. Of course," she mutters.

Jet's smile vanishes.

The tension in his shoulders doesn't.

For a moment, she says nothing more. She turns her attention back toward the room, toward Zuko, leaving Jet to wonder whether she intends to answer him at all.

"So," she says without looking at him. "You are Jet."

"Yeah?" Jet asks slowly, the word edged with mild suspicion. "…You know me?"

"Zuko mentioned you."

That catches his attention.

So somehow he was part of the conversation she just had with Zuko before. Whatever they'd been discussing, his name had apparently been mentioned.

Jet's eyes narrow slightly. "And what did he say?"

She doesn't even blink. "Mostly your name."

Jet waits for more.

But nothing comes.

"That’s it?"

"He got weird when I asked."

Jet stills because that leaves an almost impressive amount of room for interpretation when it comes to Zuko.

He studies her for a moment, trying to read anything from her perfectly composed expression and coming up with nothing.

"…Weird," he repeats. "Or Zuko weird?"

For the first time, she turns fully toward him.

The change in her expression is subtle, but unmistakable. Whatever amusement— or indifference— had been there before disappears, replaced by a cool displeasure that seems to settle over her features like a veil.

Jet's brows lift. 

He has absolutely no idea what he just did. 

Apparently, however, whatever it was, it was enough.

"Don’t pretend you know him," she says, the words carrying a certain edge. “You might think you do.”

Her gaze travels over him again as though she is weighing him against some private measure only she understands, leaving him with the distinct and increasingly irritating feeling that he is being evaluated and found several lacking.

"You don’t," she adds.

Jet stills.

For a heartbeat, he simply looks at her, the words settling between them with all the weight of a deliberate insult.

Then the irritation that has been simmering beneath his skin flares into something sharper.

Jet meets her stare without so much as a flicker of hesitation. "You've got a problem with me?"

"Several," she answers quickly— sharp and matter-of-fact.

He studies her for another moment. "…At least you’re upfront about it," he mutters, then gestures vaguely toward her. "Who are you again?"

The look she gives him could have frozen boiling water. Then she rolls her eyes with an air of profound exasperation, turns on her heel and walks away.

Just like that.

No name. 

No explanation. 

Nothing.

Jet watches her disappear into the crowd, utterly bewildered.

He remains there for a few seconds, staring after her.

"…What the hell was that?"

The evening, apparently unconcerned with Jet's confusion, carries on around him.

Conversation rises and falls. Glasses clink. Somewhere across the hall, someone laughs a little too loudly over the music in the background. The endless procession of important people continues as though Jet hasn't just been subjected to the strangest conversation of the night.

Jet glances around, as if the room itself might offer some explanation.

Instead, he spots Sokka emerging through the balcony doors.

There is a conspicuous smear of white makeup across one cheek.

Jet's gaze flicks briefly toward the empty space where one of the Kyoshi Warriors should be standing. And, ah, well... Jet doesn't need to guess.

Instead his eyes move on until they land on Katara nearby and looking around as well.

Jet makes his way over.

"Who's that?" he asks, nodding toward the woman already settled next to Zuko again— who's apparently now caught in a conversation with Aang and another small group of Earth Kingdom dignitaries.

"Who—?" Katara follows his gaze. Then recognition dawns. "Oh. You mean Mai?"

Jet looks back toward the front.

"She’s been a childhood friend of Azula."

Right.

The princess Jet has met exactly twice.

Once, when she'd tried to get her claws into Zuko in the crystal catacombs under Ba Sing Se and resulted in Zuko putting himself between Jet and her. And again at the Western Air Temple where Zuko did the same for all of them.

Jet's mouth tightens.

A childhood friend of Azula then.

That means she knows Zuko too. That she'd known him before. That she's been a part of a life that Jet can only piece together in scraps— the palace, the royal family, the people who had known him when he was still a prince.

"Yeah."

Not yet.

Finally.

Something he knows how to do.

"You were not hoping for pirates," Zuko says, though the growing amusement in his expression rather undermines the accusation.

"I was," Jet insists. "Pirates, smugglers, bandits— anything, really. Anything that gets us out of this bureaucratic nightmare."

Zuko's smile lingers, tilting his head as he watches Jet with a softer expression. "Fraudulent papers are still a real crime."

"Right," Jet says. He looks at the stack. Then back at Zuko. "Still boring, though."

Jet hums, mulling this over for all of a second before he pushes himself away and hops up onto the corner of the desk. The wood creaks faintly beneath him. He swings one leg idly, glancing down at the reports scattered between them. "Well," he says, "if we're not arresting anyone tonight, can I at least go with the guards tomorrow to check this out?"

Zuko looks up at him. "Sure."

Jet blinks. He'd half expected an argument, or at least a lecture about staying out of trouble and letting the proper authorities do their jobs. Instead, Zuko answers with such easy certainty that, for a moment, Jet isn't quite sure what to do with himself. "Oh," he shifts on the desk. "Great."

Zuko's gaze lingers on him for another moment before he looks back down at the papers, his expression softening into something quieter. "Thanks for helping by the way."

The words catch Jet a little off guard.

He looks at Zuko, whose attention seems to have returned to his own reports, as though the admission were nothing particularly remarkable. 

As though Jet hasn't spent the better part of the evening hunched over mind numbing documentation that,  until a few minutes ago, meant absolutely nothing to him.

Jet shrugs, suddenly rather interested in the edge of the desk. "Yeah, well," he nudges against a calligraphy set on the far side with his fingertips. "That's what I'm here for."

And, judging by the small smile that touches Zuko's mouth, it seems to do the trick. Warmth unfurls in Jet's chest, spreading somewhere beneath his ribs and settling there, made all the worse by the way Zuko continues to look at him, as though there's something about him worth smiling at in the first place.

Jet leans back a little, bracing his hands against the desk as he studies him.

Zuko's not wearing that stupid crown. Then again, he hardly ever does unless he absolutely has to, and Jet can't say he minds. 

Without it, he looks, for a fleeting moment, like the guy who used to half-heartedly wipe down tea-stained tables, never caring just how much residue was left behind. The same guy who'd grumbled through his chores and seemed personally offended whenever someone expected him to do them properly.

And now he's supposed to clean up an entire country.

It's almost ironic.

Except Zuko is still looking at him like that. Far too fondly, far too openly, with a softness in his eyes that makes Jet feel as though he's the one who's been caught doing something embarrassing. He shifts on the desk, suddenly in need of something else to occupy his attention.

Like the stupid, ugly ropes Zuko insists on wearing. Too bad that he still looks hot in them either way.

"You know what we should do later, once we're done for today?"

Zuko hums, tilting his head in quiet curiosity. "What?"

"Once we are done we are going to get you out of that stupid robes of your and—"

Smellerbee clears her throat. "You know we can still hear you guys?"

Zuko blinks, caught off guard, before a snicker slips past his lips. Color blooms across his cheeks and neck all the same, a telltale flush that only deepens when he ducks his head, suddenly very interested in whatever happens to be written on the papers before him.

Jet watches him with no small amount of satisfaction.

There is something almost embarrassingly gratifying about it, the ease with which he can still draw that reaction from Zuko. 

Jet should tease him for it, but somehow, for once, Jet keeps the observation to himself.

Besides, there's something rather nice about seeing Zuko blush without having to watch him squirm over it, too.

 

 

 

 

[ You Can't Be Serious?! ]

 

A guard stands stationed outside the study early this afternoon, his back straight and his hands clasped neatly behind him.

"Hey, Chen," Jet greets as he approaches.

Chen turns his head, offering him a slow, somewhat awkward nod. His eyes flick briefly toward the corridor, then back to Jet, as though he half expects someone to materialize out of thin air and reprimand him for the familiarity. 

Still, he doesn't bow— hasn't bowed the last few times, either.

Small wins.

Jet considers this something of an improvement. At this rate, perhaps in another few weeks, Chen might even manage a greeting without looking as though he's committing a minor act of treason. 

Jet offers him a crooked grin before striding past. He doesn't bother knocking.

The door to Zuko's study swings open with considerably more force than is strictly necessary.

Jet all but kicking it wide as he saunters inside with some energy in his steps and coming to a halt right that instant.

That elegant, impeccably dressed lady is seated beside Zuko on the little sofa, a piece of paper spread between them. 

Their heads just bent close together— looking up immediately at Jet's sudden entrance.

He gaze already settles on Jet cool and thoroughly unimpressed. 

"Entering without knocking is rude," she says sharply. "Not that you would know that."

Ah. Right.

Still hostile, then.

Jet regards her for a moment, taking in the pinched disapproval of her mouth before his own lips curl into a sneer. Some people, it seems, are determined to make themselves unpleasant no matter how little encouragement they're given.

He can respect the consistency, if nothing else.

"Mai, please," Zuko says, apparently neither noticing nor particularly caring about the tension between them. 

He lets out a weary sigh and sets his papers aside. "Sorry," he says to Jet. "The council meeting ran late and this is too important to leave until tomorrow."

Jet crosses his arms and settles onto the edge of the desk, making himself comfortable. "What are you doing?"

"We're going over the return of Omashu to the Earth Kingdom," Zuko explains, tapping a finger against the documents spread across his lap.

Omashu?

Wha—

Return?!

Jet blinks.

He frowns and glances at the papers in Zuko's hands, then back at his face.

Zuko had mentioned something about Omashu yesterday, hadn't he? Or perhaps it had been the day before. The last few days have blurred together in a miserable procession of paperwork and meetings, interrupted only by the business with the shipment records and the rather unexpected possibility that someone has been falsifying cargo documents which—

Well.

Jet has spent an entire, thoroughly boring morning trailing after guards, watching officials shuffle from one place to another and eventually discovering that the culprit behind the faulty paperwork and missing shipments was some old man skimming money to send to his sister in the colonies.

The missing crops? Also going to the colonies.

And just like that, his grand conspiracy had come to an end. And instead, just an old man trying to keep his family fed in the aftermath of a war. 

Which Jet can't even fault him for, really.

Still.

Pirates would have been so, so much more fun.

Ether way, between the investigation and the endless parade of paperwork that had followed, Jet's attention had been pulled in a dozen different directions. He'd nearly forgotten about Zuko's reports altogether.

Jet hums, his attention drifting back to Mai, who is sitting altogether too comfortably close to Zuko for his liking. He eyes the narrow space between them, or rather, the distinct lack of it, before turning his attention to the matter at hand.

"So, what's the problem with it?"

As far as Jet can tell, the whole thing seems straightforward enough. 

Returning the troops will take time, naturally, but the war is over. For almost two months at that.

Zuko sighs drawn out. "The governor refuses to return the city," Zuko says, sounding thoroughly exhausted by the mere prospect of explaining it.

Jet stares at him.

"So?" He spreads his hands, as though the answer ought to be obvious. "You're the Fire Lord. Fire Lord trumps governor. There. Problem solved."

Mai scoffs, leaning back against the sofa with an air of long-suffering patience. "Oh, do feel free to join our discussion," she says, a thin, sharp-edged smile curling at her lips.

"I'm sure you have considerable expertise in the relinquishment of military occupation under conditions of persistent hostile noncompliance and an outright refusal to acknowledge the sovereignty of the newly established governing authority."

Jet slowly blinks at her.

He stares for a moment, letting the words settle somewhere in the general vicinity of his understanding.

"...What?"

Zuko sighs, pinching the bridge of his nose. "What she means is that he refuses to acknowledge me as the successor Fire Lord, either."

"He…" Jet pauses, just to make sure he's understood correctly. "Refuses?"

Ah.

Ah, now he gets it.

Jet's expression shifts as the pieces fall into place.

There are still Ozai sympathizers lurking about, more of them than he'd care to think about— men who'd sooner see the old regime restored than accept that the world has moved on without their permission. 

Zuko hums, his expression growing sober. "He's rallied all the military support in the area," he explains. 

"The Earth Kingdom is getting impatient, but if we can't get Mai's father to negotiate and convince him to relinquish his hold on the city, it'll spark another fight in Omashu."

Jet stills completely.

Something hot and deeply unpleasant sparks in his stomach— irritation coiling beneath his ribs.

He shifts where he sits, jaw tightening as he tries to swallow it down, though the feeling only seems to grow more uncomfortable the longer he thinks about it. 

"Let me get this straight," he starts slowly. "Omashu is apparently still in Fire Nation hold because the governor refuses to give it up?" 

Jet swallows. The hot, unpleasant knot only tightens in his stomach but he really doesn't want to get that angry. Not here, not in front of Mai and certainly not at Zuko, who has the unfortunate habit of making Jet feel considerably more than he'd like.

Jet forcefully exhales ones. "You are seriously considering negotiating with an Ozai loyalist over whether the Earth Kingdom gets its own city back?" the words still come out sharper than he'd intended, each one edged with disbelief. "Are you hearing yourselves?"

Zuko's mouth pulls into a tight line. "The war has only just ended," he says, his voice measured carefully. "We need to resolve this without any more bloodshed."

As though that is the only thing that matters.

Jet's jaw clenches. 

He pushes himself away from the desk, unable to stomach the thought of sitting still for another second.

"You are the Fire Lord," Jet presses, the words hard and leaving no room for a rebuke. "And the Earth Kingdom needs to get its city back."

How is this even a discussion? 

How is there anything to discuss?

"Whichever way is necessary," he insists, barely leaving room for Zuko to breath. "Immediately."

Jet throws a hand out in exasperation, frustration bubbling hot beneath his skin.

"And if he refuses, then he's the one who needs to face the consequence!" He adds sharply.

Zuko looks at him, almost startled.

As if, for a moment, he'd forgotten what Jet's opinion here might be.

Mai scoffs, the sound sharp enough to cut through the room. "You're remarkably short-sighted."

Jet turns to her, fixing her with an unblinking stare. His jaw tightens, but he says nothing, giving her just enough rope to continue making a fool of herself.

"And you clearly know nothing about the political consequences this would have for Zuko," she goes on, her tone dripping with condescension.

Then she turns to Zuko, her arm brushing deliberately against his shoulder as she shifts closer to him. 

The gesture is so casual, so presumptuous, that Jet feels his irritation flare all over again. As though she has any right to touch him so freely. As though she can simply claim that little bit of space beside him and expect everyone else to accept it.

"Zuko, you should know better than to listen to some—"

Jet pulls back before she can finish whatever insult she's got ready. “You are right.”

His gaze drifts to the space between them, or rather, the distinct lack of it. To the way Mai sits comfortably close to Zuko, her shoulders brushing his, her presence beside him so natural it hardly seems to occur to her that she might be unwelcome.

And then it suddenly clicks.

Oh, she likes him.

Of course she does.

And now she's sitting next to Zuko with an infatuating air of superiority like she belongs right there. Looking at Jet like he is just the Earth Kingdom peasant that wandered in somewhere he shouldn't be.

Jet opens his mouth, a nasty little quip already forming on his tongue, something suitably cutting to wipe that infuriating look off Mai's face—

Then he glances at Zuko.

He's still looking at him with that same bewildered, somewhat stunned expression, as though he's not entirely sure what to make of any of this.

Jet stops.

Hesitates.

Another moment passes.

Then Jet exhales sharply.

Forcefully.

He still might me very, very angry but this isn't a fight he can just win with his fists. And he certainly doesn't ever want to say anything to Zuko he'll regret.

"I think you'll figure the rest out without me," he says instead.

Jet turns and makes for the door, eager to put some distance between himself and this entire miserable conversation. 

"Jet— " Zuko calls bewildered.

Jet doesn't stop. 

Only at the threshold, he turns to look back over his shoulder. 

Apparently, he still can't help himself.

"You said you wanted me to hold you accountable?" His eyes settle squarely on Zuko. "There it is."

Zuko immediately sits up straighter, opens his mouth ready to—

"So, whatever you do," Jet cuts over him. "Don't make excuses for the Fire Nation."

"Jet— " Zuko tries again.

Jet leaves before Zuko can say anything else, jaw tight, striding out and ignoring the sound of Zuko calling after him again or the way Chen scrambles out of his way.

He's already halfway down the next corridor when he realizes that he'd never got to tell Zuko about the resolution of his boring paper-discovery. 

Well. 

He's sure Zuko will hear about it somewhere else instead.
