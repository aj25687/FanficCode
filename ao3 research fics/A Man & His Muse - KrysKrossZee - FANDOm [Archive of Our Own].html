A Man & His Muse

KrysKrossZee

Summary:

Escaping from Wizarding Britain, Pansy takes up a position as an English teacher at Durmstrang. There she finds someone unexpected, someone who can't seem to give her any sort of space.

Notes:

I tried to write this in one day but words are hard this weekend so it took three. This is my second contribution for Antonin+ Week 2023 though! Thank you again to the Dolohoes admins for inspiring me.

Prompts used -

Denopoly: Confessions

Draco's Den Bingo: Durmstrang Institute

Work Text:

 

Pansy pulled her coat around her shoulders a little more tightly. Somehow it was even colder inside this castle than it was outside, which made her wonder why she had thought that coming to work in a Scandinavian country in the middle of winter was a good idea. Yes, she had wanted to put as much distance between herself and the person that she had once been but at the expense of her own comfort? She wasn’t so sure.

 

Inside Durmstrang, it didn’t look all that different to Hogwarts; only the colours were different and she hoped, as she looked around, that the maroon furnishings would make her feel somewhat warmer, but that wasn’t the case. At least not as of yet.

 

She looked around as she wondered where she should go; no-one had greeted her at the door and the fact that she was able to walk right on in without anyone asking her where she was going unnerved her but she made a point of pushing it to the back of her mind.

 

“Miss Parkinson?” A voice interrupted her train of thoughts, pulling her gaze away from the gilded mirror she had spotted that was far too high up for anyone to look into.

 

“Good morning.” Pansy greeted the man, already uneasy because he knew who she was but she didn’t have a clue about him. “And you are?” She asked, an eyebrow raised as she looked him up and down. He looked vaguely familiar but she couldn’t have told you where she had met him before.

 

The man blinked a couple of times, clearly having expected a different reaction than the one that he had received but he seemed to shake this off. “Antonin Dolohov.” He told her and she felt her blood run cold. Dolohov had been a household name when she had been a little girl and she hated that he still had this sort of effect on him. “Mr Botev sends his condolences - he knows he was meant to have met you this morning but he’s been held up. I’m here to give you a tour instead.”

 

“I don’t think I want to go anywhere with you.” Pansy said before she was able to stop herself. Some people in the circles her family had run in worshiped people like Dolohov who would do anything for the Dark Lord but Pansy was not among them, or at least she wasn’t any more. Since the end of the war she had grown as a person and that had meant that she no longer supported the cause that she once had. “How are you even here? I thought you were in Azkaban.”

 

“I’ve been entrusted to Botev’s custody.” Dolohov told her, his eyes scanning her body in a way that made her want to retreat. Now that she knew that he was here, no-one would blame her for running back home. “I am here as long as I have a job. If I don’t have a job then I have to go back.”

 

Pansy stared at him for several moments as she tried to figure out how she had gotten to this point, how she had gotten to this place where she was looking an ex-Death Eater in the eye and trying to figure out what she was meant to do now. She knew that Karkaroff hadn’t been the most upstanding of citizens but she had held out hope for Botev when he had taken over, and yet, here he was enlisting those who shouldn’t be around children to teach in his school. It was Dumbledore all over again.

 

“The tour?” Dolohov offered, gesturing behind him to the rest of the castle and Pansy knew that she was going to have to wait until later to voice her opinions and talk to the head teacher about whether or not this was a good move for her. “Unless you want to go straight to your classroom and we can arrange for you to have a tour later.”

 

“I think later might be for the best.” Pansy decided. “If you could just show me to the staff room and to my classroom, that would be great.” She didn’t want to let her students down on her very first day, even if she wasn’t sure that this was going to be a continuing thing for her. She’d at least stay until a suitable replacement could be found.

 

Dolohov nodded before he turned, leading the way with Pansy following at a suitable distance. She knew that she should probably walk by his side but she didn’t feel comfortable doing that. She didn’t feel comfortable in his presence but she knew that she was going to have to get over that for the time being if she wanted to be the best teacher that she could be.

 

**

 

Pansy sighed as she looked around the small room that she was now meant to call her own. She hadn’t exactly known what to expect when she’d been told that there was shared staff accomodation available at Durmstrang but she knew that this wasn’t it. The room was essentially a box, from the bed she could touch three of the walls and there was barely space at the end of the bed for the pitiful excuse of a desk that they had given her. There wasn’t really room for a chair so it didn’t even make sense that there was a desk. The bathroom, kitchen, and living room were all communal areas for the staff but from what Pansy could ascertain, there were only four members of staff who lived here - herself, Dolohov, a janitor whose name she hadn’t learned yet, and a Divination teacher who goes by the single name of Reba. Pansy reckoned Reba was almost as batshit crazy as Professor Trelawney had been.

 

This was temporary. She had decided that as soon as she had stepped through the door. Botov had convinced her to stay on as a teacher in this school until the end of the year but she knew that she couldn’t actually live within these walls. She had grown up in luxury, after all, and this was as far from luxury as she imagined life could get.

 

She hated that she was already regretting her decision to move to a different country but she was somewhat hopeful that her mentality would shift at some point but she didn’t think that she was going to be able to do that all that easily. She shook her head slightly, her frustration at herself building before she decided that she couldn’t spend the rest of the evening inside of this room. It was too small and she wasn’t going to be able to sleep yet.

 

Pansy headed through to the living area, smiling a little despite her bad mood when she saw that there was a fire crackling away in the fireplace. She knew that meant that she wasn’t going to be alone there but seeing the fire was the first time that she hadn’t felt cold all day. She moved towards it instinctively, her butt finding an armchair close to it and her eyes closing for a moment as she enjoyed the heat.

 

“Khorosho?”

 

She wasn’t all that surprised to hear a voice but she was surprised that that voice didn’t appear to be speaking English. She opened her eyes before blinking a couple of times and then turning her head towards the voice. She shouldn’t have been all that surprised that the voice hadn’t spoken English considering where she was but he wasn’t speaking any of the languages that she knew.

 

“Pardon?” She asked before she realised who was speaking and she also realised that the language he was speaking was Russian.

 

“Sorry, I asked if you’re alright.” Dolohov looked as if he was about to retreat in on himself but he met her gaze anyway. “I… I now realise that I should have kept my mouth shut and left you to it.”

 

Pansy let out a soft sigh. “I know you’re getting the impression that I hate you.” She said quietly as she turned in her chair to face him, her legs popping up over the arm of the chair. “And I just want to make sure that you know that it’s not you that I hate, it’s everything that you stand for.”

 

“Tsvetok, your father stood for everything that I used to.” Dolohov seemed to believe that reasoning with her was the best approach but Pansy’s eyes narrowed.

 

“And look where that landed him.” Pansy said quietly. She leaned her cheek against the back of the chair, her eyes still on him. “He’s dead and you ended up in jail.”

 

“I’m not justifying our choices.” Dolohov’s voice was just as quiet. “I’m not justifying the pain and suffering that we caused, or the deaths we’re responsible for. I fully understand that we were bad people, but that’s it. Were. Past. We were bad people. But now? Now I’m a teacher.”

 

“And what is it that you teach, Mr Dolohov?” She was sure that she had been told at some point during the course of the day but her mind had been completely fixated elsewhere. “Please tell me not the Dark Arts or Ethics.”

 

Dolohov let out a slightly cold laugh. “No, no. I’m not allowed my wand, as one of the terms of my bail. I’m an art teacher.”

 

“Art?” Pansy’s eyes widened very slightly, though she hoped not noticeably so. She hadn’t expected Durmstrang to have art as a subject, but then that was probably her only prejudices coming to the surface. “That… You’re an artist then.”

 

Dolohov’s head was tilted to the side slightly as he quite obviously looked her over. Pansy was hoping that she imagined the way that his tongue flickered over his lips. “Yes. I’m an artist. Art is what has saved my life in more ways than one. Art has given me a reason to live.”

 

Pansy was silent for a moment as she let this sink in. She could understand on some level what it meant to find a new purpose in life, she had escaped what she had been raised to believe was her purpose after all, even if she hadn’t been thrown away to rot like he had. She let out a sigh and moved to sit up in her chair properly, this did mean that she couldn’t exactly see him now but that was maybe for the best? She turned her attention back to the fire and watched the way that flames danced.

 

“He was your reason before, right?” She heard herself ask before she could stop herself. “Or at least his reasons were your reasons?” She kept her eyes on the fire, knowing that there was no way that she could allow herself to look at him now. He would no doubt hate her for the words that were tumbling out of her mouth, that she dared to bring up the past that he was trying to move away from.

 

“Yes.” Dolohov answered her without missing a beat. “I was blinded by hatred and at the end of it all, I didn’t even know if it was my hatred or his.” She heard him take a trembling breath. “I’ve been seeing a mind healer - another condition of my bail but it’s also been helpful. It’s helped me to see how wrong I was, how wrong we all were, and I hope you understand that too, as a child of that war, as a child of that awful way of thinking.”

 

“I never -” Pansy began to protest but he cut her off.

 

“But you did. You did believe the filth that was force-fed to you, because you had no choice but to believe it. It’s not your fault, but it is your responsibility to ensure that you do better as an adult and that you do better by the children in our care.”

 

Pansy chewed on her lip before she swiveled to face him again. “I will do better by them.” She said emphatically. She had never felt more passionate about anything in her life than that of shaping the minds of the youth in the proper way.

 

“Good.” Dolohov said with a nod before he rose to his feet. “And with that, I bid you goodnight, Tsvetok.”

 

Pansy blinked a couple of times but she found that she could do nothing more than whisper a ‘goodnight’ to him as she watched him leave the room.

 

**

 

Pansy reckoned that she had settled into her life as the English teacher here at Durmstrang very well. She had found her stride with the students and with the lesson plans that she had prepared, even if it at first it had been confusing for her. She had come in, expecting for the main language spoken here to be German but had found that most of her students’ spoke Danish or Swedish. Most of the students hailed from Scandinavian countries so this made sense but it meant that she’d had to adjust many of her lessons to suit them.

 

Pansy spent her evenings either looking for somewhere to live or sitting with a book by the fire. Dolohov often sat with her, in silence, with a sketchbook on his lap. She wanted to know what he was drawing but they hadn’t spoken much since their first evening in the staff accommodation. She knew that she should probably try to make conversation with him, it wasn’t as if they didn’t have anything in common after all, but she didn’t know what to say to him. She didn’t know how to talk to him when part of her still hated him.

 

And yet he intrigued her. The way that he held himself, the way that he kept himself to himself, the way that he appeared to be watching her and yet whenever she looked over at him, he had his eyes down on the sketchbook.

 

Pansy caved after thirteen days, finding herself standing over his shoulder as she decided that she had to know what he was drawing. She hadn’t prepared herself for what she found on the page, however, and she responded by taking the sketchbook from him.

 

“What’s this?” She asked as her eyes moved around the page which was covered in pencil drawings, pencil drawings of her. “What are these?” It was obvious what it was but she needed to hear him say it. Or more, she needed to hear him tell her that it wasn’t what she thought it was.

 

“They’re nothing.” Dolohov stood up as he tried to take the sketchbook back. “Please, give it back.”

 

“Why have you been drawing me?” She asked as she all but dropped the sketchbook onto his now vacant seat. “How long have you been drawing me?”

 

“You’re beautiful.” Dolohov said simply. “I have been drawing you while we’ve been sitting here together because I felt drawn to you. I can’t begin to explain it.”

 

“You’re going to have to try.” Pansy couldn’t help but get defensive but she felt violated. She had never been drawn before, or at least she didn’t think she had. Her mother had had paintings commissioned when she was younger - was that the same as being drawn? “You’re going to have to try to explain it to me, Dolohov, because this is… I don’t like it.”

 

“It’s not a bad thing, Tsvetok. It’s a compliment. There’s something about your face, about your form, that speaks to my soul. I… I’m sorry that you don’t like it and I… I’d promise to never draw you again but I don’t think I can promise that. I don’t think I could promise to stay away from you either.” Dolohov seemed to think that he could explain his way out of this one but Pansy just recoiled, taking a step back and even thinking about running but she had no idea where she would run to.

 

“I - I -” She stuttered, her voice shaking more than she would have liked. “I need to leave.” She said quietly, deciding that heading back to her room was the best way to deal with her emotions. Avoiding him was exactly what she needed to do, for more reasons that one.

 

Dolohov nodded, seemingly not going to stop her and this helped Pansy to relax as she headed back to her bedroom.

 

**

 

The knock on Pansy’s door stirred her from her slumber despite how soft it was. She blinked open her eyes and rolled over to stare at the door, wondering for a moment if she had imagined it before she heard the knock again. She rolled her eyes at no-one in particular before she grabbed a dressing gown and opened the door.

 

She wasn’t all that surprised to see that it was Dolohov standing on the other side. She had gone to sleep thinking about him and she figured that he probably wouldn’t have been able to sleep all that well if he had been thinking about her in return. If it was as he had said that he found himself called to her then it made even more sense that he wouldn’t be able to ignore the fact that she had been annoyed with him when they had parted ways earlier that evening.

 

“Can I come in?” Dolohov asked. There was a look on his face that reminded Pansy of panic but she didn’t think that she could feel bad that he was panicked about how she was feeling when she had spelled it out that he made her feel uncomfortable.

 

“I…” Pansy looked him up and down, about to tell him no before she gave the smallest of nods. “Okay.” She said quietly. “You can come in.” She agreed as she stepped aside to allow him past. He wasn’t a threat - he had no wand and he had no sketchbook with him so he couldn’t do anything to hurt her, really.

 

Dolohov stepped inside but there wasn’t really anywhere for him to go except to sit on the edge of the bed. Pansy remained standing, her eyes fixed on him as she tried to figure out what it was that he wanted and whether or not he was here with malicious intentions.

 

“What do you want, Dolohov?” She asked after a few minutes of them just staring at each other. “Why are you here in my room?” She knew but at the same time she didn’t and the awkwardness was making everything worse.

 

“I needed to see you.” He admitted. He was now on his feet again, his fingers touching her cheek and Pansy found that she didn’t mind all that much. His touch was warm, almost inviting and she had to purposefully make sure that she wasn’t leaning into his hand. “I needed to make sure that I hadn’t upset you.”

 

“And if you had upset me?” She asked slowly. “What would it have accomplished if you had, by coming back here?”

 

Dolohov paused for a moment before he answered her. “I don’t know.” He admitted, but he didn’t drop his hand from her face. Her breath caught in her throat as her eyes fixed on his. “I guess I hoped that I could apologise to you.”

 

“I’m waiting then.” She wouldn’t let him chicken out of telling her that he was sorry, even if she wasn’t exactly sure what he was going to apologise for. At the end of the day he wasn’t resposible for the way that she felt. He was here first and she was the one who had taken a job at the same school as him - and it wasn’t as if his drawings were actually hurting her.

 

“I’m sorry.” He said quietly. “I’m sorry that I have caused you nothing but problems since you got here.”

 

Pansy shook her head slightly, her stomach dropping as he removed her hand from his cheek. She wanted him to touch her again but she knew that she couldn’t say that, especially not when he was trying to apologise. “I just need to get over myself.”

 

Dolohov let out a light laugh. He was closer now, somehow, though she wasn’t entirely sure how or when that had happened and she felt her body tense for a moment, though it wasn’t an altogether bad feeling.

 

“There are many things that you need to get over, Tsvetok, but yourself isn’t one of them. So I want you to know that I’m sorry and I won’t draw you again.”

 

“What if I want you to draw me?” Pansy wasn’t even aware that this was something that she wanted until the words had escaped from her lips.

 

“You,” his fingers were back on her face, tilting her chin upwards so that she was looking up into his eyes, not that she had allowed her gaze to stray for even a second, “want me to draw you?”

 

“Yes.” Pansy whispered, her voice shaking again but this time for an entirely different reason. “I… Get your sketchbook now.” She swallowed. She made to move his hand from her chin but the second that her hand touched his, she could only close her fingers around his. She wanted him to draw her but at the same time she didn’t want him to leave her just yet.

 

Dolohov seemed to see this irony and a smirk flickered onto his lips. He leaned forwards instead of moving away to go and get his sketchbook as she had requested and the next thing that Pansy knew was that his lips were on hers.

 

**

 

“Don’t move.”

 

Pansy blinked open her eyes, her eyes fixing on the ceiling as she wondered if she was allowed to breathe. “I was going to go to sleep.” She admitted, trying not to sound annoyed because she knew that he didn’t mean any harm and she should be used to this sort of interruption by now, but she’d had a long day and she was exhausted now.

 

“I know but you’re perfect.” Dolohov said, followed by the telltale rustling of a sketchbook and clanging of a tin of pencils. “So please don’t move.”

 

Pansy let out a sigh, half-annoyed but strangely happy that he was inspired to draw her once again despite the fact that he had to have hundreds of pencil drawings of herself by then. She closed her eyes once more as she lay there, just trying to make herself relax and allow him to do the thing that he liked to do the most.

 

She considered herself lucky that she had someone as dedicated to her as he was, as dedicated as he was to immortalising her. She was his muse and she knew that she was lucky to be considered as such.

 

She allowed herself to doze off and she didn’t know how long she had been dozing for when she felt his hand on her cheek. She blinked her eyes open again, looking straight up into his eyes.

 

“You know, I think I’ve fallen in love with you, Tsvetok.” He whispered, his breath tickling her skin. She could tell from the serious look that had settled upon his face that he meant this with every single fibre of his being.

 

“You love me?” Pansy whispered back, her hand reaching up to caress his cheek. No-one had ever told her that they loved her before. Her panic sensors were being set off but she forced herself to take a breath. She couldn’t run from this, and she certainly couldn’t run from him because a part of her reckoned that she loved him too.

 

“I do.” He nodded, leaning into her touch for a moment. “I love you and it’s… It’s scary and not at all what I expected and I’m tied to this school so it’s not like we can even have a relationship outside of this place, but I love you.”

 

Pansy pulled in a slow breath before she nodded slightly. “I… I don’t think I’m in the same place, yet. But I-”

 

“I’m not expecting you to tell me that you love me back.” Dolohov said quickly. “You don’t have to love me back either. For a start, I know that we’re two different places in life but I also know you, or at least I think I know you and I don’t want you to feel pressured or for you to be scared off. I want you to… I just wanted you to know what you mean to me.”

 

Pansy tilted her head slightly before she leaned upwards and pressed a kiss to the end of his nose. She was lucky. She knew that she was lucky. She was his muse and he loved her and that was all that mattered.
