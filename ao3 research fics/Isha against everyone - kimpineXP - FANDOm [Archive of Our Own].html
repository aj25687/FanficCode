Zeri, with some victorious smile on her face, grabs a couple of pistols from Isha's hands, significantly lightening her load. She holds the weapon with one hand, while with the other she teasingly pinches her friend's tense junction of neck and shoulder. It's probably very unexpected and unpleasant, considering how she almost jumps away from the contact and grimaces. When you constantly sit like a shrimp, hunched over sketches and blueprints, such a reaction is understandable. Muscles need stretching, and you shouldn't forget your body's needs. The girl struggles with this, or rather, doesn't manage it at all.

Self-hatred enters the organism along with oxygen, settles in the lungs, spreads through all the insides, through the blood vessels, and settles somewhere in the temporal lobe cortex. It floats in gastric juice along with rare food, but it never comes out. Perhaps that's why one of her wrists can be encircled by thumb and pinky. Because memories eat away at everything, leaving a gaping hole in the skull instead of a left eye. The one around which the skin has wrinkled. It doesn't hurt anymore; it just reminds Vile-spawn of her biggest loss.

She leads Isha upstairs via Butcher's Bridge.

***

By the time they finish laying out the empty weapons and approach the designated location, twilight cloaks the sky in blue. Isha likes this time of day and would gladly slip away somewhere to walk alone or visit Arnold's kids, but Zeri won't let her pass unnoticed. Despite her apparent carelessness and relaxed state, she'd drag the girl in her arms if needed. Where this enthusiasm will lead, Isha doesn't want to know.

Having overcome endless rows of brothels, bars, underground casinos, dubious shops, and other buildings with signs that inspire no trust, the sight presented a structure differing from others not only in size but style. Against the backdrop of low wooden structures and stalls with colorful awnings and patches on them, the nearly three-story house made of old dark stone, with small windows of green bottle glass, stood out starkly. Above the black door hung a sign in white script font, illuminated by green neon: «Velvet Siren.»

Isha grimaced at the name, craning her neck to examine the sign. Who names a nightclub after a cheap whorehouse anyway?

In the alley between this establishment and some greasy spoon for workers, figures of a woman in revealing attire with a cigarette and a portly bald man were visible. They were clearly arguing loudly. Nearby, a woman with a small child by the hand entered the building through the back entrance.

Zeri grabbed her companion by the elbow and, swinging the wide door open, dragged her inside.

A thick smell of sweat, booze, and tobacco hit the nose. Dark wood tables for patrons stretched along the walls. On the left, someone played poker in a noisy group; on the right, flashily dressed men sipped multi-colored cocktails near the bar counter, whose countertop was lit with green light. But what surprised her most wasn't the strange yellow-green lighting of the place or the electronic music coming from somewhere above, but the winding catwalk right in the middle. It ran parallel to all the tables, lit from below by something bright, then curved and, like a stage, led backstage. The runway was fenced off from the rest of the space by metal poles roughly chest-high, and tabletops were installed on these poles, running along the entire perimeter of the fence. Bar stools similar to those at the bartender's station were even brought up to them. Isha examined all this with her mouth slightly agape until her gaze fell on what was right in front of her: three parallel metal poles rose from the catwalk all the way to the ceiling.

— My good buddy is working today, he'll mix you a couple of non-alcoholic cocktail versions, eh? It'll be relatively quiet, we'll relax, maybe you'll meet him. He's creative too! — Zeri shouted right into her ear, trying to cut through the music.

Her friend's hand dragged her to the bar counter. Behind it stood a tall vastaya with ash-grey skin, jet-black hair, and multiple piercings. Open lines of intricate tattoos showed on his bare arms from under the rolled-up sleeves of his brown shirt. Isha glanced at the green patch on the guy's apron — a short name — «Yan».

Zeri plopped onto a chair opposite Yan, then unceremoniously sat Isha down next to her, giving her not a second to think. The guy didn't even bat an eyelid. Glancing at them briefly from the corner of his eye, he continued polishing a green glass with a rag.

— Hey! Got anything new?

— No.

— Pff. Then same as usual,— all the girl's enthusiasm deflated somewhere. With her thumb, she pointed at her companion, — This is Isha. Pour the kid one too.

Yan nodded and got to work, while Isha gave her a sidelong stare. Kid, yeah right. Even her best friend didn't take her seriously, seeing her as a whining infant who only calms down if you hand her a gun barrel instead of a pacifier.

Regular club electronics shifted to something more melodic. The room first plunged into darkness, then lit up with bright neon. For only a second did the sounds of spotlight activation interrupt the music. And then screaming. Deafening shrieks and whistles from all sides. Someone started clapping loudly. Isha turned around in horror, covering her ears with her hands. She hadn't planned on going deaf either. Men got up from regular tables and sat at those attached to the platform. The elevated platform was lit brighter than the rest of the space. Zeri looked back and frowned in confusion, drilling a hole in the bartender's forehead with her gaze.

— Wait, but you said it'd be quiet today?

— You'd think you'd get used to the fact that it's never quiet here, Zeri.

Three women in high heels stepped onto the «stage» to the roar of the crowd. Their clothes... Isha can confidently say she's never seen anything like it before. Mini-skirts, leather shorts much shorter than her own, huge cutouts right in the middle of the chest, and fishnet stockings. It was hard to determine their age by face due to the abundance of bright makeup, but Isha decided they were definitely no older than sixty, given how smoothly and actively they moved. They smiled and blew kisses to the crowd, circling the poles, kicking legs up and thrusting breasts forward, arching their backs. Several men standing closer to the spectacle extended money to the dancers, as if luring little beasts. A tanned blonde took a coin from a stranger's hand with her teeth and immediately returned to dancing. The brunette nearby walked closer to the audience table and allowed one of them to shove gold coins right into her tight shorts.

— Hey! — Zeri pulled the girl by the ear, turning her face to her, causing the latter to hiss and wince, — Don't stare at whatever catches your eye!

Isha, like a scolded kitten, turned back to the bar counter, lowering her gaze to its worn surface with a hint of embarrassment and irritation. All her surroundings consider themselves smarter and superior, taking it upon themselves to pass her from hand to hand from one babysitter to another like a fidgety child. Child. If even the ever-understanding and condescending friend calls her that, why did she even bring her along for her utterly adult and important affairs? Thinking about how bad you feel and how poor you are, sitting mysteriously on the pier, is certainly not okay. But thinking about it to energetic music with half-naked women behind you and an inexhaustible friend beside you — twice as worse.

What to do in the current situation, Isha has no idea. Since her last conversation with Graves, she's been thinking, thinking, and thinking about it, but couldn't find a single logically correct option. From the standpoint of all her moral principles and, however slight, honesty, the right thing would be to leave her friends and become a hermit, wandering the world alone with her paranoia. No matter what bastards they are, setting them up to be shot because of her own mistake is completely wrong. But maybe everything will work out? And if not, well, Isha is also a person with a perfectly healthy ego! She doesn't want to die alone, even if heroically. The desire to let everything slide and continue living her normal life clearly looked like the winning bet. Come on, she's already endured forced adventures on her ass in childhood, why can't she spend at least her adolescence relatively stable? The world is completely unfair. Maybe she should just surrender to Piltover, give them the gemstone, and live happily ever after?

No.

Not a chance.

For a moment, the crowd quieted down along with the music. And then everything repeated again, but with renewed vigor. The music abruptly switched to an energetic, flirtatious, mischievous tune. Whistles and loud compliments addressed, as Isha could guess, to entirely different dancers now, unbearably hammered at her eardrums. Zeri and Yan paid absolutely no attention to what was happening under their noses, and especially not to her, continuing to chatter about some nonsense as if nothing had happened. The desire to bail from here anywhere, even without waiting for her drink, doubled.

Isha turned over her shoulder to find further and currently less accessible escape routes, but her gaze caught on something else. A girl with bright red hair, located right in the center of the catwalk. She wore a semi-transparent shirt, fully exposing her shoulders and collarbones, with a corset of suspicious-colored leather over it, something resembling a skirt made from a pathetic piece of fabric, and dirty high boots. Forbidden fruit is sweet, so Isha, for reasons unknown to herself, stared at how the girl spun around the pole with a playful smile. Maybe she was just attracted to people with bright hair colors, or maybe the girl just moved beautifully.

All thoughts suddenly vanished when their eyes met. The girl stuck out her tongue, winked at her, and immediately returned to dancing, already starting to flirt with another visitor.

— Zeri told you not to stare, — jerked her out of her stupor, the bartender's even voice and the thud of a glass on the table surface.

Whirling around, she almost knocked over the drink right in front of her nose. Isha stared at the glass with a striped straw.. Murky liquid reflected orange and red tones and emitted a strong scent of something citrusy. She lazily stirred it with the straw. In the reflection of the drink, the girl saw only the right side of her face, scarred, and involuntarily reached up to tousle her own bangs to try and hide it. Hide this ugliness.

For some reason, she didn't want to leave anymore, nor did she want to try the interesting drink Yan worked so hard on for her.

***

The fun and productive pastime ended with Zeri, flushed with alcohol, telling anecdotes about dishonest mermaids and lustful wanderers to the completely unimpressed bartender, who didn't laugh at all, while Isha, having turned away from her friend, wanted either to die of shame or to collapse into sleep from exhaustion right at the table under the loud music. Lack of proper sleep for the last three days was making itself known at the most inappropriate moment.

Zeri was clearly capable of reaching the airship on her own, but walking with her tipsy, unwanted adventures were guaranteed. The option to go separately disappeared very quickly for one simple reason: to reach the pier you had to cross goddamn Butcher's Bridge. At night. Completely alone. If she's going to die, it's definitely not going to be in a firefight between illegals. The only thing left was to nap for an hour or two on the bar counter while waiting for her friend to finish drinking and chatting, which Isha was successfully planning to do, if not for a woman's unfamiliar voice breaking the relative silence left by Zeri after she went to the bathroom.

— Hi there! — a drawn-out high-pitched voice approached from the bartender's side, — You've been sitting here so long, I just had to come say hello!

— Aren't you supposed to be in the dressing room, P-... — asked Yan with a note of uncertainty.

— Can you blame me for it being so bor-ring there? What're you gossiping about? Is this girl passed out or what? Hey, hi!

At the sudden weightless touch of a stranger's hand to her hair, Isha immediately lifted her head from the table and flinched back on the stool as if struck by lightning. Her hands again reached for her bangs, diligently fixing them. And right in front of her, Isha saw the very same red-haired dancer who had winked at her from the podium about two hours earlier. And now she was standing right in front of her at the bar counter, staring as if into her very soul with sincere childlike curiosity and a soft smile on her face. Very awkward.

— Good morning, cutie! I'm Pixie.

The girl friendly extended a hand for a handshake.

Isha stared at her face as if she had a gun trained on her. Hesitating noticeably, she uncertainly shook her hand with her right, then, realizing something just as quickly, Isha yanked her hand away and instantly hid it under the table. Pixie noticed, didn't she? Of course she did, even if she didn't bat an eyelid! She's definitely going to look at her askance and run away sparkling her heels. Or, what's even worse, start asking uncomfortable questions. How to avoid this shame...

Pixie, however, somehow manages to end up on the stool right next to her as if she teleported rather than walked around the bar counter. The newly-made acquaintance props her chin on her palm and peers at the girl nearby. Yan, as luck would have it, started pouring glasses for other incoming visitors.

— So, what's your name, huh?

An incredibly inconvenient question for a mute person. Perhaps any questions are inconvenient for a mute person if they can't answer with gestures. Fortunately, Isha has a contingency plan for this case: a windbreaker with a colorful blue patch on the inside. Of course, it's not there for introductions at all, but so that someone who doesn't respect personal space and doesn't cherish material things wouldn't confuse Isha's jacket with his own. But right now she doesn't even have markers, so this will do too. Taking the jacket from the back of the chair, she showed the inscription to the interlocutor. She pouted her lips and leaned in even closer for some reason, to examine it as if she couldn't see it from her seat.

— Isha. Can't talk, huh? Well, I'll talk for both of us then, don't you mind? — Pixie broke into a smile, peering into her face with shining sky-blue eyes. She exhaled with relief for a moment. Too early to rejoice, — And what's that glowing thing...

Elegant fingers with burgundy nail polish slid to the breast pocket with a stud and would have already climbed in, pulling out the ideally spherical blue stone. But Isha managed to yank the item from the curious hands and carefully put it back in its rightful place. She deliberately swayed lightly, as if saying there was nothing important in there. Damn her for not bothering to hide the gem back under the ceiling above her bed in her cabin, preferring instead to suffer and whine about what an idiot she is and how everything sucks for her. If word gets out about this stone to anyone else, then it really will suck! And she keeps dragging it around with her everywhere as if it's some trinket, not dangerous ultra-secret weaponry that almost committed genocide on a large scale. Big deal, Hextech is just being hunted by one of Runeterra's most powerful states, and a teenage girl with a strange sense of responsibility calmly walks around Bilgewater with it! Isha is indeed a real fool after all.

— Secrets-secrets. Suit yourself. Want me to tell you Yan's secret?

Pixie leans towards her interlocutor's ear and, covering her mouth with her palm, whispers something. By the end of the story, Isha finds it difficult not to break into a smile, peeking sideways at the serious bartender serving beer mugs to men.

— It's true, I swear!

She can't hold back laughter and giggles, covering her mouth with her scarred hand.
