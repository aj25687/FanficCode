To Be Young

Chapter 1: Part I

Chapter Text

Part I:

The old lady was back again.

Jinx paid her no mind. She was relatively harmless, and only stopped by every few weeks to watch her or make idle conversation. Not that Jix often replied to any of her questions. 

The blue-haired girl had no idea how the woman found her workshop, or even why she stuck around. The fallen vent fan lodged between two tight stone walls wasn’t exactly a… safe place for the elderly. 

But sometimes she brought food and left it by the entrance. So Jinx wouldn’t throw her out just yet. 

She liked to watch Jinx as she worked with rapt fascination, and sometimes, wonder - as if she couldn’t believe that the blue-haired girl was actually capable of what she was doing. Not like she had any idea what it was that Jinx was doing. Or maybe she did. Either way, Jinx certainly didn’t.

Jinx just liked to take things apart, remove as many moving pieces as possible, and then put them back together again to see if they still worked. Then, she’d have more pieces for her own toys. The ones that went boom.

Although, now that she thought about it, not many of them went boom anymore. It had been a long while since one did. They mostly just jittered about, buzzed, or made satisfying repetitive motions. Jinx decided that she’d need to resolve that. Maybe make one big boom soon to make up for her lack of booms recently. 

She looked down to see what she had been idly tinkering with. It was an engine of some sort. She found it in a junk heap near the sanctuary - the one she wasn’t supposed to go near. 

“But it always has the best stuff!” came an excited voice. Jinx nodded to the young pink haired girl that had popped up right beside her. She was right, it did have the best stuff. “Maybe we can go again, you and I, after I get back,” the girl continued. “Would you like that, Powder Jinx?” 

Jinx twitched at the clouded memory of a name long gone, but nodded all the same. The girl, like the old lady, was harmless. Even nice to her, most of the time. That’s why she hadn’t sent her away like the others. But sometimes, the pink girl would slip up, and say something that she wasn’t supposed to. Something that Jinx had tried to keep locked away. Jinx had gotten mad at her the first few times, but then she hadn’t come back for a while and Jinx was worried that she had pushed her away. 

But the girl had come back eventually, meek and regretful, and Jinx decided to keep her around. She was pretty much the only family that Jinx had left, even if she didn’t like to think about it. Jinx hadn’t forgotten the girl’s name, oh no. But she kept it locked up in a tight box at the back of her mind, and simply called her “pink” instead. It hurt less this way.

Even if she missed Vi... NO! Jinx shook her head firmly to dislodge the intrusive thought. Change the topic.

The young girl and the old lady were similar in a lot of ways, even though the old lady rarely spoke. They both seemed content to let Jinx just do her thing without interfering, while occasionally offering light praise or a suggestion. They also both reminded her to do things that were necessary, like sleep and eat. Not that Jinx ever needed to really eat, but she was getting more and more tired recently.

Jinx muttered to herself at the memory of the old lady’s face when she had found out that Jinx rarely ever ate. It was a look of shock and surprise, before quickly turning into a pained worry that had sucked the laughter right out of Jinx. She had quickly run off before coming back soon after with a bowl of Jinx’s favorite meal from Jericho’s.

A weird coincidence, but Jinx wasn’t gonna turn down some good, hot grub. 

The recollection faded away and Jinx realized that she had been trying to unscrew the same gear for the past few minutes. She put all of her effort into it, but it just wouldn’t turn at all. She punched the brass engine in frustration, and her knuckles cracked against the stiff metal.

“Stupid Piltie junk,” she cursed under her breath. “They always make them too tight, too too tight. But then, they wouldn’t want their toys breaking. Not like me …” she trailed off and reached over to grab one of the heavy wrenches by her workbench to work against the metal and try to loosen the gear.

She grasped the tool and turned back to slam the gear into submission, but before she could even raise the wrench, a strong arm reached down in front of her to grab hold of the engine.

Jinx’s light pink eyes darted back and forth between the engine, the arm, and the woman it was attached to. The lady had never come this close before and the contact made Jinx distinctly nervous. 

But… strangely enough, she didn’t feel like she was in danger. It had just been so long since anyone had come near her. Since anyone had even wanted to approach her. The closeness was a strange sensation that pulled forth too many old memories for her linking, though.

“W-wow,” Jinx chuckled to try and distract from her discomfort at the proximity. “You’re well-built for an old lady.”

The lady let out a breathy chuckle as her arm strained against the stuck gear. Then, with a creak and a hiss, the gear came loose and twisted. Her arm turned as she unscrewed the gear fully, before lifting it up and holding it towards Jinx.

Jinx dropped the wrench with a loud clang and hesitantly took the gear from the woman, her eyes continuing to dart between it and the lady’s face the whole time. She gingerly took hold of the brass ring and - wow was it heavy - set it down next to the engine.

“Thanks, old lady,” she muttered, before she continued to fiddle with whatever was inside the engine. A strong hand slipped against her back and gave a few gentle pats. The feeling was oddly familiar to Jinx, and she froze. 

The lady wasn’t supposed to touch her. She wasn't even supposed to come this close. She was supposed to stay on the other side of the fan and only watch her work.

“But she did help you, ” Pink’s voice in her head echoed. Jinx sighed. The voice was right. She’d let this one go. But just this once! And only because she was tired. 

She had been tired a lot recently. As a matter of fact, maybe it was time for her to go and rest. “Go away, lady,” she finally said as she got to her feet. “I’m tired.”

The woman didn’t reply, and instead gave her one last firm rub on her shoulder before she turned and headed towards the entrance.

What a strange old, lady.

Vi unlocked the door to her and Caitlyn’s shared home and stepped inside. She scrubbed her boots off on the entrance mat, a ritual that Caitlyn continued to insist on and pester her about whenever she forgot about it. There were some rituals of the Topsiders that Vi would just never understand. 

Just like also needing to hang her jacket on the hook by the door instead of wearing it around the house.

She continued deeper into the apartment, shedding more of her personals as she went. It was muscle memory at this point: keys in the small metal bowl, Vi’s one pair of boots on the rack next to Cait’s seven , jacket on the coat hook, lock the door behind her. It was tedious the first few times, but with Caitlyn's repeated nagging, Vi had gotten used to it.

Vi took a few steps into the entrance hall before she paused by a small wardrobe, as was also a kind of ritual at this point. Framed pictures were scattered across the top. A few of them we of Vi and Cait together. A few were of Cait’s parents. And one that Vi had managed to get off of Ekko that showed Vander, Powder and herself candidly in the bar. Supposedly, Benzo had taken it with one of the old daguerreotypes that had managed to find its way down into his shop, just to see if it worked.

She picked it up and gazed at the faces one by one before her eyes fell onto Powder’s. Her sister was staring at her as she tried to stack all of the bar’s stools on top of each other while Vander hurriedly moved anything glass out of the way.

Vi chuckled at the memory. It had been so long ago, and so much had happened since then, but it somehow still only felt like yesterday. 

“Hey, Vi,” Caitlyn’s voice called out to her, and Vi lowered the frame back down to look up at her. 

The lines on her aging face creased beneath her eyes as she gave her wife a warm smile. “Hey, Cupcake,” she replied. She stepped forward to give the woman a tight hug, and ran her calloused hands through Caitlyn’s gray-streaked blue hair. “Hate to break it to you, but I think you’re going gray.”

“Oh, hush you,” Caitlyn chucked as she pulled away. Her smile mellowed as she reached up to frame Vi’s face with her hands. “How is she?”

“Still young,” Vi replied with a soft smile. “She called me an old lady today, though.”

Caitlyn huffed. “Oh, please. You’re hardly old.”

Vi shrugged. “In her eyes, I’m plenty old. In the lanes you were lucky to reach forty, let alone forty-eight.” She nodded upwards. “The hair doesn’t really help the picture either, Cupcake.” Vi’s hair hadn't yet gone gray, but it had lost its signature red hue years ago, faded over time to a dull brown.

Caitlyn gave her wife a sad smile. “Life has not been particularly kind to either of us,” she admitted. “At least you’re not going gray.”

“Yeah, well I’m also not fifty,” Vi countered as she pulled away from her wife and went to sit down. Caitlyn gave an indignant huff as she followed. 

“You’re lucky we’re married, Violet,” she warned, using her wife's full name. “Otherwise I might take offense to some of these jests.” 

Vi just rolled her eyes before the mirth slowly left her face and she let out a melancholic sigh.

“What’s wrong?” Caitlyn asked, concerned.

Vi looked at her wife sadly. “She’s getting weaker. The shimmer’s wearing off,” she said. “I had to help her today. Unscrew a gear." She shook her head. "Wasn't even that hard.”

Caitlyn’s eyes widened. “And, she let you?” Vi nodded. “She let you come close?” Another nod. Caitlyn blinked in surprise. “Then… maybe it’s finally time.”

“I don’t know,” Vi’s face twisted into a grimace. 

“Come on, Vi,” Caitlyn said. “It’s been years, decades since she’s even done anything. If the shimmer is wearing off, then that means we have to do something soon!”

“The only reason she lets me around is because she doesn’t recognize me, Cait,” Vi said angrily. “To her, I’m just the nice lady who watches her work and sometimes brings food! She doesn’t even remember who I am!”

Caitlyn sighed. “Then what’s the problem? I don’t understand. She let you help her today, why wouldn’t she let you do more?”

“Because I don’t want her to explode,” Vi said softly. “She’s… she’s still not well, Cait. She still talks to people who aren’t there. There are still things that set her off.” Vi shook her head. “Sure, she’s more subdued, and I’ll admit she hasn’t tried to blow anything up lately, but… that doesn’t mean she won’t try if she recognizes one of us.” Her eyes lifted to meet Caitlyn's. “Especially if she recognizes you, Cupcake.”

Caitlyn’s eyes softened and she reached out to caress her wife’s face. “But you said it yourself. The shimmer is wearing off,” she reasoned quietly. “We don’t have much time.”

Tears slowly pooled at the corner of Vi’s eyes. “But, what can we even do?” she asked, defeated. “I don’t want her to spend the last few years or months of her life in a psychiatric ward. They’re no better than prisons.”

“You said she was more docile,” Caitlyn said. “That gives us some time. So let's focus on the other problem.”

“She’s more docile because she’s getting weaker,” Vi said quietly.

“I know you don’t believe that,” Caitlyn replied firmly. “She hasn’t been weaker since she’s stopped lashing out. She’s only been getting weaker recently.” Vi was silent. "We have to believe that she's at least a little better."

"That doesn't just... happen," Vi countered.

"Maybe not," her wife admitted. "But we can choose to believe that it has, or we can continue leave her on her own." That shut Vi up.

“Fine,” she finally said. “You’re right. We… we have to at least try. I won’t…” she trailed off as she swallowed. “I’m not going to just leave her again.”

Caitlyn leaned over and kissed Vi on her temple. “We can go see Viktor tomorrow,” she said softly. “If anyone would know what to do, or where to even start, it’s him.”

Chapter 2: Part II

Chapter Text

Part II:

It had been a while since Vi had been to the academy.

In the first few months following Jinx’s attack, all there was to see of it was rubble. And in the years following, it had been too painful for Vi to visit even briefly.

But, Vi’s work as an enforcer had needed her to go and visit semi-regularly and the pain slowly dulled over time. Not completely, but it didn’t squeeze her heart quite as much to walk through the opulent halls towards Viktor’s lab.

He let them in immediately, and Vi shot her wife a confused look. “I may or may not have sent a page ahead,” Caitlyn whispered. “This seemed important enough to do so.”

“Does he… does he already know?” Vi asked, worried. Caitlyn shook her head and Viktor cleared his throat.

“So, I am told you have a problem that I may be well suited to solve?” he began with an eyebrow raised.

Caitlyn nodded. “Yes,” she said. “It’s about… the effects of shimmer. We didn’t know who else we could turn to.”

Viktor nodded. “Shimmer is a finicky thing,” he mused as he gestured to the right side of his body. “It’s very hard to fully predict its effects, and even more difficult to regulate them. The one thing that we do know, however, is that it is temporary.”

Now it was Vi’s turn to raise an eyebrow. “But, you’ve perfected it! You’ve used it to heal yourself? How is it… temporary?”

Viktor chuckled. “To be honest, I have no idea how I’ve ended up where I am now,” he answered. “Nights and nights of endless experimentation on myself, on an unstable hexcore, with shimmer. Countless sacrifices along the way… so many of them regrettable…” he trailed off as a far away look filled his eyes. Then, he snapped back to reality. “And yet I still have no idea.”

“But you know more than the rest,” Caitlyn led hopefully.

“Certainly more than most,” Viktor admitted. “I can try to help you. What is the problem?”

Caitlyn and Vi looked at each other for a moment in relief before she began to explain. “There is someone we know who was seriously affected by shimmer. Her life depends on it and, well, it's wearing off.”

Viktor nodded sympathetically. “They usually do. As a matter of fact, they always do,” he said. “Why not give her more? That usually will solve the problem.” 

Vi shook her head aggressively and Caitlyn continued. “We really don’t want to do that, if possible. The way it was introduced into her… wasn’t normal. This isn’t an addiction problem. It’s even more than a dependency I’d say. It’s like her… her lifeblood.”

“Hmmm,” Viktor hummed thoughtfully. “So we’re talking about the work of Singed,” he deduced. Vi nodded gravely and Viktor’s face darkened. “I see.”

Caitlyn folded her hands nervously. “She needs help,” she said. “And you were the only one we could think of.”

“Who are we talking about?” Viktor asked. “Who would this even be for? Do I know them?”

There was a deep silence in the room for a few moments as Caitlyn tried to figure out how to either come up with a cover, or break the news. It turns out, she wouldn’t need to.

“It’s for Jinx,” Vi said bluntly.

Viktor’s eyes widened. “Jinx? The terrorist?” He raised an eyebrow as Vi grit her teeth. “She’s still alive?”

“Yeah,” Vi scoffed. “She’s still alive.”

“I didn’t even realize,” he said. “She’s been so quiet. I- most of us just assumed she had blown herself up or something decades ago.” He looked back at the two women in his lab. “And you want to… help her?”

Vi’s face darkened. “Yes,” she said. “You got a problem with that?”

Viktor held up his hands in submission. “No,” he quickly replied. “You have done a lot for this city… for me. I trust that whatever you have planned, that it’s for the good of Piltover.”

Caitlyn nodded in thanks. “Thank you, Viktor.”

“So,” he said as he leaned against his desk. “What is her condition? How is she affected by it?”

“She was overcharged with it a long time ago,” Vi explained. “Oversaturated to try and heal her dying body. But, it’s wearing off. She’s getting weaker, but not older.”

Viktor raised an eyebrow questioningly. “What do you mean?”

“She doesn’t look a day out of her twenties,” Caitlyn said. “But she’s almost forty-five.”

“Thin, too, real scrawny,” Vi added. “There’s no way she could have possibly done any of the things she does without it. She used to barely eat or sleep, too. But lately, she’s always tired. She can’t do the things she used to - they’re getting difficult for her. The glow is leaving her eyes…” Vi trailed off as tears threatened to blur her vision. “She’s dying.”

Viktor nodded solemnly. “Well, as I said, shimmer’s effects are almost always temporary. Even such a large dose has its limits. You said you think it's the shimmer that's been keeping her alive?” Caitlyn nodded for Vi. “Then, frankly, it’s a miracle that its energy has even lasted this long.”

He scratched at the purple veins creeping up the side of his face. “I don’t know if there’s much that I can do,” he admitted. “The hexcore is able to stabilize the effects of shimmer, but only to a degree. I’m not sure that it can-”

“You have to try!” Vi interrupted him, her voice raised. “She’s dying. And I don’t know how much time she has left!”

Caitlyn pulled her wife back, placing a firm hand on her shoulder to try and calm her down. “He knows,” she assured her. “We will find a way, even if it's not with hextech.”

“Singed is long gone,” Vi muttered. “No one who produces shimmer still knows the extent of it’s power. This is the only place we have. I’m not leaving empty handed.”

“If I can ask,” Viktor asked gently. “Why do you care about her so much? After everything she’s done?”

Vi simmered quietly and she clenched her fists. It was all she could do to contain her anger. The comforting hand that Caitlyn placed on her shoulder also helped. “She’s… she’s my sister,” she finally managed to say. “And I don’t care what she’s done. I promised her once that nothing would change that.”

Viktor gazed at her for a few moments before nodding in understanding. “I can understand the burden of attachment to something that others deem I shouldn’t become involved with,” he said simply before turning to grab a journal. “We do things that others may never comprehend for that burden.” He paused, and then glanced back at Vi over his shoulder. “For what it’s worth, if everything you have told me here is the truth, I believe you are doing the right thing.” 

Caitlyn nodded her head in silent thanks.

"I will, of course, help you,” he added as he took a seat. “But first, you must tell me everything you know.”

Things began to move rapidly after that. Viktor had not so subtly told them that time was of the essence, and Caitlyn suggested that they go to visit Jinx immediately afterwards. Vi was hesitant to overwhelm her sister, but Caitlyn always had a way of convincing her.

“It’s for her own good,” she said quietly. It was all she needed to say, really.

Vi paused at the entrance to the ventilation shaft when they arrived, and Caitlyn almost fell into her. Vi put up a hand to steady her wife. “You should… wait here,” she said. “I don’t want you to… trigger her or anything.”

Caitlyn nodded. That was a good idea. Setting off Jinx is the last thing they wanted to do. She let Vi go on ahead of her and hung back, observing where they were. 

She hadn’t ever been this far down in the Undercity before. They were very close to the mines and the air was stale and sour. The only light came from her flashlight and a single, green bulb that marked the entrance to the ventilation shaft where Jinx’s home was.

It was definitely secluded, and if her and Vi’s character analysis was correct, she could understand why Jinx would choose such a space. She was in hiding, alone, and this was certainly a good place to do so.

She was broken out of her thoughts by Vi reappearing through the entrance. “She’s not here,” she said.

“Oh,” Caitlyn replied. “Is that unusual?”

Vi shrugged as she slipped her hands into the pockets of her jacket. “It’s a little weird. She usually only leaves at night. She has this sort of schedule from what I’ve seen,” she explained and she leaned against the frame.

Caitlyn’s brow furrowed in understanding. “You’re concerned,” she said without question.

Vi scoffed. “What? No,” she tried to brush it off. “Jinx can… she can take care of herself. She has for over twenty years. She’ll be fine.”

“Violet.”

Her full name made Vi relax against the rock face. “I’m not… concerned about her,” she reiterated with a sigh. “I mean, I am. Just not about her not being there. This whole thing just has me kinda on edge, y’know?”

Her wife stepped forward and placed a reassuring hand on her shoulder. “We need to do this,” Caitlyn said softly. “Even if it goes south, it’s what she needs.” She caught Vi’s eyes and held her gaze. “It’s what you need.”

Vi let out a deep sigh. “You’re right,” she said quietly. “Why are you always right? You’re not allowed to always be right.”

Caitlyn just chuckled softly and shook her head. “It comes with experience,” she chided as she leaned in for a hug.

With a roll of her eyes, Vi wrapped her arms around her wife. “I have experience too, you know,” she muttered. “What do they call us? ‘Piltover’s Finest ?’”

“Something like that,” Caitlyn replied with a laugh as she pulled away. Her gaze shifted seriously again after a moment. “So what do we do now?”

Vi shrugged. “Come back tomorrow?” she suggested. “Or we could stick around. But I have no idea when she will be back.”

Caitlyn peered around the corner to look into the shaft, but she couldn’t see very far. “Is there even a place for us to wait?” she asked. She had to admit, she was curious to see this workshop. Vi had described it to her several times, but she never could quite picture it fully.

With a sigh, Vi read right through the question and rolled her eyes again. “Yeah, Cupcake. Follow me,” she said and led her wife through the opening.

Caitlyn’s eyes widened as they stepped out onto the large ventilation fan. Giant, neon murals covered almost every surface. Monkey heads and giant deep sea creatures that she had only ever heard about. Caricatures of countless faces, many of whom Caitlyn had never seen before, and several whom she never thought she’d see again. Her gaze lingered on a large mural of what looked like a young Vi and Powder together, right beneath a face that looked as though it had been drawn with utmost care. A face Caitlyn had never expected to see in any capacity ever again - Silco. 

Vi coughed, and broke Caitlyn out of her awe. “Jinx… she did all this?” the former sheriff asked. It was a far cry from the vulgar graffiti they used to find left behind at the scenes of Jinx’s carnage. Vi only nodded in reply before she gestured to a worn down couch at the edge of one of the other blades. Caitlyn followed her to it and sat down beside her, still taking in the chaotic beauty of the place. “It’s oddly beautiful,” she whispered in awe.

“She always was an artist,” Vi mused. “The walls in our home used to look like this until she learned better.” She nestled her head into Caitlyn’s shoulder and closed her eyes. “I was happy to see that she never lost it.”

Caitlyn wrapped an arm around her wife and continued to take in the violent tapestry that ringed the shaft. It had taken her some time to originally come around to the idea that Jinx could be saved - that Jinx was worth saving. But over the years, and as Vi told her more and more about her little sister, the more Caitlyn had realized that there was more to the young girl than bombs and bullets. That her chaos was, in a way, just as amazing as Vi’s heart. And as Vi had told her about her visits, and how Jinx acted now that her aggression had calmed down, Caitlyn’s heart had continued to soften towards the idea to the point that she too had begun to push for it.

What she saw here, in Jinx’s private sanctum, solidified that fact. Vi was right. Viktor was right. She was right. They were doing the right thing.

The thought comforted her as she slowly drifted off - tired from the long day and the stress of finally taking action on this front. However, it had only felt like she had just closed her eyes when she was suddenly startled awake by the barrel of gun being pressed into her forehead and a raspy voice speaking to her.

“Hey, Hat Lady,” Jinx said. “Been a while since I’ve seen you.”

The old lady never visited twice in a row. And she never brought anyone back. Ever. That was one of the reasons Jinx let her stick around.

But now, both of those truths had been made lies by the scene before her. The old lady was asleep on her couch, right in Claggor’s spot , cradled in the arms of her guest. And damn, could she have picked a worse guest? 

It had been a while since she had seen Hat Lady. Jinx couldn’t tell how long it had been. Time was one of those things that always seemed to slip away from her. But even she could tell it was years by the streaks of gray in her otherwise perfect blue hair and perfect Piltie face.

“Caitlyn,” Pink quietly reminded her and Jinx glared at the apparition. Of course Pink would remember her name. Not that Jinx had forgotten her name. She just didn’t like to acknowledge it. Hat Lady was more fitting of her title anyways, what with her stupid big hat and all. Even if she wasn’t wearing it right now.

But why was she with the old lady? Jinx and the old lady were… they were a thing now. A recurring, yet unspoken thing. The closest thing that Jinx had to a friend since- don’t say her name!

Before she could even comprehend what she was doing, her gun was out and pressed into the forehead of Hat Lady. The action caused the older woman to stir and wearily open her eyes, and they widened immediately as she focused on Jinx.

“Hey, Hat Lady,” Jinx said. “Been a while since I’ve seen you.”

There was a time once when Jinx would revel in the look of subtle fear that crossed her rival’s face. A time where that would be the sign to chuck a bomb or pull out Fishbones and wreck some havoc as she watched that fear slowly transition to anger. 

Now, like most everything else she used to enjoy, it just made her feel even more empty.

But feeling empty would not break her resolve. She tightened her grip on her gun and pressed it further into the lady’s forehead. Her eyes darted towards the sleeping old lady, her friend , and then back to Hat Lady just as quick.

“Why are you here?” she finally managed to ask.

The lady didn’t respond immediately, but that was okay. Jinx was patient. For now.

When the lady finally did speak, her words didn’t make any sense. “We wanted to see you,” she replied simply.

Now that almost made Jinx drop her gun. They wanted to see her? What kind of shimmer-crazed bullshit was that? She wanted to see her? Not get her, not chase her, not kill her. See her?

“Why?” Jinx pressed, trying to keep the confusion out of her voice. It didn't really work. But she didn't let the Hat Lady answer, either. “You’re not gonna take her,” Jinx whispered, her voice practically hissing as she fought back the tears pricking at the edge of her eyes. “You’re not gonna take her away from me. Not again.”

The woman’s eyes widened in realization. “You…” she began. “You know who she is?”

Jinx raised her head and squeezed her eyes shut, trying desperately to block out the static she knew was about to come as she took a deep breath. “It’s easier…” she said in a pained voice. “...if I don’t.” She opened her eyes slowly, ready to face whatever hellspawn decided to mess with her this time. 

But she was met with silence. A cold, eerie silence. Even Pink, standing right beside her, didn’t say anything. She just gazed up at her with Hat Lady, both with understanding faces, as if what Jinx had just said made total sense. She almost preferred the voices.

“It’s okay,” Hat Lady Caitlyn finally said. “I’m not going to take her from you.”

“LIAR! ” a faint voice called out from the depths of the ventilation shaft, and Jinx suppressed a shudder at how much it sounded like her own. “You’re lying,” she said weakly. 

Caitlyn shook her head as much as she could with a gun pressed into her forehead. “No, Jinx,” she replied. “I promise you, I’m not going to take her from you again.”

Jinx growled as she lowered the gun from Caitlyn’s head and let it hang limply at her side. “ Would it really be so bad to just try and trust her? ” Pink asked hopefully. “Just this once? ”

“Yes!” Jinx hissed. Pink flinched away and took a few steps away from her, and immediately Jinx panicked. “No, wait, I’m sorry,” she called out to the girl only she could see. “Don’t leave me! N-not again,” Jinx choked out as she reached for Pink.

But Pink shook her head with a small smile. She didn't look scared. Almost... hopeful. “I’m not leaving you, Powder, ” she said as she began to fade away, and Jinx didn't even realize that the name didn't affect her. “I’m right there. ” Tears welled in Jinx’s eyes as she watched the last of her demons turn from her and walk away. For the first time in twenty-eight years, four months, and seventeen days, Jinx's mind was completely and utterly silent. 

Then suddenly, the woman next to Caitlyn shifted and let out a quiet groan as she started to stir. Jinx snapped her head around far too fast and watched as the old lady began to wake up.

No no no, the old lady couldn’t wake up. Because once she woke up, and she was with Caitlyn, it was all over. Jinx wouldn’t be able to pretend anymore, it would be right in front of her. She'd have to face it. She didn't want to face. "Please, don't make me face it." But there was nothing she could do to stop it as the old lady turned and blearily opened her pale violet eyes and…

Vi.

Violet.

Sis.

Vi.

Chapter 3: Part III

Two sisters finally reunite. Jinx's shimmer finally gives out.

Chapter Text

Part III:

Vi had never sat up so fast in her life.

The sight of her little sister on her knees crying awakened that same instinct that she had felt so long ago, and an immediate desire to comfort her overwhelmed her soul. But she also knew who Jinx was now, and despite her urge to comfort her, that reality held her back. Instead, she was forced to watch as the girl broke down in front of her.

What did she miss? What had happened?

She shot Caitlyn a concerned look at her side, a silent question as to what had forced her sister into this state. Caitlyn just shook her head in reply. “She’s… she needs you,” she whispered quietly, and gently nudged Vi forward. The momentum forced Vi off of the couch and she slowly, almost robotically, moved towards her sister.

“H-hey,” Vi said quietly, and the only sign that the girl had even heard her was a slight twitch in reply. She didn’t even really know what she was supposed to say, so she opted to simply call out. “Hey, Jinx?”

Jinx looked up at her with tears streaked across her gaunt cheeks, and a flash of recognition lit up her eyes. “V-vi?” she whispered, her voice hushed, as though she couldn’t really believe it. As if she said the wrong thing or even breathed too hard, the vision of Vi would just drift away as it had so many times before.

But at that tiny whisper, Vi let out a choked sob and rushed forward with her arms wide. She crashed into her sister full force and swept her up in a tight embrace. Jinx’s head immediately found Vi’s bulky shoulder and pressed into it as she wrapped her thin arms back around her to return the hug.

The embrace was an explosion of catharsis - a culmination to nearly three decades of lost sisterly love thrown away into violence and chaos and time. Both women wept as they held each other, letting out everything that they had stew inside them for so long.

Vi had to be careful not to crush the small girl in her arms as she squeezed her tightly, refusing to let go no matter what. “I should never have left you,” she whispered gently into her sister’s ear. “I never wanted to leave you. I wanted to come back, but I…” she trailed off in a choked sob. “I couldn’t and I’m so, so sorry.”

Jinx only somehow found a way to squeeze her tighter. “I don’t care anymore,” she mumbled into Vi’s neck. “Just please don’t go.”

“I’m not going anywhere, Jinx,” Vi whispered in reply. “I’m right here.”

“P-promise?” Jinx stuttered, refusing to let go.

Vi smiled, even if her sister couldn’t see it. “I promise, Jinx.” 

The two of them stayed like that for a long while, simply in each other’s embrace as they both cried into the other’s shoulder. Whether they were tears of relief, sadness, or at a sudden realization that their decades of familial strife were over, neither could begin to guess. Perhaps it was a culmination of all three. But slowly, together, they calmed down, and they both had a chance to simply breathe again.

“Thanks,” the frail girl said quietly after a moment. 

The near-silent word startled Vi, and she pulled away to look down at her sister. “Thanks?” she echoed.

Jinx’s eyes avoided hers, and the girl somehow managed to get even quieter. “For calling me… Jinx,” she whispered. “You never did before.”

“Oh,” Vi said, and her posture softened even more as she pulled Jinx back into a tight hug. “You don’t need to thank me for that,” she replied quietly. Her mind drifted back to all those years ago when she had desperately wanted to reach out and give her sister this hug. “I love you. Even though you’re different, even though we've both changed. I’ll always love you, Jinx. You’re my sister.”

It seemed as though those were the words that broke the last shreds of chaos from Jinx’s tortured mind. The small girl let out a low cry of emotion before she buried her face as deep into Vi’s shoulder as it could go. Her entire body pressed up against her sister, as if trying to physically submerge herself in the warmth and comfort that her presence promised her.

She so badly wanted to believe that promise, and this time, it seemed as though it really was different. “I l-love you, too, Violet.”

Vi’s arms ached from how tightly she was holding onto her sister, but she couldn’t care less. She nuzzled her face against Jinx’s head, and gently kissed the top of it. Out of the corner of her eye, she could see Caitlyn as she crouched down next to them, trying to catch Vi’s eye. They gazed warmly at each other as Vi rubbed Jinx’s back in slow circles as her sobbing gently petered down to a silent shudder every few moments before she finally grew still and relaxed in her hold.

“Jinx,” Vi whispered after a moment. She shuffled to let her sister pull away, but she just continued to hang on Vi’s shoulders. Vi chuckled, at first just thinking that her sister had cried herself to sleep as she had done numerous times when they were younger. “Jinx, comeon,” she nudged her sister.

A gasp came out of Caitlyn’s mouth as the movement made Jinx fall backwards and the girl fell limply onto the metal blade of the large fan. Vi’s eyes widened and she had sprung forward in a moment to check on her sister. 

“No, no… no, please no,” she whimpered as her breathing became increasingly frantic as she shook her sister’s limp body. Her hands desperately searched the young girl's neck for a pulse, but she was shaking too much to accurately tell. “Fuck, fuck… fuck! ” she cried out in frustration. This wasn’t real. This couldn’t be happening.

Caitlyn gently pulled Vi’s hand away from Jinx’s neck and placed a steady two fingers against her pulse point. Her eyes widened quickly, and she looked at Vi with a pained expression on her face. “She’s still alive,” she said. “But barely. We need to get her to Viktor now.”

With shaking hands, Vi picked up her sister and cradled her small, limp body in her arms. And then, she ran.

Viktor’s eyes widened as the door to his lab was nearly blown off its hinges by the force of Vi’s shoulder. The woman practically fell through the doorway, a small, frail body clutched in her arms as she rushed into the room.

“Please!” she choked out. “You have to save her!” 

Caitlyn came through the door shortly thereafter as Viktor swiftly cleared off his lab table and guided Vi over. The woman helped Vi lay her unconscious sister down, before gently leading her away so that Viktor could work.

The arcane scientist held back a gasp as he laid eyes on Jinx. It was hard to believe that this petite, near-death girl was the cause of all the pain and terror that had wrought itself on Piltover all those years ago. At first glance, without knowing, it barely even looked like Jinx could have been born when the chaos was happening. 

But he did know, and for a moment, Viktor questioned his motives. Was he really about to help her? Save her life?

A single glance at the tortured look on Vi’s face answered his question for him. Of course he was. 

He hurried to grab his hextech tools, laying them out on the table beside Jinx as he searched for a pulse. This wouldn’t work without one. After an agonizing few moments, he finally felt it, and let out a breath he hadn’t realized he’d been holding. He went over to a safe in the wall and unlocked it, pausing only for a moment before he took out a small, brass box and placed it alongside his tools.

Caitlyn and Vi watched from across the room, the former holding the latter back before she could rush forward to her sister’s side. “Calm down,” she whispered into her wife’s ear. “It’s going to be okay, Viktor knows what he’s doing.” The words offered little comfort, but Vi grabbed and squeezed Caitlyn’s hand in acknowledgment as Viktor removed a large, pulsating purple mass from the box and placed it in the receptacle of some hextech contraption.

“I have… no guarantee that this will be permanent,” he said, turning to face the two women with a grave look on his face before he continued. “Or even that this will work at all.”

“Is there a chance?” Caitlyn asked. Viktor nodded in reply, and Caitlyn looked down at her wife. Vi closed her eyes for a moment, and when she opened them back up, they were full of a raw determination that Caitlyn had not seen for years. She gave a quick nod, and signaled to Viktor that he should begin.

Viktor flipped his goggles down and flipped a switch on the device before he turned back to the two women behind him. “You may wish to wait outside,” he said. “It might not be pretty.”

“No,” Vi said firmly.

“Vi,” Caitlyn said gently as she placed a firm hand on her shoulder. “Let’s go for a walk.”

Vi shook her head. “I’m staying.”

“Vi.” Caitlyn’s voice was firm, the same tone she always took when what she was saying was going to happen whether the other parties involved liked it or not. Vi hung her head in defeat. She let her wife lead her out of Viktor’s lab and into the hallway, where they took up a languid, idle pace.

“I can’t lose her,” Vi said quietly. “Not again.”

“You won’t,” Caitlyn replied, conviction in her voice. “Jinx is strong, stronger than most. She will make it.”

Vi was hesitant to let that hope burn in her chest, but she found that she didn’t really have a choice.

When Viktor came to get them an agonizing three hours later to tell them that he had finished, Vi nearly bowled him over to get back into the lab. She didn’t even wait to hear if her sister was okay - she couldn’t wait. She would find out on her own, for better or for worse.

The girl - no, the woman - lying on the table was completely different from the frail, young girl that Vi had carried into the lab hours ago. Her skin was richer rather than the sickly pale hue it was before. Her muscle mass had increased to a normal amount. Her hair was a deeper shade of blue than it was before, only featuring streaks of the previously lighter color. She looked healthy. She looked… older. She looked her age.

And then her eyes fluttered open.

Vi’s knees gave out and she collapsed at her sister’s side as two, beautiful gray eyes blearily looked around the room before settling on her. “V-vi?” Jinx asked weakly.

“Yeah,” Vi whispered, and took her sister’s hand. “I’m here.”

“Y-you… didn’t leave,” Jinx whispered, almost as if she were in awe.

Vi let out a choked sound as the tears she had held back finally spilled forth. “No, never again. I’m never leaving you again.” She smiled softly down at her sister. “I’m sorry that I ever did, and I couldn’t come back.”

A relieved smile crossed Jinx’s face, and her own eyes began to shimmer with tears. “Where am I?” she asked, trying to blink the tears away. “I feel… funny.”

“You’re safe,” Vi replied quickly. “You’re topside, but you’re safe. We brought you to a friend of ours, he fixed you up.”

“Fixed me…” Jinx trailed off as she looked down at herself, and Vi gave her a steady hand to guide her into a sitting position. “Whoa…”

“Y-you… grew up,” Vi said as her lip wavered. She tried to stop it, but she found she couldn’t. 

Jinx lifted a hand to run her fingers over her flush skin, and she stopped when she came to the first tuft of her signature cloud tattoo. “My tattoos are faded,” she said quietly. Vi let out a choked bark of laughter, which caused Jinx to quickly turn her head to see her sister holding back laughter with tears in her eyes. “W-what?” she asked curiously. “Do they… do they look bad?”

The strange sense of familiar nervousness, and that childlike wish for her approval only made Vi smile wider. It brought her back to the first time that Powder had braided her hair and asked if Vi liked it, or the first time that she had brought Powder on one of the jobs. Her sister was still in there, Jinx or Powder, it didn’t matter. Whomever she was, it was fine. Her sister was back .

“Come on, sis,” Vi said as she helped Jinx stand up. “Let’s get you home.”

It had been so long since they had been together - since they had been a family - that Vi didn’t really know what to do. It felt strange to walk around Piltover with Jinx voluntarily. It was not a reality that Vi had ever imagined actually happening. She had fought for this for so long, so tirelessly, but now she was faced with a pressing question. What came next?

Obviously, Caitlyn and her would prioritize getting Jinx the help she needed, both physically and mentally. But after that? Did they just move on and pretend like nothing had happened? What were the next steps?

As if she could sense the turmoil within Vi’s mind, Caitlyn placed a gentle, comforting hand on her back. A silent gesture that said “ whatever it is, we’ll do it together. ” And for Vi, after so much strife, that was enough.

They’d taken what was perhaps the longest road to get here, with so many bumps along the way. But in the end, the three of them left the ornate corridors of the academy and walked out into Piltover together, the city that Caitlyn was from, the city that Vi had learned to call home, and the place that one day, maybe , Jinx would be able to call home, too.
