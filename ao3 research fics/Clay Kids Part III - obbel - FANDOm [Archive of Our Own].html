Summary:

“I don’t think you want someone like me in your group,” Lee says, stepping back, and Jet steps forward quick and grabs his wrist.

“Believe me, you don’t know what I want,” he tells him, his eyes intent on Lee’s. On Lee’s gold eyes. “I get it, man. Dirty little secret. You think you’re the only one who’s got it? Me, I got off lucky—I take after my mom.”

Chapter 1

Chapter Text

Two lovers, destined for one another


The song Jet sang gets stuck in his head. That’s not them. It’s not. They’re not lovers. They’re not anything. Even if Zuko wanted them to be. A war divides them, just like the spiritsdamned song. Zuko chews on his lip.

“I’m sorry,” he says.

Jet looks at him, his eyes soft and warm, and the expression is too much, too open and vulnerable, too willing to forgive the unforgivable secrets Zuko keeps from him. Zuko looks down, at their clasped hands, thinks about taking his back, about taking everything back.

But the ground begins to rumble, and Zuko is on his feet just as fast as Jet, fingers no longer intertwined.

Without a word, they sprint into the larger tunnel, toward that maddeningly handleless door. As it sinks into the ground, they move as one—Zuko dropping low, Jet soaring high. Two halves of the same strike.

They hit the Dai Li agents at the same instant. Both go down before they can seal the door. Zuko lowers his agent carefully to the floor. Jet lets his drop with a clang.

“What was that for?” Zuko hisses at Jet.

“Sorry,” Jet says, in that voice that means he’s not sorry at all. “Take his uniform,” he suggests with a kick to the unconscious agent’s ribs.

“No,” Zuko shakes his head. “It’s too heavy. I’m faster like this.”

Jet shrugs. “Suit yourself,” he says and starts making his way down the path.

The Dai Li’s base is huge. Zuko isn’t even sure if this is the main hallway, but it goes on seemingly forever, breaking off into smaller side tunnels and more stone doors. They peer into each room they come across. Most are empty, glowing eerily, green and sinister. They walk past a room full of women, all dressed identically, repeating the same phrase in unison.

“I’m Joo Dee. Welcome to Ba Sing Se. I’m Joo Dee. Welcome to Ba Sing Se. I’m Joo Dee. Welcome to Ba Sing Se. I’m Joo Dee. Welcome. . .”

A shiver works its way up Zuko’s spine. He glances at Jet, who looks just as disturbed. He’s about to reach for Jet’s hand again when a door starts to open to their left. They duck into an empty room, watching carefully through the window until the agents pass.

“Maybe we should split up,” Zuko says when they’re gone.

Jet whips his head around to stare. It’s overwhelming, a little, a lot. Zuko’s been the subject of a lot of Jet’s scrutiny, but Jet is staring hard enough to break. This feels like bending, like being bent, like Jet has moved around the organs of his body, plucked them out of the way, to see right through him, right to his very core, to the place where he keeps all his secrets.

Zuko flinches. Not yet. It’s not the right time, yet.

One day, he resolves, he will bare himself to Jet. He will correct all the half-truths and blatant lies he has allowed—encouraged, manipulated—Jet to believe, let Jet see all his jagged edges and torn up pieces, and not just the ones he wears on his face. The truth of the circumstances of his birth. And he will face whatever judgement Jet chooses to deliver because of them. But that has to come later.

“Why?” Jet hisses at him.

“We don’t have a lot of time. We have to meet Smellerbee. We can cover more ground this way.”

Jet stares hard, once more, and then nods. “If something happens, get out. Go meet Smellerbee. Don’t come for me.”

“Jet—” Zuko starts, but Jet cuts him off.

“Just promise me, Li,” he says. “Promise me, you’ll get out. I’ll be fine, I swear. These guys got nothin’ on me.”

It falls flat, Jet clearly nervous about what comes next. But Zuko doesn’t call him on it. He just nods. “You, too. I can handle myself.”

Jet pulls him in close, presses a kiss to the top of his head. Zuko doesn’t want to move away—ever.

“I know,” Jet says. “I’ll meet you outside.” And then he’s walking toward the next tunnel, the next empty room.

Zuko runs in the opposite direction.

.

.

.

Li’s right, it’s faster this way. Jet looks over his shoulder, though, and can’t help feeling the same way he did leaving Smellerbee, like this is the last time he’ll see any of his friends ever again.

But it can’t be. He still has to find Longshot, has to get them all of this spiritsforsaken hole. And go home to Li.

No—shit, that’s not right. Because Ba Sing Se is not home, Li is not home. Even if Jet wanted him to be. Home is gone, burned down and washed away and buried deep. And Li is just Li.

Stupid, stubborn, beautiful Li, who kisses girls and holds Jet’s hand and walks through the world with his too-gold eyes. Jet wants everything from him, anything. Anything at all.

Damn it.

Li told him to look for the records room if he couldn’t find Longshot’s prison cell. An organization like this must keep records, he insisted. Jet wouldn’t know, but Li said it like he knew what he was talking about.

It’s not going to help, though. Jet could barely read the menu at Pao’s, the one time it was offered to him—one of the earliest times, when he went in to look after Li, before they knew he wasn’t a normal customer. He knows the character for tea, and that’s about it. The rest is trivial, just extra things in the water. Mushi always brews exactly what he’s in the mood for, anyway.

So even if he found the records room, it wouldn’t tell him anything. He looks out for Dai Li with keys instead, or Dai Li escorting prisoners, or any indication of where the cells might be.

Jet doesn’t find any of that, but he does find the Avatar’s bison, held captive in an enormous room. The poor thing is chained on a stone dais, a cruel metal cuff on each leg pulling in six different directions. It gives a low growl, eyeing Jet, in the Dai Li uniform, suspiciously, but it can’t really move away.

Or move closer to bite him, which. Maybe Jet is lucky it’s restrained. The last time he saw the Avatar was after, and he’s not sure how much the bison understands, but given that it’s the Avatar’s bison, he’s willing to bet it understands enough.

“Hey, there,” Jet says quietly. He puts his hands up, approaching carefully.

The bison snorts, hot air hitting Jet’s face, then growls again, baring so many teeth. Jet stops moving forward.

“It’s okay,” he says in his most calming voice, the one he hasn’t used in a long time—the one he used to use during the Terrible Twos (and Threes and Fours) when the Duke wouldn’t settle down for anyone. “I’m not here to hurt you. I’m here to help.”

Then, of course, an alarm starts blaring, and the bison goes ballistic, and Jet has to dive for cover to not get trampled to death.

.

.

.

Smellerbee finds Song almost immediately. The women’s cells are just where the agent said they would be. Smellerbee runs down the stairs and waits until the coast is clear to pick the lock, thanking the spirits for a normal door with a key and none of that bender-controlled nonsense.

It’s dark inside, just like the rest of the base, lit up only by the glowing green crystals. Smellerbee can just make out the women in their cells, a number both greater and fewer than she was expecting. She thought they’d be packed in like sardines, what with all the whispers about how many the Dai Li have disappeared. But it’s not like that. Every cell is full though, with women and, disturbingly, girls, some younger than Smellerbee. They cry, or they rock themselves, little arms wrapped around their own skinny bodies, dirty thumbs stuck in their mouths.

Some of the older women seem to be asleep, lying listlessly on the cold floor. Some of them stare at the empty space in front of them, in a trance, their faces cast in sinister green shadows. Their eyes move back and forth, following an invisible something as it bounces from left to right. Smellerbee walks right past them, and they don’t so much as glance up at her.

She hurries to the end of the row to where Song and her cellmate—

Wait a minute, is that—

It is—it has to be. She’s still wearing her Water Tribe clothes and everything. The Avatar’s friend.

The one who had a crush on Jet.

.

.

.

"Psst!” someone hisses. Katara turns her head around to look.

Outside the cell is a small person with a bowl cut and a headband, red lines painted on their face. Despite the strange getup, they look vaguely familiar. Katara tilts her head, scrutinizing.

“Smellerbee!” Song says excitedly next to her. Katara’s eyes dart between the two of them, still unable to place this person. Then Song drops her voice lower, asking, “What are you doing here?”

Instead of answering, the newcomer, Smellerbee, whatever kind of name that is, just starts picking the lock on the cell. Katara leans over to watch small, nimble fingers work a little metal tool into the lock, wiggling it slightly. Smellerbee squints one eye in concentration, and in no time, the lock pops open, the cell door swinging free.

“We’ve gotta go,” Smellerbee says. “Jet and Li went to get Longshot. We’re meeting up outside.”

At the name, Katara goes white. That’s where she knows Smellerbee from. She’s one of Jet’s.

“No!” Katara blurts out, grabbing Song’s arm, pushing Song behind her. “We can’t trust her!”

But Song twists out of her grip, steps around Katara’s side. She’s frowning. “What? What are you talking about, Katara?” She is halfway out of the cell already. Smellerbee motions for Katara to follow, but Katara glares at her, shaking her head.

“She’s with Jet! Jet. . . Jet’s not a good guy.” Katara crosses her arms over her chest, feet planted firmly.

Song turns back to look at her. She’s smiling at Katara like Katara is saying something funny. “No, it’s okay,” she says. “I know Jet. He’s, I mean, he was good to me. So. Maybe whatever happened with you was different. But he was good to me. Come on, we should get out of here.”

Song’s all the way out, standing with Smellerbee. They look at her, expectantly, and the women in the other cells are starting to take an interest, too. Some look curious, some scared, some downright hostile.

“Are you coming?” Smellerbee asks, sounding annoyed.

Katara grinds her teeth. She shouldn’t. This has Jet written all over it. If he sent Smellerbee, there’s no way he’s doing this out of the goodness of his heart. It’s got to be a setup, a trick. Just like last time.

She scoffs, remembering the ice she pointed at his face, his feral, manic grin—and all the lies he told, lies slipping so easily through his lips, about how the village deserved to drown. Lies that would have made her drown the village.

She’s blushing, she knows, cheeks burning not just because Jet lied to her, and she believed it, but because he also held her close, told her she was good, and he was good, too. His strong arms around her, flying through the trees to their hideout. The Freedom Fighters. The name comes back to her now—

But her thoughts are interrupted by one of the women in the neighboring cell. “Hey,” she calls. “I’ll go if she doesn’t want to!” She reaches an arm out to grab at Song. “Let me outta here!”

“I’m coming with you,” Katara makes up her mind. It’s the best option, for now. She dealt with Jet once before. She can do it again. Easy.

Song nods, looking pleased. “We’ll come back for you,” she promises the woman. “All of you.”

“C’mon,” Smellerbee motions. “We gotta go.”

.

.

.

Smellerbee leads them back up the stairs. Unfortunately, a pair of Dai Li waits for them at the top.

“Stop right there!” one of them yells.

Smellerbee is on him, knives drawn.

Song takes out the other one, throwing her own knives. It pins him in place, and huh—that’s kind of cool. Smellerbee’s got to ask Song to teach her how to do that. Later. When they’re all safe.

For now, she just has to stab this guy until he quits yelling so much and—shit, she didn’t stab fast enough. Another three agents rush in to help the first. Katara launches herself at them with no hesitation, but with no water to bend down here, she’s only able to duck and weave, dodging the rocks hurled at her and getting in the occasional punch.

Song knocks out the agent she pinned with a nasty left hook. Smellerbee’s agent has pretty much stopped making noise, so she runs toward them both, wishing she had brought a longer blade.

The three of them handle the remaining Dai Li. Smellerbee leaps onto one’s head and digs her fingers into his eyes and her knives into his softer parts. He howls, then trips over his own loosened stone. She rides him all the way down, hopping off right before he collapses to help Song and Katara fight the others.

Blows fly until Song drops low, ducking in quick to trip one of them, and he falls against the wall. Song stomps him out with a vicious kick to the head.

Katara winces. “You didn’t have to—”

The last remaining agent sees an opening and charges, sending huge chunks of the wall flying her way. Katara dodges just in time and pivots to counterattack.

Smellerbee tries to get on top of him, but he keeps her off by bringing down another part of the wall. It knocks her off where she’d tried to climb him like a tree. He gets Song, too, a big piece of rock landing on her shoulder, another bit flying off to cut her face. It stuns Smellerbee, briefly.

She tries to shake it off, but the world is spinning and she can’t find her balance. Song rushes over to her, moving the pieces of stone off, even as she grimaces, grits her teeth through the pain. They can’t let this guy get one over on them.

“Stay back,” Katara orders them, and ordinarily Smellerbee would resent that, but her vision’s starting to go black, and there’s a ringing in her ears that only gets louder, and the world is spinning so fast that Katara’s moving in a different way now, arms and legs flowing like water. That can’t be right, but—oh, wait, no, she’s a bender. 

Smellerbee tries to blink away the black spots. In between blinks, the ringing gets unbearably loud. Smellerbee tries to get back on her feet, but Song keeps her sitting down. 

Smellerbee blinks even faster. She sees Katara wipe the sweat off her forehead and then attack with it, bending it into sharp, slashing ice. She didn’t even know that was possible. . . 

Katara takes down the last agent, and Smellerbee goes down, too. The horrible, horrible ringing is the last sound she remembers. 

. 

. 

. 

It’s not faster on his own. Zuko has no one to watch his back anymore, has to creep among the shadows, pressed impossibly flat against the cold, stone walls. Then those walls start to move of their own accord, and an alarm starts blaring, and Zuko just runs. 

There’s a swarm of Dai Li agents coming from somewhere ahead. He doesn’t have to be an earthbender to feel the way the ground shakes. It must be an entire platoon, running at full speed. 

Zuko flies through tunnel after tunnel, stopping short and doubling back as the stone doors slam shut, as intersections suddenly block themselves off. The prison is going into lockdown. Smellerbee must have gotten to Katara, or maybe Jet found Longshot. Now they all have to get out of here.

He curses. His mental map is wrong, rendered obsolete by the constantly changing tunnels, all the dead ends that were open just a minute before. It’s made so much worse by the constant noise of the alarm. The green crystals lighting the base begin to shine brightly, almost blindingly. 

Zuko ignores it all as best he can, trying to force his brain to work through all the chaos, to remember the way to the main entrance. He thinks if he turns around, it’s just around the corner, to the right.  

He hurries along a side tunnel and only narrowly avoids a stream of Dai Li running past. He throws himself back against the wall and waits for them to pass. The agents flow out into the main cave, lining themselves up in perfect parallel lines. Zuko crouches down near the mouth of the side tunnel, as close as he dares. 

The agents stand at attention. Even from his hiding spot, Zuko can see how tense their posture is, still as the stone they move so effortlessly.  

He’s never seen so many out in the open like this, either. On the outside, they lurk in the shadows, almost always in pairs but sometimes alone, stepping in quietly to end conversations, kidnap refugees off the streets. Here, though, safe in their own base, they line up one after the other, revealing their numbers. There are so many of them, enough to control all of Ba Sing Se. 

Some of them are so young, barely out of adolescence. Barely older than me, Zuko thinks. He puts the thought out of his head. It doesn’t matter. They’ve all been trained to stalk prey, strike quickly and ruthlessly. Zuko could take out a few rows, maybe, if he resorted to firebending. But the rest would overwhelm him in a matter of minutes. 

Zuko means to retreat back down the tunnel, look for another exit, when his breath catches in his throat, threatening to choke him out. His blood runs cold, and it spreads, his whole body frozen in place. He can only watch in horror as a figure descends to the front of the room, flanked on either side by earthbender agents moving the ground. 

She’s here. His sister. Azula. 

She’s dressed in Earth Kingdom robes, but no one could ever mistake her for anything but Fire.  

“Will you make yourself useful and turn off this irritating noise,” she commands rather than asks of the earthbenders, gesturing violently in the air. The earthbenders bow to her and disappear. Soon, the alarm stops sounding. Zuko still doesn’t feel like he can breathe. 

Azula calmly makes her way to the front of the room. She stands perfectly still, shoulders squared with her hands clasped loosely behind her back, and addresses the Dai Li. 

“Remarkable,” she sneers, contempt dripping off her words like poison from the fangs of a two-headed rat-viper. “When Long Feng placed the Dai Li under my command, I assumed he was running a professional organization. It seems I was mistaken.” 

Her golden eyes flash dangerously. Zuko can sense the fear in the room, palpable and cold. 

Azula continues, “Instead, I find myself standing before a collection of incompetent, bumbling liabilities.”

She spits the last word. Despite himself, Zuko hopes no one flinched where she could see it. 

“Lake Laogai was designed so people vanish without a trace. How impressively ironic that the only thing to disappear under your watch was your ability to keep them there.” 

Azula takes slow, measured steps along the first row of Dai Li agents, her eyes narrowing slightly whenever someone shifts or swallows. 

“Did you mistake Long Feng’s absence as an excuse to neglect your duties?” She tilts her head slightly, as if she were really asking a question. 

No one is stupid enough to answer, and she moves on, stopping in front of one agent, leaning down, centimeters from his face. Zuko wonders if it’s random, or if she blames him in particular. 

“Let me be perfectly clear: You will recover every escaped prisoner. You will erase every rumor. And you will do it swiftly. Because the next disappearance I arrange will not be so forgiving. Dismissed.” 

Chapter 2

Chapter Text

Humanity kills, we all suffer from insanity still / And if I said it then it is or it’s gonna be real


Before the end of Azula’s speech, Zuko runs for it. His mental map of the base is still a mess, but he has to get out of the main area before the Dai Li, has to get to Jet. Fortunately, it seems like every single agent was called in to listen to Azula, so he doesn’t worry about stealth, just moves as fast as he can through the never-ending tunnels.

Zuko thinks he’s getting close to where he and Jet split up from Smellerbee, but then the tunnel moves on its own, and he throws himself into the next room. It’s an office—belonging to someone important, from the looks of it.

He starts to rummage through the desk for anything useful, prisoner records, a freaking map of this place, but the sound of the door slamming open sends him scrambling underneath the desk, praying to whoever is listening that no one comes over here.

“Princess Azula,” a Dai Li agent says, and Zuko’s heart leaps into his throat. He presses his own hands to his mouth, determined not to make a sound, but his heartbeat hammers so fast, so loud, it must be audible.

“What,” his sister snaps at the agent, voice flat with boredom-tinged irritation. Zuko hears her pacing around in front of the desk. He presses his hands harder into his mouth, bites the inner corner of his cheek. “What is so important that you kept me here in this hovel?”

“We have identified a terrorist cell within the city made up of those born of dual heritage.”

“And?” Azula hisses, irritation overtaking the boredom. “You think I care about tainted blood?” She seems to be moving further away, back towards the door. Zuko doesn’t dare to take his hands off his mouth—his heartbeat still pounds in his ears. “We don’t recognize them. People born of two nations rarely know where their loyalty belongs.”

“No, Your Majesty,” the agent says hastily. His voice is also farther away, like he’s following Azula. Zuko hopes against hope they are on their way back out. “We learned the identity of their leader. He is definitely one of yours. The Dragon of the West. We are moving to take him into custody.”

They’re going to take Uncle.

Zuko bites down on his cheek so hard he draws blood. It takes everything in him not to leap out from under the desk, shove his dao up against the agent’s throat. But Azula is still there, even if she is not responding yet. Zuko shakes with fear and rage, unable to move, to make a sound. He has broken out into a cold sweat. It feels like he is dying.

Azula remains silent for an unbearably long moment. Then, finally: “My, my. This is worth my time.” Just from her tone, Zuko can imagine the expression on her face, a cruel smile curling her lips upwards but only at the edges. “Good job, you—whatever your name was.” She’s probably gesturing in the air, a dismissive wave. “Take me to him.”

“Yes, Your Majesty,” the agent replies. “We planned to take him in earlier, but as you’ve convened most of our agents—”

“Be quiet,” Azula cuts him off. She’s probably holding her hand up. “I have no interest in listening to excuses for your failures. Take me directly to the exit. I will personally oversee this mission, since it’s painfully clear that, left to your own devices, you’re incapable of executing even something so straightforward as bringing in an old man. A pity. One would think an organization like this could function without constant supervision.”

With that, the door slams open and closed, and Zuko lets himself breathe a shuddering breath.

Uncle. One thought plays on repeat in his mind, screaming just as loud as the alarm Azula silenced. He needs to leave, now. He needs to find Jet and get out of here, get to the palace before Azula does, and rescue Uncle.

But Jet could be anywhere in this maze. He could have been caught once the alarm sounded, and the Dai Li were on high alert. He could have found Longshot and gotten out already. Zuko doesn’t even know how to get out of here. His head swims with the possibilities, and it’s too much to think through.

He knows this: Uncle is in danger. Azula is on her way to him. Longshot and Song have been captured, and Jet and Smellerbee are on their way to them. Reasonably, that leaves Zuko to rescue Uncle, right? That seems fair.

Jet is capable, Zuko reasons to himself as he sneaks out of the room, trying to orient himself in the labyrinth that is Lake Laogai. Jet is capable, and he told Zuko to get out if anything happened. Something has happened. Jet will be fine.

So will Smellerbee. They’ll find Longshot and Song. They don’t need Zuko. Uncle needs Zuko. Uncle, who has been with Zuko since his banishment, except when Zuko told him to get lost, who followed Zuko anyway and got them jobs, and seems to be happy here, wants to see Zuko happy—

Zuko is hyperventilating, hauling in shuddering breaths as he stands, frozen in place for the second time.

No! he tells himself, forcing his breathing under control. His years of meditation and breathing exercises will not go to waste. He will not die here in this miserable hole because he couldn’t get it together. Uncle needs him. He is getting out of here, and he is saving Uncle.

.

.

.

They can’t find a proper exit, only a window looking out, the water’s reflection glowing green from the crystals inside. Song smashes the glass, letting the water pour into the Dai Li’s base. Katara bends it out of their way, pushing it back until Song can climb through the open window.

Katara passes Smellerbee to her. She’s so light, even for her size, barely weighs more than a baby otter-penguin.

Once they’re all through, Katara takes a deep breath and bends the water to form an air bubble around them, propelling them to the top of the lake.

Underwater, Smellerbee drifts in and out of consciousness, alternating between clinging tightly to Katara and then letting go, then grabbing for her again in a panic. They finally break through the surface, and Katara can breathe a little easier, in more ways than one. Standing at the shore of the lake, Katara’s more than ready to head back to the Upper Ring, but for Smellerbee’s faded voice.

“Wait,” Smellerbee says faintly.

“What is it?” Katara asks, not stopping, only pausing briefly, adjusting Smellerbee draped over her back. Song keeps a hand on Smellerbee’s shoulder, making sure she doesn’t fall.

“We have to wait. . . wait. We have to. . . for Jet.” Smellerbee is so hard to hear. “. . .and Longshot. . . Li.”

“Azula!” the girl in their group yells out, her tone suggesting they have history.

“Her Royal Craziness!” Sokka adds. “Oh no, we’re screwed!”

Zuko’s eyes flick to Jet, who still frowns deeply, whose fists still clench. He hasn’t said anything since they made it to the gondola.

“That’s my sister,” Zuko confirms, looking away. “She’s. . . a problem. But she’s probably only interested in me, and maybe the warden. I have an idea. But you have to let them,” Zuko points to the second gondola, the one full of guards, descending toward them, “onto the gondola.”

Jet scoffs loudly. Zuko ignores him.

“Pretend to lose the fight. We need her to think you’re under control so she’ll go after me. I’ll draw her away and then catch up with you.”

Sokka exchanges glances with the normal sized man and the girl. They seem to be having a conversation with their eyebrows alone, the result of which is Sokka giving him a hard look and then nodding once.

“Okay,” he says, gripping Zuko’s shoulder. “Anything to make Lady Lights-Your-Face-on-Fire go away!”

“Sounds like a trap,” Jet interjects, his first words to Zuko since they left the cell. He stares at Zuko, gaze hard and unblinking. “How do we know you won’t just betray us all as soon as your sister gets here?”

Zuko squares his shoulders. “I’m not on her side. Even if I helped her capture you, I’ve already been disowned. They’d still keep me here. That’s what my father said.”

Jet’s frown deepens. “You’ve been in contact with your father?”

Zuko forces his breath into obedience, keeps it from becoming the stream of flames he wants to unleash. “He told me via the warden. He didn’t even come visit himself. I’m trying to help you!”

Jet looks like he has more to say, but the mountain of a man interrupts him. “You got a better plan, kid?” he asks Jet, voice flat and unamused.

Jet glares at him, then eventually shakes his head.

“Then let’s take the only option we got. I’m not getting my face lit on fire.”

“Watch the warden,” Zuko says to the enormous man before Jet can say anything else. “That gag looks loose.” He gets a death glare from Jet and the warden in return as the man ties the gag tighter.

Zuko memorizes Jet’s face anyway. Even like this, he wants to remember. Just in case.

“We’ll wait for you,” the girl promises.

Sokka nods, and then Zuko slips back out the window of the gondola, and climbs to the top, waiting to face his sister once again. And because she’s her, she takes flight much more gracefully than he did, blue flames igniting from her fists and lifting her into the air, propelling her straight at him.

“I didn’t know firebenders could fly!” Zuko hears Sokka squawk.

Zuko takes a deep breath, hoping that his timing is right, that this gamble will pay off, that for once in his miserable life, his luck will hold. He waits for the other gondola to reach theirs, makes sure the guards have connected the two, and that the fight has started. Then he jumps onto the roof of the other gondola.

“Azula!” he yells. “What are you doing here?”

She flies up to land opposite him on top of the second gondola.

“Recapturing escaped prisoners,” she says breezily.

“That didn’t go so well for you at Lake Laogai,” he says. “Have you gotten better at it since then?”

She narrows her eyes at him. “What do you know about Lake Laogai?” She takes a step back, one foot sliding into a stance he recognizes. He mirrors her, hands coming up with his palms facing outward.

“I know you lost control of the Dai Li! What will Father say when you lose to me, too?”

“Clever, Zuzu,” she sneers at him. “I see what you’re doing.” She punches a fist of flame at him, but it’s half-hearted, nowhere near her usual power.

He returns fire anyway, putting his all into it. She blocks, but he’s glad to see it strains her, just a tiny bit.

“I’m not doing anything,” he says, moving as she moves, always keeping her in his line of sight.

“Oh, come on,” she says, jumping and twisting as she kicks, sending a burst of fire off her foot. He sweeps wide, sends his own. “You’ve always been a terrible liar.”

He doesn’t deny it. “I’m the one you want,” he says, and punches in rapid succession, forcing her to jump. “Your fight is with me!”

He lunges forward, his fists throwing twin arcs of orange flame. She sidesteps with infuriating grace, her blue fire answering in a blast. He dodges, but it scorches the cable above their heads. The gondola sways precariously.

He finds his footing, then rolls, coming up low, and aims an uppercut toward her stomach. She deflects it with a sweep of her forearm, barely batting an eye.

“You wouldn’t fight me in Ba Sing Se!” he yells. “You had the Dai Li do it for you. This is your last chance! I know I can beat you!”

She laughs, and it’s just as piercing as before, maybe even more so, now that they’re out in the open, and the sound can carry across the boiling lake.

She doesn’t answer him, just attacks with blinding speed, lightning crackling in a lethal combination of kicks and punches. He absorbs the first blow, managing to redirect the flames, but the second sends him skidding backward across the gondola roof.

He steadies himself at the very edge, arms making wide, desperate circles. Over his shoulder, he risks a glance at the water churning violently, impossibly far below them. Then he jumps over another blast, landing in a somersault. He leaps up, sending as much fire at her as he can manage, buying himself enough precious seconds to sever the connector between the gondolas. He lets the flames erupt from his palm, into the air this time, and the heat and power push their gondola down the cable, away from the rest.

“Princess!” a guard calls.

“Deal with the prisoners!” Azula snaps at the guard. “I’m more than capable of handling this.”

She turns her full attention to Zuko, and he can feel the fury radiating off her. He braces his core as she unleashes herself completely, and the world turns blue.

Lightning-laced fire explodes from her fingertips. The sheer force of it pushes him backward centimeter by centimeter. Her fire finds every weakness, every gap to exploit, scorching his arm, his cheek, his thigh.

She drives him to the very edge, one foot dangling over the empty space below. A blast strikes him dead center, and he can barely diffuse the flames. He stumbles backward, slipping over the edge, only catching himself by his fingertips on the upturned eave of the gondola’s corner.

She stands over him, grinning wicked, and it’s move or die. Digging deep, he twists his body and swings himself sideways. Her killing blow goes screaming past his good ear and vanishing into the open air below. The momentum carries him up and over, feet slamming back onto solid metal.

He blocks, deflects, dodges, putting to use everything Uncle and his tutors and the Freedom Fighters ever taught him. Turning her strength against her, he lets her fury burn itself out, until he sees the opportunity to drop through the gondola window, right as her next blast scorches the air where his head was not a second before.

Through the roof, he can hear the crack and hiss of her fire, searching for him.

But the gondola has arrived back at the platform, and he throws himself out the door, sets off in a sprint.

.

.

.

“We should leave already!” Jet insists. “He’s with his sister. We can’t trust them together. They’re Fire Nation, and they’re going to attack us!”

“Did you see that fight?” Sokka asks incredulously. “Azula almost killed him! I really don’t think they’re working together.”

Jet paces back and forth, pulling at his hair. That looks like it hurts.

“He saved us from Princess Psycho,” Sokka points out. He wishes Jet would sit down. It’s making him anxious. “We promised to wait for him.”

“They’re Fire Nation!” Jet says again, like Sokka didn’t hear him the first time.

Sokka is about to point out that they’re all very aware of that, but Chit Sang stands up, towering over Jet. Jet glares up at him, but Chit Sang says, in his deep, boom-y voice, “Sit down, Jet. You’re being too loud.” It’s a tad hypocritical, but it does the job. Jet sits and glares at all of them and pulls at his hair some more.

They had pretended to surrender to the guards, like Zuko told them to, waiting until he got his sister far enough away, and then overpowered them and left them tied to the gondola platform. Jet had wanted to kill them, which was just so typical. He was way too quick to drop the net on a man, if you asked Sokka.

Which his dad did. His dad asked if that was maybe not a bad idea. It made Sokka look at him different, a different different from the different when they reunited at Chameleon Bay, which was way different from when he’d left the South Pole.

“No, Dad,” Sokka had said, firm, and his dad had respected his choice.

But he’d been unwilling to leave them totally free, and they eventually compromised on knocking the guards out and tying them up.

Jet did it with his fists. Sokka had to look away, but he could still hear the sounds, bones connecting with bones, and the number of hits it took it made his stomach roil. Suki showed him a choke instead, cutting off the blood to the brain just long enough to put someone to sleep, and it made Sokka feel a little better. But not very much. The guards still looked really dead when they left them there and started climbing the volcano.

Suki sits next to him now. Sokka would put his arm around her, but he feels a little weird doing it in front of his dad. And Jet too, actually. Plus, it’s still incredibly hot. They’re not very far from the volcanic lake.

Sokka sits and sweats through another of Jet’s rants about Zuko, another “SHUT UP, JET!” from Chit Sang, and an answer to his question of how Chit Sang and Jet knew each other: “We were assigned toilet duty together, but he was too pig-chicken shit to help out.”

Sokka’s about to make a joke, but there’s a loud noise, like something ricocheting off metal, and then Zuko reappears, sprinting and carrying some kind of lopsided shield thing. Water drips off it, but it still does a great job of deflecting the freaking fireballs being pelted at his head while he runs for his life.

“We gotta go!” Zuko yells as he runs past them. “We’re stealing my sister’s air ship!”
