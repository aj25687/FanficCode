According to her mother, her worst trait was her inability to take no for an answer. 

 

That’s what she liked to tell Caitlyn as a kid, over and over again. It was true. Caitlyn would demand a mile when she was given an inch.

 

Her parents wanted a lady. A diplomat, a girl who kept her hands clean and her head down. A councilwoman. Caitlyn could never be that. 

 

From that moment when she was a kid, running through the woods, leaves underfoot, wind whipping through her hair, she knew her place wasn’t behind a desk. It was out here, with guns that felt like extensions of her arms and that rush, the kind she’d never get pouring over paperwork. 

 

She remembers the hesitation of the enforcer chief as she fired. The sinking feeling. The party, trophy thrust into her arms, adults twice her size jingling with noise. The building was beautiful and lights stunning, the dresses gorgeous, and all she wanted to do was run away. 

 

“Why are you shooting?” That was the force captain’s question. She didn’t say anything. Didn’t know at the time. She just...didn’t take no for an answer. 

 

Sometimes, late at night, she wondered why the hell she couldn’t take that. A comfortable life with a husband and kids. A closet with clothes and paved roads and pens. Mirrors and windows. Beauty. Grace. She had everything, and she wanted more.

 

Her bed was so soft she thought she could sink into it. 

 

If the whole world tells you to do something and you refuse, does that make you the idiot? 

 

Maybe. 

 

She tried to be her mother’s daughter for a time. But her dresses got dirty and it was always the girls her mother hated being kissed rather than the boys she liked. 

 

It was lonely, standing out on that staircase alone, trophy in hand. It was even lonelier pretending to be someone she wasn’t in a crowded room of people. 

 

So when she was eighteen she missed her own birthday party and signed up to be an enforcer. And when her mother got her stained right next to her family’s tent, Caitlyn found a crime scene, and she didn’t stop.

 

She knew what they all thought of her, a rich princess who would go running at the first sign of danger.

 

She saw it in Jayce’s eyes, the only one who believed in her, who she thought understood it. Having the fucking audacity to offer her a desk job, like she some stray dog.

 

Vi thought it too when Catilyn first met her on the other side of prison bars.

 

Vi also called her cupcake. And looked at her with something like yearning. Vi was pretty, and strong and had tattoos Caitlyn wanted to kiss every inch of. Vi had a good heart, after everything. If it had been the other way around, Caitlyn would have lost hers. 

 

Vi came with her, and a bunch of enforcers, to talk to the council. Caitlyn didn’t think she would.  

 

Caitlyn hadn’t ever been in the council building. She’d heard about it, from Jayce and her mother. About how beautiful it was. How open it was. How intimidating it felt, standing in front of the council. How much responsibility it was, and an honor, to serve it. How crazy it was for Jayce, sitting in one of those chairs. 

 

And here she was. Less than a hundred feet away. 

 

Vi let out a long breath of air beside her. Her eyes subtly flicked from side to side, tracking the enforcers around them. The missing hextech gem felt heavy in Caitlyn’s pocket. 

 

“Hey,” She said, “This will work,” 

 

Vi nodded, not making eye contact. 

 

She held her head high, as the shimmering door opened. The people of the undercity deserved better than what they got. The people at the very bottom, living only for shimmer deserved better. The people who had their lives ruined by enforcers deserved better. They deserved politicians who actually did something, for once. Vi, who had long scars on her back and an old look in her eyes, Vi who had been fighting her entire life, deserved better. Caitlyn wouldn’t take no for an answer. She had a job to do

“This is Vi. She was born in the undercity, even though we failed her in countless ways. She risked everything to show me what life way really is like down there.” 

 

Vi’s head turned, meet’s Caitlyn’s gaze. Surprised. 

 

“People are starving, sick, ravaged by shimmer, they live in constant fear of the coordinated efforts of violent crime lords. One man leads these efforts. Silco,” 

 

The council stared back at her, all eight of them. Caitlyn didn’t back down. 

 

“We’ve done investigations into Silco, they yielded no such level of organization.”

 

“And who lead these investigations?” 

 

Silence. The damning kind. 

 

“What does this Silco even want from us?” 

 

Vi stayed quiet next to her. 

 

“He believes the undercity should be independent, he calls it the nation of Zaun”

“What about these?” Jayce said, dropping a grenade on the table “Do you know who made them?”

She looked over to Vi. 

 

You don’t have to do this.

She kept walking.

“No!” 

 

“Cait, please,” Vi said, “Don’t make this harder than it has to be,”

Vi didn’t say anything. Caitlyn took her hand.

 

“And besides,” Caitlyn said, “We’re not done,” 

 

Vi looked at her, confused, “What else is there?” 

 

A few weeks ago, after Caitlyn gave Vi the potion, Vi jerked up, a panic look in her eyes. After she calmed down they were close, so close Caitlyn could feel Vi’s breath on her face. She thought about kissing her then. She didn’t.

“Woah,” Vi said, pulling apart, “That was…”

“...Honestly?” Vi said, smirk and all, “Yeah,” 

 

Caitlyn kissed her again. Just because she wanted to. Vi leaned into it, hand sliding down her neck. Caitlyn could still taste the disgusting fish Vi ate when they first went into the undercity. 

 

Their foreheads leaned together, Caitlyn’s hand tangled in Vi’s. 

 

“Do you wanna go inside?” Caitlyn said, “It’s still raining.” 

 

“If I go inside, I’m never coming out.” 

 

“Would that be so bad?” 

 

“I have to do this cupcake.” 

 

“Why?” 

 

“Because,” Vi said, voice cracking, “When people rely on you, you don’t get to be selfish,” 

 

The words were old, repeated, and worn. 

 

“What if,” Cait said, tracing circles on Vi’s arm, “We relied on each other?”

 

Vi looked at her as if Caitlyn were Atlas lifting the sky off her shoulders. She crashed in her, strong arms hugging her, Vi burying her face in Caitlyn’s neck. 

 

“I want you to rely on me,” Caitlyn said when Vi didn’t reply, “and I think you deserve to be a little selfish,” 

 

“Thanks, cupcake,” Vi whispered, and Caitlyn could feel her chest rumble as she spoke.

 

Things were going to be okay. After all, She wouldn’t take no for an answer.
