walking on water

orphan_account

Summary:

Prompt: “I may be the smartest man I know, but I’m nothing compared to you.”

Notes:

Re-uploaded prompt from 2019-ish.

Work Text:

Gellert’s hair in the sunlight was ethereal. Albus knew he shouldn’t be staring, but Gellert didn’t seem to have noticed. And if he had, he wasn’t saying anything about it. So maybe it was fine that Albus’s gaze lingered for too long, and that he occasionally stumbled over his step as they marched through the woods.

Their discussion had drifted back to the hallows, as it so often did. Gellert ran his mouth at full speed about the infinite possibilities of the Elder Wand. Albus punched in some questions and words of agreement whenever Gellert’s spiels were put on hold for an intake of breath.

It was yet another gorgeous August day. Albus recalled how much he had despised the sun in the beginning of the summer, when his whole world had narrowed down to being head and sole breadwinner of three. The days had stretched out into a haze of food, work and keeping Ariana under the radar. The blinding sunlight, on top of it all, had been almost insulting.

But everything had changed once he’d been introduced to Gellert. Albus was amazed, really, about how much leisure time he had when he actively chose to do his chores as quickly as possible. Yet no time with Gellert seemed long enough. He was always left craving more. 

They reached the lake in the middle of the woods, with a surface as clear as glass and snares of reed encompassing it. This was their usual hangout spot – they’d idle here for hours and let their conversation flow freely; words, laughter and spells flung between them.

Albus was about to sit down in the shade of their usual tree, where the grass had been completely ground down from their previous talking sessions, when he noticed Gellert making a sudden turn.

”Gellert?” he prompted.

Gellert fixed his attention back to Albus and started unbuttoning his shirt.

Albus’s stomach flipped. ”What are you doing?”

Gellert jerked his head towards the lake. ”It’s hot, come on.”

”Uh,” Albus said, eloquently. He immediately lost his train of thought when Gellert carelessly dropped his shirt to the ground.

Gellert started kicking off his shoes and socks. ”I think there might be some grindylow in there,” he mused.

Albus opened his mouth and closed it, several times. ”And merpeople. And who knows what other beasts,” he managed eventually. “Any muggle might walk by.”

Gellert looked at him. Albus wasn’t sure why he added that last part.

”The muggles don’t come this far out, you know that,” Gellert drawled. Magic was already buzzing in the air about him when he drew his wand out of his pocket, playfully twirling it between his fingers. He raised one eyebrow, as if in challenge. ”What, too chicken?”

Albus exhaled through his nose. Gellert knew all of his weak spots. ”Fine,” he grumbled, reaching for the buttons of his own shirt.

Gellert gave a full, unrestrained smile. He didn’t wait for Albus; instead stalking straight into the lake until the water reached his midriff. He leisurely waved his wand, creating tendrils of water that reached for the sky in spirals before letting them crash down on him in one big shower. Gellert swept a hand through his wet golden locks and looked utterly beautiful.

Stripping down to his trousers, Albus grabbed his wand but stopped short at the point where land met water.

Gellert swam a bit closer to where he was standing. “Aren’t you coming?”

“Watch this,” Albus said, with an air of mystery. He performed a few intricate waves of his wand. A ripple of magic, invisible to the eye, breezed past Gellert.

Nothing happened, and Gellert was confused, until Albus stepped on the water with his full weight – without breaking through the surface. Albus laughed when Gellert gasped a few words of surprise in German.

“How do you do that?”

Albus took a couple steps forward and walked around Gellert’s head to the center of the small lake. “It took me a while to figure out,” he admitted, trying to sound nonchalant about it while actually basking in Gellert’s astonishment. It took a lot to impress someone as talented as Gellert.

Gellert immediately proceeded to get out of the water and occupy Albus’s previous spot. He made a valiant effort to copy his complex wand pattern with no luck. Forced to think for himself, he considered different charms while Albus danced around and performed cartwheels, the surface below undulating in small waves. Albus had either combined spells or invented this particular one… neither of which would surprise Gellert, who had never met anyone as gifted as Albus.

After a good five minutes, when Albus had sat down and started forming sculptures out of large drops of water, Gellert finally figured it out. With an excited cheer, he cried out to Albus, who got up and gave him a round of applause.

“Come over here!” Albus called.

Gellert ran up to Albus, overcome with a surge of happiness. “I may be the smartest man I know, but I’m nothing compared to you,” he told him, grinning.

Albus beamed back, all freckles and crow’s feet and sunshine, and Gellert was overtaken by an impulse to reach for his face and kiss him.

Almost as soon as their lips touched, Gellert pulled back, realizing in a rush of panic what he was doing. Albus’s eyes were very wide – and that was about all Gellert had time to register before Albus frantically hauled him back in again and deepened their kiss into something wonderful.

When the kiss came to a satisfying end and they both withdrew by an inch or two, their breaths still mingling, they couldn’t help but stare at each other in equal amounts surprise and wonder. Then, Albus gave a small smile, and Gellert felt as if the wind had been knocked out from his lungs, as the terrifying thought struck him – he was in love with his best friend.

As soon as the thought occurred to him, he fell through the surface of the water. He came up, spluttering and coughing to the sight of Albus bent over in half, howling with laughter. Gellert pulled him by one of his ankles, cutting Albus’s laugh short as his spell broke off and he, too, fell down into the deep with Gellert.
