Chapter 1: Sober

Chapter Text

The second wizard war finally ended with the Order's victory. With all the events that followed, the summer vacation was postponed so that the school campus could be fixed and each family could regroup properly, after all, there were too many deaths for the One Who Must Not Be Named to truly be defeated.

The following year the students would have replacements for classes missed in the last two semesters due to the war and the invasion of Hogwarts. Graduating students would have the opportunity to redo their final year and obtain a basic education diploma, it was not mandatory and many decided to enter the job market straight away as employment rates were starting to rise again but this did not apply to everyone. Some outstanding students who decided to come back to finish their senior year were Neville Longbottom, Hermione Granger, Luna Lovegood, Ana Abbott, and even some Slytherin students like Blasio Zabini, Theodore Nott, and Pansy Parkinson.
The atmosphere inside the school at the beginning of the school year was one of union between the houses, except for Slytherin, which was stripped as the focus of villainy within the castle. It was no secret that everyone avoided crossing, talking, or even looking at any Slytherins. Even though the only one to take the opposite side in the war was Mr. Malfoy, and yet he had his reasons.

You see, my dear reader, I am not here to judge whatever decisions protagonists of the story make, since the role of deciding what is, or is not appropriate to the situation is up to you. However, I urge you to recognize that any decision cannot be classified as anything other than that, within the applicable reality there is nothing purely good or evil. Life is not a 19th-century romantic tale and neither is this story. So there will be no outbreaks of villainy or heroism. There will be only humanity, as it should be.

Given the setting, the school year's initiation ceremony brought a sober vibe to the main hall, McGonagal gave a lengthy speech on the need for unity and reconciliation within the postwar context, but even the new headmistress failed to achieve complete impartiality. After dinner, everyone went up to their respective dorms wondering how the school year would go without Harry Potter and all the extraordinary adventures that accompanied The Chosen One.

The first week of classes was quiet, but due to the reduced number of 7th-grade students, most classes were taught with students from all houses present. This applied, for example, to the potions classes that were still the responsibility of Mr. Slughorn, head of the Slytherin and also a hero at the Battle of Hogwarts alongside Minerva. He kept the Slug Club meetings, now being less exclusive. He was also commonly seen advising Slytherin students and defending the honor of his own house against all the vile stereotypes that haunted him.

One student who repeatedly sought his advice was Pansy Parkinson. Everything she experienced during the war made her rethink her entire life. During the war, she did not take sides although many assumed that because of her friendship with Draco, a Death Eater, she was one of the Slytherins who came to defend Voldemort. However, these were just guesses. Assumptions that, like it or not, affected her.

She didn't expect public recognition, far from it, she only expected indifference. Of course, it was understood from her entire past within the walls of Hogwarts that this would be the least amount of treatment within the students. The most benevolent ones took this path, yet most simply despised it. As well as Blasio. You see, she wasn't surprised or even upset about it, she just wanted to get through with good grades to get a good job.

Pansy was the typical stereotype of a popular rich girl. Always beautiful and attractive with a sadistic and prickly personality. She wasn't an easy person to deal with, she wasn't usually sweet or considerate. Quite the opposite. However, there were, in fact, exceptions. Those who managed to survive the storm that was her heart could be blessed with moments of encouragement and affection by the fireside. These exceptions were, by now, Mr. Zabini and Mr. Nott. Although Mr. Malfoy were, once, graced with this privilege as well.

Pansy didn't get along very well with girls, couldn't establish bonds of loyalty with them. Not that her relationship with Draco was completely mutual, but what could she do if the boy had her wrapped around his long and thin fingers? Every time she stared into those pale blue eyes it was as if he could actually see through her, read her and it completely disarmed her. She was then a helpless girl, hostage in front of those frozen lake eyes. Of course, the relationship between the two was quite troubled, the boy realized all this and could not stop himself from taking advantage of it. In every way possible. Blasio and Theo tried to remediate the situation in several possible ways, however, it's not as if Pansy didn't have a clear view of the whole situation. You see, my dear reader, the problem with things of the heart is that reason does not always play a role in decision-making. And that said a lot about Pansy, and about Draco too.

For the first few weeks, Pansy spent every night crying. After all, it wasn't enough that her summer vacation had been a real nightmare, she felt lonely without Draco around. Blasio was a good friend, really great. Theodore was her faithful confidant. But when you're 16, it's hard to overcome the pain of first love's end. Oh, reader, just because this story is not based on romantic ideals that it doesn't have romance among its subjects, for what would we all be without such encouragement in our hearts? But it is undeniable that with all the crying came a load of relief too, well, she wouldn't need to bend over to his will any longer, nor would she be stuck in her supporting role. She could finally focus on her and perhaps a more optimistic view of the future.

She was a good student, had excellent grades in D.A.D.A. (Defense Against the Dark Arts), and intended to become an Auror. This brings us to our potions class. This was a discipline she did with Mr. Nott, but without Mr. Zabine. There were other Slytherin students but she was not categorically extroverted. Pansy had a social mask, of course, like all of us, but it wasn't necessary anymore. Much less appropriate given the circumstances. Luckily or unluckily, her bench ended up being composed of Theo, Neville, and Hermione, with Neville beside her and Theodore beside Hermione.

As for Mr. Longbottom, he had been a great war hero and had gained relative prestige upon returning to school. He sometimes got applause in the hallway, and as much as he had gained relative self-confidence over the past few years, that didn't stop him from blushing and stammering his thanks. He was, after all, a shy boy and quite insecure. No wonder he had great affection for Luna who was, in his view, completely self-assured in all her authenticity. The girl had affection for him too, but it didn't surpass the admiration and friendship. He understood her perfectly and, although he was upset, he didn't revolt. He just accepted and decided to continue feeling very lucky that he already had her friendship.

In contrast, Hufflepuff Anna Abbott seemed to have a great fondness for him. According to Hermione, she had been seen looking at him knowingly and quite dreamily countless times. The boy was extremely kind, and as the years passed he also became relatively attractive. He, in his insecurity, told Granger that all this was just speculation and that she should stop imagining such things, Anna could feel some embarrassment if she listened. She was attractive, sweet, and kind. But he didn't see her as more than a good friend. So, to some extent, he understood Luna. In that way, their friendship was perfectly pleasant. Very rarely were their disagreements and there were never fights. At least until then.

Anyway, the four were on the same bench, because Ms. Abbott, like Blasio, did not attend potions classes. The two would be perfectly fine alone on the bench, but in this particular class, Pansy and Theo needed another pair and, at Slughorn's decision, ended up joining their pairs. As for Pansy, it goes without saying that she and Theo were great friends. As for Hermione, this one despised her, Neville was something between contempt and indifference. He tried hard not to get into conflict but wouldn't back down if he did. Something he adopted after the war was to always defend his point of view before giving in to peace.

Pansy avoided any dialogue as much as possible, she knew the position of each of the two and didn't want to generate more dislike. In the class in question, each one had to produce a Rejuvenating potion. Hermione tried to stay focused without worrying about the Slytherins. Please, don't misunderstand Miss Granger, dear reader, but she has acquired certain precepts about any student in emerald robes based on her experiences at Hogwarts over the years. Pansy was the protagonist of many of these situations, all of which were minimally unpleasant. About Theo, there was a certain indifference. But she couldn't help but assume anything. For there is this about us humans.

In the case of Neville and Pansy, each paid attention to their own workspace. Pansy wasn't terribly good at potions, but she was pretty good. Her Methodism and delicacy were great tools in the art of brewing potions and that gave her a fair standout among the others. Even during the period in which Prof. Snape administered them. Throughout the class, Pansy took a few peeks at the next table to see how Neville's preparation was going. She knew he had difficulties and thought that maybe a little kindness wouldn't hurt. Theo realized what was going on and smiled at the girl's attitude. A shy but genuine smile.

The girl made some subtle suggestions, a "If you cut the daisy roots more evenly, the result will be better." here, or a "Try putting in less leech blood." there. At first, Neville felt invaded and prepared to answer something but ended up muttering a shy "Thank you." paired with a bland smile. At the end of class, Neville's potion turned a slightly opaque acid green, while Pansy's was the same shade but bright. Hermione's was like hers and Theo's. Slughorn congratulated the Three's result and told Neville that his potion almost turned out perfect.

He smiled and thanked her. He looked at the girl beside him thoughtfully and when they were all released he waited for her outside the room as discreetly as possible. He couldn't quite describe what led him to take such an attitude when he was questioned later in the Gryffindor dormitory by Ginny. But the fact is, she was one of the last to leave, and seeing him outside she stared at him with a certain air of doubt, waiting for an explanation. He felt his throat go dry, he was slightly intimidated, and he couldn't think clearly either. For some reason, she was particularly prettier that day.

The first thing he managed to formulate before regaining his focus was “Why?”. The girl continued to stare at him with an air of doubt, until he completed the sentence, "Why did you help me?" She shrugged and explained with a short sentence, "I can't tell." and she tried to add a smile. He felt a twinge of curiosity, Pansy had a side he didn't know.

The fact is that a very thoughtful Neville went to the Great Hall, had a pleasant dinner, and exchanged discreet glances with the Slytherin. Blasio seemed to notice, who confronted her more at night inside the Slytherin common room while doing some homework for the next day. She just responded with a shrug and completed explaining the whole situation. Theo described the whole scene in an exciting way, finally, she was showing herself more pleasant to the others. Blasio laughed nasally and felt sorry for Neville. He had no idea in what he was getting himself to. Blasio thought to himself about the possible provenance of the situation and decided that, for now, he would just have fun with the whole situation.

He himself had changed his stance on many things, and his friendship with Pansy was one of them. He was now sure he could count on her and had decided he would look out for her. Not that she really needed it, but just as a form of care. He expected the same from her, and they both had this silent agreement then. That night Neville and Blasio slept thoughtfully, Theo thought about the possible outcomes of it, all while Pansy herself had other matters on her mind.

The next day, Neville went to Luna to tell her everything and ask for some advice. He wanted to repay the kindness but still didn't know which way was most appropriate. He told her all of it and added that he had thought of asking Pansy for a walk-in Hogsmeade. After all, the next weekend would be free for a visit to the village and Sunday would be Halloween. But he didn't think it was a good idea, the possibility of refusal was quite high and his colleagues from other houses might view the attitude with bad eyes. The ravenclaw told him to ignore it all and do what he wanted. He shouldn't be stuck with other people's expectations since he had his own. Neville smiled, Luna always knew what to say.

So the next day, he waited for her at the door of the spells class after it ended. She was accompanied by Blasio and Theodore. Both realized what it was when they saw the boy acting nervously in front of her. They looked at each other, accomplices, and walked ahead without even consulting her. She was slightly irritated by the attitude of her colleagues but tried to be patient. He, in slurred words, made the invitation. He knew all too well what prompted him to ask her for a ride on Honeydukes in Hogsmeade next Saturday, though he never admitted, his young expectations played a role in all of this. Now, what led her to accept the invitation, that is an absolute mystery. As much for him as for me, my dear reader.

Anyway, they both had dinner and went to their respective dorms. Neville did not told his friends what had happened and would not do so. At least not now. But Pansy's friends heard her tell it all in her neutral tone, bordering on interest. But she would never admit that she had her own expectations of all this. Not now, not ever. And that was one of the fundamental differences between the Slytherin princess and the young Gryffindor. For her, certain things remained in secrecy. From others and from herself. They both slept with their heads spinning in thoughts that night.
