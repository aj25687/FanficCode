On: Anger II

Aifrit

Summary:

Sevika has an anger issue.

She knows this. Has known this since she was a kid. With all the sumpsnipes who tried to pick fights or hustle her for the few cogs she had, her father made sure the same kids would never bother her again. The frustration and fear of being helpless under her father’s stern rule exploded. Always in her fights. Sometimes, elsewhere.

Notes:

Her father died when she was a preteen, and while a part of her was relieved, it left a gaping hole in her heart. Her mother did what she could, but there was no structure in Sevika’s life. And her anger became worse.

An older teen witnessed one of her street fights one day. She lashed out at him in self defense, thinking he was in on the assault, but he subdued her quickly and complimented her skill, though sloppy and unrefined. The teen, Vander, introduced her to his trainer at a gym well hidden in the backstreets of the Lanes.

The gym was old and musty, but a safe haven away from the rowdy streets. Sevika learned boxing and mixed martial arts from her trainer. He immediately recognized her issues and worked to get her to channel that anger in a more organized way through training and meditation. It stuck with her for the rest of her life. Whenever she feels overly stressed and frustrated, she’ll either hit up her favorite gym or work it out at home.

Work Text:

A heavy pattern of thumps startled you awake. You ignored your aching muscles just enough to hone in on the noise.

Thump! Thump, thump! Thump-thump thump!

You reached over in bed, finding the opposite side lukewarm and empty. Then everything clicked and you relaxed.

Sevika was up unusually early. From the sound of it, she progressed through her usual drills on the punching bag in the spare room. Her strikes rang out clean and precise.

There was no way you’d be able to get back to sleep. You got up and padded towards the room, leaning against the door frame.

Sevika saw you immediately and stopped, wiping her forehead. Gym shorts and a sports bra was all she had on. A thin sheen of sweat covered her face and neck.

“Did I wake you?” she asked.

You nodded.

She shook her head. “Sorry. Forgot t’ close the door.”

“Rough night?”

“Yeah. I just…” Sevika wet her lips. “Just need some time.”

And you’d give her exactly that.

You knocked the door frame. “Want some coffee?”

“That’d be nice. I’ll be out in thirty.”

Thirty minutes was more than enough time to make breakfast to supplement the coffee. Judging by her somber expression, she definitely needed both. You closed the door behind you. The strikes on the bag resumed, every one as precise as those that came before.
