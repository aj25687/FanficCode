Across the River

 

Pounding footsteps.

Crashing glass.

Screams and bellows.

The scent of metal, gunpowder, and smoke.

Tears falling as fast and hard as the day I was born.

"MOM?! DAD?!"

My godfather told me mom was going to be busy today, he told me to stay home. But when I saw the Enforcers flooding the streets, I knew something was wrong. I followed them and made it to the bridge, my eyes burning instantly as the smoke and flames rose into the air. Now here I was, running down the concrete and steel bridge, desperately trying to find her.  It smelt like burning garbage, burning flesh. I felt my throat throb. I felt like bending over, but instead I skidded to a stop when an enforcer clashed with a woman in front of me. Her hair was as blazing as a fiery red flame; the blur of its color and the smashing of a glass bottle were the only things I remembered before I was pivoting the other way, legs moving before I told them to, lungs burning, fighting for a fresh breath in the dense air that was my oxygen. I remembered the bottle’s shattering as I ran, the crash blending in a horrific symphony with the crackling of fire and the shouts of the enforcer. 

Coming back through a plume of smoke, the crack of a gunshot stops me in my tracks. Barely thinking, only hiding, I soon find myself in a crevice between a wooden pallet and a crate. So many sounds, so many scents, just as much blood... I wanted to scream, I wanted to vomit… I wanted my mom. 

I stayed hidden there for what felt like days, until the screaming stopped. 

An eerie, childlike voice sings from further down the bridge. I don't go towards it; instead, I run towards where the gunshot cracked, the echo of it still fresh in my mind. It was quiet. Too quiet. I nearly fall and fall when my food slides through a pool of blood. 

The smoke cleared a little further down and....

 

"Mom..?" 

 

My legs shake just as much as my voice does. I sprint towards the pile of rubble where my mother lies, her body just short of the bridge’s edge. No. Oh god no. Please, not her. Not her and daddy both. I made it to her side, my bleary eyes landing on her bleeding torso. It was everywhere; I didn't even know where she was hurt. I made it to her side. Her eyes are shut. I grab her hand. It's not as warm as I remember. I look back up at her eyes. 

"Mom, come on... You gotta get up..." I whisper. No response. My bleary eyes land on her bleeding torso. It was everywhere; I didn't even know where she was hurt. 

"You gotta get up, we gotta go home!" 

She doesn't move. I look around the bridge, trying to find someone.

"Help!" My voice cracks, barely echoing through the smoke. "Somebody! Anybody...!" The words barely make it out of my mouth before I begin to sob, the tears falling uncontrollably. I move to nuzzle under my mom's arm, my whole body shaking with each sob. It's not for a long while until I hear the falling of footsteps, and I sniffle, looking up. Through my fogged eyes, I see Vander. There was metal strapped to his hands, crimson tinted its bronze coloring. He looks down at me with soft, remorseful eyes. He kneels beside us, the weight of his steps settling the dust around him. He moves slowly, carefully, as if afraid a sudden shift might shatter what’s left of me. I could smell the scarlet on him through every sniffle.

“I’m sorry,” he murmurs.

The words break something open. I clutch at my mother’s arm, trying to pull it closer, trying to keep her here, trying to keep her with me—I need her with me!

But it’s too late. 

 

Vander’s hand settles on my shoulder—big, warm, steady—anchoring me when the world feels like it’s tipping sideways.

“We have to go,” he says, voice thick. “It’s not safe here.”

I shake my head at first, pressing my forehead to her freezing skin, wishing she’d stir, wishing any of this could be undone. Begging. But the smoke is growing heavier, curling around us like a warning. Vander gently lifts me, and I don’t have the strength to resist. My fingers slip from her arm, and the emptiness feels like a second death. As he carries me away, I twist to look back. Her silhouette lies framed in the flickering orange light, still and small against the ruin. My chest tightens, and fresh sobs claw their way up…but they don't leave. No sound comes out. They only sit in my lungs as her body sits in the rubble; quiet, stained, streaming out in slow, agonizing streams of liquid.

Vander pulls me closer, his voice low and rough. “I’ve got you.”

The world burns behind us, but I let my head fall against him, because there is nothing else left to do.

“Hey, Powder! Come look at this!” she blurts, dusting herself off. 

Powder gasps, already using Eris’s head to push off and get on the roof, eliciting a yelp from the girl below. Golden, shining, clean piltover. Buildings that held their chins up, streets that cut through sleek structures like an intricate labyrinth, a golden city that blinked back at her. Roaring overhead flew an airship; she flinched at its blaring, ducking down as it soared over the gleaming rooftops. 

“One day, I’m gonna ride in one of those things.” Powder said with an expression full of wonder. 

“And one day, I’m gonna shoot one of ‘em down,” Mylo added, making a little gun gesture with his fingers before Vi slapped his hand down. Eris snickers and walks behind Vi as Claggor speaks up. 

“Vi, are you sure about this? Look, if we get caught--”

“We’re not gonna get caught. We’ll be in and out before anyone notices.” Vi hisses. She comes to a stop on the roof and looks down. “All right, everybody, follow me. Just don’t look down.” And with a few jumps and agile moves, she’s across the alley and on another building. Eris swallows nervously, keeping her eyes up, on Vi, on the way light danced over the rooftops; anywhere but down. Don't look down. With a sharp breath, she lunges forward, her steps as light as a deer dancing over rocks and logs,  and within seconds she's right beside Vi. Mylo follows after, then Claggor.

“Couldn't we have at least just walked there?” Claggor complains.

“Gotta stay out of sight for this one,” Vi says, her eyes on Powder across the way. Little Powder, just a jump from the rest of them…but she’s got that look on her face.

“Called it.” Mylo starts, the familiar flavor of obnoxious slipping through his jagged teeth like bad breath, “This is on you, Vi.” He chirps. Vi only groaned.
Eris sighs and takes a step. “I’ll get her–”
“No!” Vi interrupts, looking back at Powder. With the smallest flinch, Eris shrugs it off.

“Powder, look at me. What did I tell you?” Vi called.

The blue-haired girl takes a shaky breath. “That… I’m ready.”

“That’s right! So?” Vi continues, trying to coax the girl over. And with a view of iffy glances down and a quick breath, it works. With some clumsy moves and a falling scare, all five of them are on one roof. Mylo scoffs and rolls his eyes, and Eris smacks him as they walk on. She never favored Mylo, not when he was always such an asshat to the smallest of them. Especially when he was only the second youngest, he had no room to talk, barely any to hold up that big head of his.
Coasting rooftops for a while, Claggor can’t stop worrying.

“What if Vander finds out we’re all the way up here?” He whines.

“Look around you. You think anyone topside is going hungry?” Vi snaps. “Besides, this is exactly the sort of job Vander would’ve pulled when he was our age.” She stops as they reach the edge of the roof. “I’m going. Are you with me or not?” The boys exchange a glance, Mylo shrugs, Claggor sighs in defeat.

“Vander is gonna kill us.” He grumbles

“Yeah, only if we screw up. So don’t screw up.” Vi barks, jumping onto the balcony below. Eris stood back with Powder, waiting for the–

“All clear!” Vi’s voice rings out. The rest of them jump onto the balcony below, looking around with curiosity. Eris goes to rattle the handle, groaning as it doesn’t budge. 

“Who locks their balcony?” She whines as Mylo goes to lockpick it. 

“There are tons of Enforcers down there…” Claggor mutters, looking over the railing.

“Means we’re in the right place,” Vi replies, glancing at Mylo.

The enforcers below seemed distracted by conversation.

“Like, hallucinations? Yeah, I am," the second answers. The first pauses—

“No, I mean dating—are you alright?” The conversation fades away as they walk off.

“Working on it. Seeing as I’m the only one who knows how to pick locks, I suggest--”

With a step from Eris, a foot flies by Mylo’s face, his words jumble at the loud bang by his ear, the force forward breaking the lock he was hunched in front of. The others did the same, grabbing anything that seemed valuable. At the sight of a chalkboard, Eris saunters over, head tilted in a silent study of the many illustrations and mathematical equations. 

“Must be an inventor.” Eris hummed. She barely spares the others a thought, too focused on the work on the board. Gears and calculations, the beginnings of a moving product, the pieces of a puzzle she wanted to solve; her eyes had stars in them. Just under it, held by yet another golden stand, she spotted a piece of chalk. Chuckling to herself, Eris grabs the chalk and draws a smiley face in the first blank spot she can find.

“Uhhhh… guys?” Mylo says, breaking her attention from the lines and numbers. He stood at a desk, hunched over it along with Vi and Claggor.  

Eris walked over, seeing the beginnings of a machine over their shoulders. Flat, triangular, almost like a little stage for a little blue crystal, held up and in place by a golden claw. The gem was no larger than a silver cog made sphere, but it seemed to glow in the light leaking in from the window. Her eyebrows knit together, curiosity seeping into those brown eyes, which then turned to amusement as she watched Mylo get zapped when he tried to touch it.
“Wait, Vi, how the hell did we find this place?” Claggor blurted.

“It was a tip from Little Man,” Vi huffed, something like exasperation pulling her eyes a little wider.

“Little man?” Mylo started before Vi interrupted. “Just leave it. Come on.”

“How can anyone have so much stuff?” Claggor marveled, picking up a trinket.
“Simple. Just get born lucky.” Mylo scoffs, tossing another thing into the bag.

Eris watches Powder wander off from the corner of her eye, shrugging as she returns to searching.
They pilfer through the office for a while before Eris’ ears perk up to the sound of footsteps, muffled steps, steps that weren’t theirs. The alarm fills the room; they heard it too, silence pulling all eyes to the entrance. The doorknob turns, and Mylo hurries over, jamming a chair right under it. Eris ran down the hall where Powder had disappeared behind.

“Powder! We gotta go!” Eris whisper-yells, watching as she gathers the remaining blue crystals. She grabbed the girl by the arm, dragging her back down the hall as a man’s voice rang out, “Is someone in there?”—because the shuffling from behind the door and echoes of muffled voices obviously meant he needed to ask.
Eris sees a flash of blue from the corner of her eye before they turn the corner, grabbing the bag from the floor and pushing the girl forward. Securing the bag, her heartbeats pick up in speed as Mylo and Vi urge her to hurry.

She felt her heart splintering under the weight of it all, the pressure so immense it threatened to crush her entirely. Still, she forced a smile through her tears, soft, trembling, painfully tender, a fragile promise born in the wreckage around them. Holding his face felt like clutching a lifeline in a vast, merciless sea. She leaned closer, willing her love to be enough, to be stronger than the blood, the pain, the inevitable. In that moment, caught between despair and devotion, Myra understood what it truly meant to fight for someone, even as the world fell apart around them.

"Shhh.... It's going to be okay. I love you. So much," she sobbed out.

His face tilted instinctively into her touch, his breathing ragged and uneven beneath her palms. Myra felt it, the way her hands seemed to steady him, anchoring him to the moment even as he slipped further away. She clung to that fragile connection, watching his lips part as if he were trying to speak. She leaned closer, desperate to catch his words, to hear her name on his lips one last time. But nothing came. Instead, a shallow cough tore from his chest, spraying warm blood across her face. She flinched but didn't pull away. His expression faltered, strength draining from his features as his eyes struggled to focus on her. She watched helplessly as his gaze clouded, her face blurring in his vision, his chest rising and falling in shallow, desperate gasps.

"No," she whispered, the word barely sounded at all.

She stayed there, hands still cradling his face, silently begging him to hold on, to stay with her, just a moment longer. But his eyes fluttered, then slowly fell shut. His hand slipped from its tense grip and went limp, his breathing stuttering before stopping altogether. His body sagged lifelessly against the chair. The silence that followed was suffocating, thick as fog, broken only by the uneven hitch of Myra's breath. The air was heavy with the metallic stench of blood. Her skin and clothes were soaked in it, her fingers trembling as she pressed her forehead gently to his. Her heart shattered in her chest.

She couldn't breathe properly. She couldn't speak. She couldn't think.

All she could do was stay there with his body in her lap, sobbing, as the world came to a standstill around them.


What Could Have Been

At the sound of movement, Myra looked up. Caitlyn was regaining consciousness. Vi was getting freed. But most importantly, Jinx was walking across the crosswalk holding a new weapon. It resembled a shark, its engine parts glowing blue. She moved it to rest on her shoulder, violet tears falling down her cheek.

She had a straight shot at the Council tower.

The red moon cast an eerie light over the whole table. Eris' leg bounced as the Councilors calmed down and engaged in civil conversation. After a lot of arguing, it was time to vote, all in favor. Eris glanced over at Mel as she removed her crest ring, setting it against the table with a soft clink before raising her hand, a bright spotlight shining down onto her.

"I support Councilor Talis' proposal for peace."

A terrifyingly bright blast of blue filled the cannery, temporarily blinding all of the women. Myra covered her eyes with her arm, shutting them tightly as Jinx's scream echoed in her ears. With an explosion of energy, a missile launched out of the mouth of the shark gun, a trail of blue electricity trailing behind it. Myra peeled her arm away from her eyes.

"JINX!" She shrieked, staggering to her feet.

Harpy and Ledger lay stargazing on the roof of Harpy's house, along with some younger Firelights. Harpy looked up at the star-speckled sky, the blood moon casting a haunting glow over the tree. Suddenly, a stripe of blue ripped across the sky right above them. Her eyebrows knit together, and she glanced at Ledger, who looked just as confused. One of the kids spoke up. "Look! A shooting star!"

Shoola raised her hand next, followed by Bolbok, Hoskel, Salo, and Kirraman. Eris' eyes widened. They all agreed. She looked at Viktor, who looked a mix of excited and worried. Her eyes landed on Marcus, who offered a gentle smile and a nod. She looked away from him, catching something in the window.

"What is that...?" Her eyes darted around the window before they widened.

Caitlyn let out a pained scream, resting on Vi as they watched the missile touch down on the Council building, a plume of dust and smoke erupting from where the window was. The billowing cloud of blue smoke broiled over Caitlyn and Vi, and Myra launched forward, blowing past them as she pursued Jinx.

 

A Physical Fight

Boots slammed against the pavement, and the shock sent Myra crashing to her knees. She pushed herself upright, vision swimming, concussion buzzing behind her eyes, shoulder throbbing in pain from the gunshot in her shoulder, and emotion blurring everything else. Ahead, a streak of blue whipped around the corner of a building. Gritting her teeth, she forced herself into motion again, chasing Jinx all the way down to the docks. Cornered at last, Jinx spun, only to take a punch square to the jaw that folded her to the ground instantly. Myra loomed over her, chest heaving, every fiber of her body burning as it had ignited from the inside. Blood ran down the back of her arm, dripping off her slender fingers.

"No... no, wait, My–!" Jinx barely formed the phrase before Myra was on her, knees pinning her down, one hand clamped around her throat, and the other pulled back for another strike. Myra stared into those bright pink eyes—eyes she had watched grow up, change, harden. The face of the girl she had raised as her own stared back at her.

She froze. Her grip didn't loosen.

Even as Jinx's nails dug into her wrist hard enough to draw blood,

Myra couldn't let go.

"If I EVER. See your face again," Myra snarled, clenching down on Jinx's throat and eliciting a squeak.

"I WILL kill you."

With a shuddering breath, she finally let go. Jinx rolled to her side and dragged in air. It hurt, each inhale snagging as though wrapped in barbed wire. Tears streaked down her cheeks, a dark bruise already blooming across her throat. Without a word, without even daring to look back, she scrambled upright, the metallic jingle of her gear breaking the heavy silence, and bolted into the night for the last time. Myra stayed where she was, collapsing onto her knees as the world around her dimmed to a distant hum. She stared at her trembling, blood-slick hands, unable to feel anything except the hollow ache where her fury had burned itself out. A single hot tear slipped down her cheek—not grief, not sorrow. She had exhausted those long ago. When she lifted her head, her gaze locked on the smoking silhouette of the Council Tower. The stab in her chest dulled into a cold, steady throb. Her expression hardened, resolve settling into place like armor.

This wasn't an ending.

It was the moment her grief hardened into something sharp enough to cut the world back.
