Chapter Text

The sun had barely started to rise, golden strings of light reflecting off the hardwood floor in Black Brew Coffee. The music was yet to be turned on, leaving the cafe in rare silence; the only sounds being the three sisters breathing and squeaky movements from sneakers and boots. The women had about an hour to spare before everything had to be in order–this morning, however, was proving to be already an annoying one.

“Bella, what have I told you about closing?” Narcissa Black complained with a groan, her usual mask of indifference replaced by an irritated frown. She rubbed her temples, leaning against the wooden front counter with a coffee cup in hand. A pile of crumbs had been hidden underneath the display case.

Bellatrix–the eldest, but still managing to be the least mature of the three– simply smirked and bit into a ham and swiss croissant, ignoring the crumbs falling to a freshly swept tile, much to Narcissa’s dismay. “Can’t remember,” she replied with an impolite grin, not bothering to swallow her food before replying. She shifted on her feet, shoving the last of the croissant into her mouth.

With a roll of the eyes the third sister, Andromeda, emerged from a swinging red door behind the two. Her voice was already agitated as she sent a glare in Bellatrix’s direction, crossing her arms and joining Narcissa’s side. “The entire point of closing is ideally that it’s easier for the people opening. Honestly, if I have to listen to my daughter’s voicemail about how dirty my shop is at five in the bloody morning again–”

“Psh, as if Nymphadora’s any better,” Bellatrix replied dismissively, waving her hand and brushing leftover crumbs on her apron. She turned to the coffee maker, inhaling the scent of fresh caffeine. She filled up her mug, before taking a long sip with a satisfied sigh. Only after a beat did she notice her sisters had grown still, observing her.

“Isn’t that missing something?” Narcissa asked in the way she always did, with one eyebrow raised and just the slightest curl of her lips that only her sisters could truly pick up on. Andromeda rested her hip against the counter, eyes darting between the youngest and elder sibling, appearing to hold back a chuckle.

Bellatrix, confused, looked between the two, frowning. “What are you talking about, Cissy?” she huffed, sipping her coffee and resting against the register.

“Oh, I just assumed you would have made your favourite coffee order,” she replied cooly, “What was it again? Andy, do you remember?”

Before Bellatrix could open her mouth to defend herself, Andromeda quickly answered with an infuriating grin, “Iced caramel macchiato with vanilla sweet cream foam and… oh, yes, extra caramel drizzle.”

“That is not true!” Bellatrix argued, slamming her coffee cup down, furrowing her brows and busying herself by grabbing a rag from the sanitizer bucket and beginning to clean the display case. “I only take my coffee black—”

Narcissa huffed. “Bullshit. I’ve seen your pick up orders, just because you use a different name at the shop next door—”

“Why are you looking at my phone!” Bellatrix's voice raised, raising her arms.

“Cissy stop, she’s embarrassed,” Andromeda said in a mocking voice, watching Bellatrix’s face flush an even deeper red before turning her focus to the stack of cups waiting to be restocked.

With the tap of a pen against a clipboard, Andromeda paused any further arguing and caused their heads to turn to her. “Oh, Bella, I forgot to mention something. We’re interviewing a new applicant today. Hermione Green… Hermione Gr… well, anyhow, it doesn’t matter. She’ll be around mid day, so expect it. Seemed like a nice girl over the phone so do us all a favour and don’t scare her off, yeah?”

Bellatrix gave an annoyed eye roll, huffing like a discontent child. “Why am I the one doing the interviews? Cissy always says I’m off putting, anyways.”

Ignoring the jab, Narcissa replied, “Because Andy wants to take her bloody lunch break for once. And I’m more friendly with the customers.” With a groan she pushed herself off the counter, walking to the front door and flipping the sign hanging on the glass to the open side.

“Do neither of you think I possess the ability to be a kind, enjoyable person?” Bellatrix asked with a frown.

“Hm, not particularly,” Andy responded with a smug grin, placing the clipboard down and looking under the counter for an extra box of muffins.

“Cissy, really?” Bellatrix scoffed, waiting expectedly for the younger sister to stick up for her. To her disbelief, Narcissa simply gave a shrug of the shoulders, glancing out the window momentarily before walking back to the front.

Andromeda simply sighed, clapping her hands together. “Alright, enough bickering for the day. Luna and Neville should be here in a couple minutes, anyways. Let’s get to work.”

Chapter Text

Hermione Granger had experienced lots of stress in her life. Exam weeks, where she spent countless hours in the dead silence of an overpacked library, right before getting into one of the most competitive PhD programmes in the country. Jobs were no exception, of course. She could still recall the horrors of waiting tables when she first started graduate school, the clatter of dishes and whiny complaints from customers. 

Yet through it all, she had typically managed to balance the weight of it—a barista job surely couldn’t be too much of a challenge. 

In fact, Hermione could have described her morning prior to her first shift at Black Brew Coffee as relatively calm. It had started off with a warm pot of coffee, a phone call with Ginny, cuddles from Crooks. She had even managed to avoid morning traffic, arriving to work five minutes early. 

Everything was running smoothly. 

Until it wasn’t.

“Granger!” Bellatrix barked, her face bright red. Her curls stuck to the sweat on her temples, short puffs of breath escaping her nostrils. Her apron had been properly abandoned once the rush started, unbothered by the coffee stains now sporting her white button-down. 

Any softness the older woman had shown her prior to her first shift completely disappeared the moment she put her apron on. If anything, she was harsh. Mean. Consistently impatient with any type of inefficiency from the new employee, not to mention an obvious instinct to control everything. 

Including Hermione, it seemed.

Chatter of impatient customers filled up the shop, mixed with the sounds of order numbers being called out and mispronounced names. The indie music Neville had chosen before opening was now barely audible, drowned out by the chaos. Everyone blurred together behind the counter, like a choreographed dance of sorts over the scents of pastries and freshly brewed coffee. Except Hermione was finding a way to be the one who couldn’t find her pace in the mix of it all, internally panicking rather than finding her flow. 

“Come on, have you never opened a box before? Grab one of the bloody knives and—oh for God’s sake, just move over.” 

Firm hands gripped Hermione’s shoulders, the young woman jumping at the unexpected contact. Her heart skipped a beat. Bellatrix huffed, shaking her head and murmuring under her breath as she grabbed a small knife off the counter and sawed off the tape. 

Glancing up from the box, Bellatrix furrowed her brows, “Are you just standing there looking pretty, or can you learn how to move from here to here?” she asked sharply, gesturing towards the front counters, where customers gathered in an impatient line. 

“Oh, it’s really fine!” Came a chipper voice from behind the pair, Neville giving an older man his change and throwing a look over his shoulder. “But if someone could perhaps start making the drinks I would appreciate it, since—”

“You’ve got to be shitting me,” Bellatrix let out a loud groan as she opened the box, stomping over to the ticket machine, watching order after order spill out. 

Hermione quickly opened the bag, hurrying to grind the beans, before a sharp yell stopped her in her tracks. 

“Am I going mad, or did I not just tell you to go up front? It wasn’t a suggestion, pet.”

“Right, I know, I just—”

“Go!”

The raised voice had Hermione jump just the slightest bit, before she ducked her head and went up to the register next to Neville, fumbling with the machine. Her cheeks were flushed a deep red, and she swallowed as she began to take orders, trying to block out the frustrated grumbles and curses from behind while Bellatrix made all the drinks. 

But it wasn’t the work itself that had Hermione stressed. The flow became more familiar and comfortable, Neville stepping in whenever there was confusion. No—it was her boss, who lingered behind her. Whenever Hermione’s confidence grew, and she began to feel just a smidge less disorganized, Bellatrix was quick to throw her off her rhythm. Whether it was a correction (which typically felt unnecessary), or simply just… lingering—being almost too close for what could be considered comfortable, and her eyes continually shifting to watch Hermione’s hands.

Whichever reason it was, Hermione couldn’t shake off the discomfort it caused. 

As the line slowly began to die down, Neville glanced over at Hermione, who wiped a bead of sweat off her brow and gave a short, shaky smile. 

“You know, I cried on my first day,” he finally said, closing his register and giving Hermione an attempt at a reassuring smile. “I thought I was fired for sure… anyway. It’ll get easier the more you go along.”

Hermione gave a short nod. “Yeah. I think I’m getting it… just, a lot. Busier than I expected,” she replied, fidgeting with her fingers and sighing as she rested against the counter. She glanced behind her as customers who had lingered at the tables slowly trickled out, headed off to begin their day. 

A huff had Hermione jump, Bellatrix making her presence known. “You stayed at the register the whole shift, pet. How brave,” she rolled her eyes, looking down to inspect her nails.

Hermione gave a short nod, slipping the paper into her pocket before giving a quick wave. Her shoulders relaxed a bit. One less person to feel on guard around in what was turning out to be a very confusing place. 

“Right. I’ll see you around then, Tonks. As a friend,” she added, teasing.

Andromeda’s cackle finally escaped, now sounding not much different from the sister she had just been reprimanding, and Hermione found her feet moving just a bit quicker. 

What a way to end her first shift.

A curl of smoke drifted from Ginny’s mouth as she raised a brow. “But?”

Hermione sighed. “It’s a bit stressful, I suppose,” she admitted, rolling her shoulders. Ginny shrugged in reply.

“So? I mean, it’s not like you’ve ever let stress stop you,” Ginny replied casually, shrugging one shoulder. “I mean Jesus, midterms have been…”

“Awful,” Hermione finished in agreement, tutting. “But you know, I like what I study. It feels more like a hobby than school.”

Ginny rolled her eyes, taking a sip of Coke and glancing over at a child who had promptly fallen on their face after chasing a football, failing to hold back a chuckle. “Well, lucky you,” she trailed off for a moment, before continuing, “But you’ve had loads of different stressful jobs. What could be so different about making coffee?”

There was a beat of hesitation before Hermione responded. “Well… there are three owners of the shop. They’re all sisters. And it’s a small business, so naturally they’re the managers as well. But one of them…”

Furrowing her brows, Ginny prodded, “One of them, what?”

“I don’t know. She’s just strange, I guess. One moment she can be all charming and charismatic, and the next she’s yelling about something I don’t even know about. Puts me off a bit,” Hermione admitted, staring down at the apple in her hands instead of biting into it, chewing the inside of her cheek. 

“Well… I mean, don’t let her get to you, yeah? She’s not the only strange boss you’ve had. I’m sure it’s nothing to worry about, really. Plus, with school and all, it’s probably best if you stick to a few shifts a week, anyway.”

Hermione couldn’t quite shake a lingering sense of unease. However, she let her shoulders drop a bit, giving a short nod at her friend’s reassurance. “I guess you’re right, Gin. At least today I get the day off. Do you want to get takeaway? I heard there’s a new Chinese place a couple of blocks down. We could—”

Ring! Ring! Ring!

The obnoxious buzz of her phone vibrated in Hermione’s pocket. She frowned, grabbing it and tilting her head in confusion when she realised it was Tonks calling. Her stomach dropped. She paused before accepting the call and putting it on speaker, Ginny’s head hovering curiously over the screen. “Um, hey, Tonks. What’s up?”

“Granger. Glad you finally picked up. I called three times—was your phone off?”

“Wait, Bellatrix? What are you—”

The sound of Tonks arguing in the background barely made it through the line. 

“Hermione, is that her?” Ginny hissed, failing to keep her voice at a whisper. 

Pushing aside a flicker of internal alarm at her friend’s line of questioning, she ignored her before continuing, “Why are you calling me on Tonks’s phone?”

“Listen pet, we have a line out the door, and Lovegood decided she needed to call in sick last minute. While you obviously spend a good portion of the shift fumbling about, my dear niece isn’t much better anyway.” 

A distant shout could be heard, a scuffle, before Bellatrix’s voice came through the line again. This time it was strained and hurried.

“Do it for me, pet. Really, it just—”

“Oh for the love of God, hand it over, Bella!”

Hermione couldn’t help a chuckle from escaping as she heard Bellatrix’s scoff in the background, followed by a long sigh from her niece. Rising to her feet, she ignored Ginny, who was beginning to protest, waving her arms around. Yet Hermione acted as though she wasn’t there at all.

“Please do not feel obligated whatsoever to work this shift. If you want the extra hours you’re free to do so, but don’t let my aunt of all people be the one to pressure you into it.”

Was she really going to do this? All because Bellatrix asked it of her?

Evidently, yes.

“Don’t worry about it, Tonks. I wasn’t up to much anyway. I’m on my way.”

“Oh, come on!”

Ginny’s annoyed yell was all Hermione heard before she grabbed her purse, muttered a quick apology, already on her feet and calling back over her shoulder. “Gotta go, sorry!”

Hermione didn’t give herself any time to reflect as she hopped in her car, plugging Black Brew Coffee into Maps and putting the gear into drive. 

There’s no logical reason for me to come in. I can’t be this much of a pushover, can I? Hermione thought to herself as she pulled into the car park, worrying her bottom lip. 

Bellatrix’s moods swung rapidly, leaving the younger woman constantly on edge. She couldn’t deny that her thoughts had begun to feel overtaken by the older woman, even outside of work. It was difficult to pinpoint why exactly Bellatrix stood out so much. 

Why, even after her first shifts with Bellatrix had been consumed by anxiety, she still found herself thinking about the next?

Inhale. Exhale. 

Hermione pulled into the car park, turning the car off before she grabbed her bag and made her way through the door. She was immediately greeted by a smirking Bellatrix, and Tonks rolling her eyes beside her. She swerved through the line of customers, understanding Bellatrix’s desperation. The place was packed. 

Maybe it wasn’t personal, then. They just really needed another employee. 

But… why her? Especially since all Bellatrix had done so far was criticise her. 

“See, I knew you’d come!” Bellatrix cooed while Hermione set down her bag, throwing on an apron from the rack. Hermione felt a mix of dread and a warm feeling she refused to name as Bellatrix approached. 

With a pat on the head, the older woman leaned in, and Hermione shivered when she felt hot breath against her ear. “There’s a good pet.”

Suddenly, Hermione was feeling rather flustered. 

Tonks pulled her out of it as Bellatrix swiftly moved back to the counter, grumbling at the complicated order from an elderly gentleman.

“Thanks for coming, ‘Mione. You can just help me with the coffees—you still need to be trained for that, don’t you?” Tonks asked, waving Hermione over to the counter. 

“Oh, yeah. I’ve only been upfront so far, so I haven’t had the chance to.”

Bellatrix rolled her eyes at Hermione’s reply, but didn’t say anything. 

Handing Hermione a ticket, Tonks brought her over to the espresso machine. “Well, once you get the hang of it, it’s not anything too tricky. Just watch me do it—we should probably pick up the pace though… don’t want any customers getting antsy.”

Hermione followed Tonks’s instructions with a short nod, taking a sharp inhale and grabbing a plastic cup next to her. 

“Alright, let’s do this then,” she agreed, although her hands trembled, and she swore Bellatrix’s eyes were still on her.

The moon cast light over Hermione’s hardwood floor, filtering through the half-open window. The breeze was a relief as she sank into her worn sofa, swirling the wine in her glass. Crookshanks rested at her feet, the vibrations of his purrs easing the exhaustion of the afternoon 

But this time, it had been exhausting in a different way.

It hadn’t made Hermione feel as though a heavy weight was resting on her shoulders, or like she might as well give up entirely.

Tonks was more than friendly. Silly and loud, but also patient in a way that Hermione didn’t know she needed. She had been quick to fall into the rhythm of making the orders, the tasks feeling more enjoyable rather than terrifying. 

And Bellatrix had been… 

Well, not that bad, actually. 

Hermione had been preparing herself for the reprimands. Getting corrected and ridiculed for what felt like the most minor mistakes. 

Instead, however, Bellatrix was… friendly? Welcoming? 

Warm, perhaps?

That felt like a weird way to word it, Hermione concluded.

She wasn’t as snappy, or outright rude, to the young woman. That didn’t make her behaviour any less odd though. If anything, it just left Hermione more and confused on edge. There were multiple instances where Bellatrix’s personality shifted entirely.

Brief touches when she walked by, pet names that left Hermione’s cheeks burning—and, embarrassingly enough, another type of burning altogether. 

It didn’t mean anything though, did it?

Bellatrix was an objectively attractive, intimidating woman. That much was obvious. Her eccentric, gothic fashion was easy to draw attention to. The way that, no matter what room she entered, she made sure that all eyes were on her. Her seductive, low voice. Jet black curls that fell over her shoulders. Her sharp jawline—

Hermione blinked, shaking her head, quickly putting the glass of wine down. She rolled her shoulders, trying to shake off the way her heart had begun to race against her ribs, and her face feeling much too hot to blame it on alcohol alone. 

No—she was getting too tipsy, that was all. 

Thinking too much into things. 

Hermione thought about things far too much, most of the time. Not a new trait. 

Nothing to worry about. 

With a yawn Hermione stood, stretching her arms and rising on her heels. Crookshanks let out a meow of disapproval before hopping off the couch along with her, heading to her room to inevitably claim his side of the bed. She set her glass down in the kitchen sink, deciding that the dishes would simply have to wait until tomorrow morning before following in her cat’s steps. 

She kicked off her trousers and jumper before falling into bed with a huff, rubbing her eyes. She’d almost forgotten her morning class tomorrow. And the closing shift with Narcissa.

The woman was nice, but God sometimes she was downright scary. Perhaps it was the cold, detached attitude that was so at odds with her sister that caused her to feel so unsettled.

A ding from her phone had her brows furrowing, slightly annoyed by the interruption as she had just turned the light off and was about to close her eyes. A notification from an unknown number.

She frowned, opening up the text message. 

Unknown: Thanks for coming in. Helped a lot. –Bellatrix

Hermione sat up against her headboard, her heart speeding at the text message. Bellatrix?

For a moment, Hermione considered not replying. She worried her bottom lip, her finger hovering over the keyboard before carefully typing out a response. 

Hermione: Thanks

There was a pause before Hermione decided to add another question.

Hermione: How’d you get my number?

A few minutes went by, and Hermione almost considered turning off her phone and chalking it up to Bellatrix being pressured by one of her sisters to be kinder, until a ding of a reply had her opening the app once again. 

Bellatrix: Stole Tonks’s phone.

Bellatrix: I’ve been thinking about you. 

Hermione felt heat bloom in her cheeks, spreading down her chest. Suddenly, her hands felt damp. She couldn’t mean that kind of thinking… well, it had to be in a friendly way. Maybe she was regretting her behaviour? Apologising? But that didn’t make sense, not really. 

Bellatrix: Sorry. I don’t know what I’m saying. I had a bit too much wine with Cissy. 

Hermione: No, it’s okay. I think about you too

Immediate regret hit her when she sent the message. 

Stupid. She was being stupid. Evidently, she was probably too tipsy herself. After a beat of consideration, Hermione decided to send another message. A bolder one.

Hermione: I guess I’m surprised. I thought you hated me or something 

Bellatrix: I only act like that because I like you, pet. It’s hard not to tease when you get so cute when you’re embarrassed.

At that, Hermione felt her mouth grow dry, and an ache develop in her core. 

Hermione: You should sober up and go to bed before you say something you regret

Bellatrix: Mmm. Okay, bossy.

Bellatrix: Goodnight, Granger. 

Crookshanks pawing at Hermione’s face brought her back to reality, and she pursed her lips, closing her phone and setting it on the nightstand.

She sighed as she gave the feline a few head pets as he made himself comfortable next to her, purring contentedly. 

Perhaps she could text Bellatrix tomorrow. When their heads were both clear. 

But she feared that would only make it more complicated. She couldn’t deny that she had… noticed her appearance before. Gotten flustered by her comments. Was that all meant to be flirting? 

Hermione groaned as she put her hands over her face, flipping onto her side. She should try to get some rest. Tomorrow wasn’t going to be any easier. 

But the lingering thoughts of their messages back and forth stayed in Hermione’s mind, and she found herself tossing and turning until the sun rose.

Chapter Text

The following days proved to be utterly exhausting. Midterms were approaching faster than Hermione felt comfortable with, and material that previously sparked passion in her was replaced with a pit of dread. 

Hermione groaned as she threw her backpack on top of the table in front of her, rubbing her temples. The library was overcrowded with other students, all studying for their midterms and trying to keep their sanity in the process, huddled together and hunched over their laptops and textbooks. While they all attempted to keep their volumes to a whisper, it had gradually turned into a room full of chatter.

The blank Google Doc almost mocked her as she stared at it. She blinked, and then let out another groan. Philosophy of the Mind was easily one of her favourite courses throughout her academic career. Psychology and philosophy were her passion, and she knew she wanted to teach it like her professors did since she’d taken the first introductory class. 

Typically, it wasn’t as if this midterm would be any challenge. If anything, she’d enjoy it. Free will was a fascinating topic to explore. She always found herself reading books and articles on it, even writing research papers on her own time (which, Ginny always relentlessly teased her for). 

But she just couldn’t get her brain to work. To cooperate. 

Her thoughts kept wandering back to a certain woman. Bellatrix. 

When she woke after the texts they had shared, Hermione was half convinced she’d simply dreamt of it, until she found herself rereading the conversation. She cringed when she did. She felt embarrassed—she had been too vulnerable, too honest. While part of her wanted to blame it on the alcohol, delude herself into thinking she was drunker than she thought and said something she didn’t mean, she knew that it wasn’t the truth. 

Ever since she started working at Black Brew Coffee, her thoughts had gradually been taken over by her boss. Her teasing, how her skin burned every time Bellatrix’s fingers brushed over them… she found herself yearning for the next time she’d see the woman. What was once dread and anxiety had slowly shifted to excitement. Every interaction played in her head on a loop, Hermione finding new ways to analyze them and try to make sense of it all. 

Bellatrix was just so… confusing. Her moods and attitude could change at a whim, never consistent. Criticising snarls from the older woman, that shifted to flirtatious purrs in her ear. But then, as her shifts continued, Bellatrix gradually became more teasing, cocky… always lingering close by, giving Hermione small brushes of physical contact that were so quick yet so heated it gave her whiplash. 

The thoughts of it raced in her mind, looping and only growing louder. Shifting her eyes away from the computer screen, she reached for her phone again, gnawing on her bottom lip.

Nothing. 

“Hermione?”

At the sound of her name Hermione raised her head, a smile tilting her lips the moment she saw Ginny standing over her. 

“Trying to study for midterms, huh?” Ginny asked with a nod, sitting herself across from Hermione and thumping her backpack down next to hers. “I swear, I’m going bloody mental. I don’t think I’ll be able to make it.”

Hermione sighed in agreement, running her fingers through messy curls. “Tell me about it. Dr. McGonagall is absolutely fascinating, but God this paper.” She pursed her lips. “And it certainly doesn’t help that my word count is at a strong hundred and two at the moment.”

Ginny whistled in response, shaking her head and pulling her own laptop out of her bag. “I thought you liked writing for this class?”

Hermione leaned back in her chair, staring up at the fluorescent lights above them. Blinked. Leaned back down with a grimace. “I do. That’s the problem. I should be able to focus but I’m just so…”

“Distracted?” Ginny finished, now scribbling down notes on spare pieces of paper. 

“Yeah,” she agreed, fidgeting with the rings on her fingers. There was a beat of silence as Hermione pondered. 

All Ginny knew so far about Bellatrix was that she had been Hermione’s intimidating, unforgiving, borderline sadistic boss. So, potentially not the best first impression. 

But the tension of trying to keep it all to herself, the conversation replaying in her head in never-ending cycles, was almost enough to drive Hermione mad. So, exhaling deeply, Hermione blurted out, “If I tell you something—well, something that might sound a bit… strange—you won’t judge me, right?”

Ginny raised a brow as she looked up from her laptop, shifting in her chair. “Probably not. Now spill.”

The confirmation was enough to make all of Hermione’s hesitation go completely out the window. “You know Bellatrix?”

“You mean, the boss you abandoned our day at the park for?” Ginny smirked. 

Hermione huffed. “Yes.” She paused before continuing, “Well—that night, after my shift… she texted me.”

Cocking her head, Ginny inquired, “Texted you?”

“Yes. To thank me for coming in,” she swallowed, tutting as a ring slipped off her thumb as a result of her fidgeting. “But we kept texting, and um…”

Unable to hold herself back, Ginny exclaimed, “Hermione Jean Granger—did you sext your boss?!”

Hermione’s face turned a blazing red as she clamped her hand over Ginny’s mouth, scanning around the library to check no one’s head had turned. A librarian spared a glare in their direction, her mouth opened in offense. “Christ, can you keep your voice down? I don’t exactly need the whole library to know my business!” she hissed, ducking her head.

Unbothered, Ginny licked Hermione’s hand until she yanked it away with a disgusted frown. Ginny grinned. “So, you did then?”

“No,” Hermione replied promptly, crossing her arms over her chest. Ginny huffed, almost seeming disappointed at the response. “But she… well, she was drunk. And she told me—she told me that she’s been thinking about me.”

Ginny’s jaw dropped. 

“And I…” 

For a moment, Hermione considered simply lying about what she had replied. It was so embarrassing. But she sighed, looking away from Ginny and admitted it to her.

“I told her that I think about her too. Then she said she only teases me because I’m cute when I get embarrassed, and then I said she needed to get some sleep before she says something she regrets, and then…”

“Then you went and had sex?” Ginny suggested bluntly, motioning her head. 

“No!” Hermione responded, her eyes wide. “No, no of course not. We haven’t—we haven’t talked since then. I’ve been too scared to text her anything.”

Ginny frowned, now fully abandoning her work entirely. Hermione had already done the same. “Wait—why not? Come on, ‘Mione. She quite literally told you she’s been thinking about you. She might as well have told you to take your pants off.”

Hermione had suspected her friend would’ve told her off for it, telling her that it was more than a bad idea. But Ginny had always been a bit impulsive. She snorted, still double-checking no one had become nosy enough to tune into their conversation. “I don’t know, Gin. I mean… I think it’s flirty. But what if I’m wrong? Plus, I’m pretty sure she was drunk out of her mind. There’s a very good chance she’s embarrassed about it, and probably hopes I’ll never bring it up again. Not to mention, she’s my boss.”

With a hum, Ginny tapped her chin. “I thought when people are drunk, that’s their real selves or something. Plus, do you know how many people have affairs with their bosses? It’s not like it’s a huge company. I’m sure the ethics are… fine.”

“Well, I don’t know that for sure. And no, alcohol just lowers your inhibitions and impairs judgment by primarily affecting the brain's prefrontal cortex—”

“Oh God, never mind. Sorry I even said anything,” Ginny moaned in response, rolling her eyes. “Anyway. That doesn’t change the fact that it seems pretty glaringly obvious she wants you. Plus, if you text her and she doesn’t reply, or it goes against some policy—which I highly doubt—you can just quit or something. There’s probably a hundred coffee shops in London. It’s not like you’ll be banned from the barista world.”

Hermione bit at a fingernail. “Yeah, but it’s not just about that.”

“What is it about then?” Ginny raised a brow. “Your ego?”

Shrugging one shoulder in reply, Hermione took another sip out of her coffee cup. 

Gone cold. Gross. 

“I mean, maybe? I just don’t want to be let down. And I don’t know… the more I think about her, the more I think this might be something more than just wanting to get laid.”

“Hermione Granger has a crush,” Ginny finally said with a smirk, leaning back in her chair. “That’s why you’re so obsessive over this. You like her.”

“What—no. It can’t be that,” Hermione responded quickly, her stomach doing a flip. “I can’t like her. I mean, she’s… she’s attractive, but anyone would notice that. She’s mean, she’s hot and then cold, she’s cocky, so full of herself, and—”

“And you like that,” Ginny finished confidently, satisfied to see Hermione shift uncomfortably at the realisation. 

Hermione averted eye contact, instead staring back down at her hands. She really needed to stop biting her nails… “Look, Gin. Feelings can’t happen. It’s not worth any consideration. I know her… people like her. Well, they’re probably just looking for an entertaining fuck they can forget about the next morning.”

A sigh. “You can’t know that for sure. Maybe just… I don’t know. Text her a bit and try setting the flirting aside?”

“That’s easy for you to say.” Hermione rolled her eyes. “Harry’s like, sickeningly wholesome.”

Ginny laughed, “Yeah, fair enough. All I’m saying is, maybe lean into it a little bit. It’s about time you live life on the edge.”

Hermione rubbed her temples, feeling a headache start to form. “I don’t know.”

The sound of a notebook being flipped open drew Hermione’s thoughts away from the older woman, Ginny opening her laptop screen back up for her. “This conversation isn’t over. But, we should probably attempt to get some work done. And for me to do that, I need you to focus too. Parallel play, or something.”

Hermione reached to check her phone once more, and gasped when Ginny quickly snatched it out of her hands and zipped it up in her backpack. 

“Hey! What was that for?”

“Absolutely not,” Ginny replied firmly, her brows furrowing. “You’re going to drive yourself crazy waiting for your screen to light up. You’ll get it back when your paper actually has even a concept of an outline.”

With a grumble Hermione looked back at her screen, reopening up an endless supply of tabs. “Fine. But I wouldn’t care either way.”

A snort. “Yeah, sure, ‘Mione. Whatever you say.”

The tube was packed as usual, Hermione barely managed to squeeze through the crowd of people. The air was thick and humid, and she found her nose scrunching at the foul scent. It was to be expected, however—rush hour was always a nightmare. Bodies blurred together as everyone tried to hurry to their destination, unaware and uncaring of how their limbs bumped against one another. 

Hermione had decided to be more environmentally friendly during the New Year, shifting from driving to taking the underground. If only she had known the chaos of navigating the tube station. 

Hermione let out a long sigh as the doors finally closed, her eyes scanning the array of people. 

Maybe there’s a seat, oh God please let there be a seat, I—there!

Hermione raced to sit down as an older gentleman sat up, getting off at his stop. She ignored several irritated glances at her for taking the seat. Not that she particularly cared. Her legs burned from the long walk from the library to the underground, and she dreaded the idea of having to be squashed between fellow passengers while holding onto the rail for dear life. 

The session with Ginny had been more productive than Hermione had anticipated it being—her friend always had a way of distracting her from whatever nonsense was going on in the young woman’s life, instead, bringing her back to the present. 

But despite the amount of work Hermione had managed to complete, and the upbeat presence of her friend, Bellatrix had still lingered. She craved the sensation of her phone lighting up, Bellatrix’s name flashing from the screen. 

She pulled her phone out of her pocket, her lips pulling into a frown when the screen only had one notification, an Instagram reel sent from Ginny. She exhaled lowly.

Maybe she’s waiting for me to say something. Maybe… maybe she’s nervous. 

Thoughts bounced back and forth in Hermione’s head. The lines had already been blurred. One more text couldn’t do anymore damage than there already was. 

Hermione chewed on her bottom lip as she reopened the message app once more. Her thumbs hovered over the keyboard, but before she could stop herself, she had already typed out the word and clicked send. 

Hermione: Hey

Hermione: Do you regret what you said last week?

Hermione’s leg bounced up and down as she stared at her phone, embarrassed by how her stomach churned as she waited for the response. Hey? God. 

She sounded pathetic. She self-consciously glanced up from her phone and around at the people sitting beside her, ensuring that no one was nosy enough to see what she had been doing. 

The tube came to a screeching halt, and Hermione was forced to put her phone back into her pocket as she left the station. Her pace as she walked was quick, trying to let out the nervous energy that she buzzed with. There was a sharp ringing in her eardrums that she just couldn’t shake off, and she found herself wiping her damp palms on her trousers. 

She grit her teeth, finally giving in to the urge to check her phone once again.

No new notifications.

Fuck. 

Great. She’d done exactly what she’d feared the most. Made a fool of herself. 

How was she supposed to face Bellatrix again at work? She’d jumped to conclusions, ignoring the reality of the situation—being that she’d stepped out of line. 

Wind had started to howl against her face, causing Hermione to shiver. The chatter of people passing by her was simply a background noise to her spiraling thoughts, entirely tuned out. The orange glow from dusk reflected across pale cheeks as she hurried her pace, willing herself to keep it together before she got home. 

Hermione’s flat was finally just in view, and the young woman let out a deep breath. She just needed to try and forget about the whole thing. She considered simply blocking Bellatrix’s number—pretend that she never even got a text in the first place. Pretend—

Ding!

Her phone vibrated from her pocket as Hermione walked up the stairs to her flat floor, and suddenly she felt her entire body start to tremble. 

With shaking hands she reached for her phone, quickly swiping to the messages app. Her heart dropped when she saw three notifications from Bellatrix. She swallowed, clicking on the text thread. Her eyes widened when she read the messages. 

Bellatrix: Hi, pet. I was wondering how long it would take you. 

Bellatrix: I don’t regret a thing. 

Bellatrix: You should come over. If you don’t have any regrets, that is. 

At that, Hermione’s mouth went completely dry. She stopped right in front of her front door, stuffing her keys back into her pocket. This was a bad idea. A stupid idea. She should go home, cuddle up with Crooks, drink her feelings away.

She absolutely should not be turning right back around, rapidly texting a reply lest Bellatrix change her mind. She was supposed to be thinking about her job. Bellatrix’s track record of behaviour made her a risk. Unstable, unpredictable.

But despite all of it, Hermione couldn’t help but be drawn to it. Wanting it. The alarm bells blaring in her head would simply have to be ignored for the time being. It was reckless, stupid. Was that going to stop her… no, she decided. She deserved to be reckless once in a while, didn’t she?
