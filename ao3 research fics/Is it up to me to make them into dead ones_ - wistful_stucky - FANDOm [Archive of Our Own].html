Chapter 1: My Blood in Your Bones

Chapter Text

Jayce’s trek to find Viktor had been utterly painful, if he were to say the very least. His leg still ached, and he was sure he was running on desperation at this point. The brace securing his bones and muscles in place was clunky, built from his adrenaline and the leftover knowledge brimming over from when he had constructed Viktor’s brace. There had been none of that careful consideration left in him when he made it, though. The only thing that remained ever constant was Viktor.

 

Viktor. His Viktor. (Not anymore, he reminded himself. He couldn’t allow himself that luxury). The Viktor who had pulled him from that ledge, the Viktor who believed in his dream when nobody else did. The same Viktor who remained as he always had been, hunched over his desk and sitting with him whether they were joking, or arguing, or saying nothing at all. Jayce decided then that this was the Viktor he wanted to remember most.

 

Right now, though, he had no time to focus on his memories of the man Viktor used to be, the man he had thought his friend. The small hand in his own was leading him ever closer to the Herald. Towards the man he must slaughter. The child’s hand felt freezing cold in his larger one, and pale. All of them were so, so pale. The commune seemed to hold it’s breath around him as he passed through. Jayce felt as if they were holding still, despite how they moved and walked. It seemed almost too perfect. How it was meant to be. 

 

Jayce shook the thought from his head and tried to focus on the bigger picture. All the people here had unnaturally rosy cheeks in contrast to the pale glass that was their skin. They barely acknowledged him, just stepping around him and making a clear path without even glancing up, as if it was programmed into them. They were bees in a hive, working effortlessly and perfectly executing every step they took as if they shared one singular mind. 

 

As they grew closer to the heart of the commune, Jayce smelled it before anything else. His face scrunched up and he pulled his hand to his face to cover his nose. The smell, god, the smell. It was an overbearing sort of hospital cleanliness overlaying a syrupy sweet smell like a fermenting fruit. The smell of death, permeating his sinuses and lungs. For a moment everything was just that, but he swallowed and pushed past it, letting go off the child’s hand without thinking and stepping into Viktor- The Herald’s cocoon.

 

The scent brought him back to- then. That night. When Viktor died, and was replaced with the Herald. 

 

-

 

Jayce didn’t know what happened, at first. One moment he was standing in the council room, and everything seemed like it was going good, for once. He had felt it in his chest, a rising sensation of hope that filled his lungs and flooded his bones. They were going to save Piltover and Zaun together. Viktor would live to see his dream become a reality. 

 

And then it had all come crashing down around him, literally. There had barely been any warning before the ceiling caved in, pillars crumbling around him and crashing to the ground. The world seemed to spin around him, and his eyes had to be lying, because there was no possibility where this was his reality. He had no time to think before it was all over, despite having gone in slow motion just seconds before.

 

Everything was distant, far away from his mind as his head moved itself, looking hazily around what couldn’t be the room he had just been standing in. Because the room he had been in was tall, and golden and filled with people. The room he stood in now was all reds and pitched in darkness and smoke and screams. 

 

He spoke with Mel for a moment, he knew he did. He didn’t know what he said, only what came next. Because when his mind had cleared enough to think- properly think- he remembered who had been standing directly next to him before everything went up in flame somehow both hotter and hazier than any in his forge. 

 

Viktor. His partner, his- Viktor. His Viktor. It was then the world really crashed around Jayce, the panic settling into his bone marrow and leaking him dry of anything but adrenaline and the sudden need to take back Viktor’s undoing.

 

Viktor’s body was limp in his arms and cold, cold, cold. Jayce didn’t want to, couldn’t ever, think too long about the way his partner’s back flopped against him, no longer bound by anything holding his fragile, dying, dying, dead body together. He was far too light than a man his age and height should be, and Jayce’ mind raced through his slim knowledge of the human form. Why hadn’t he taken that health and safety elective in the academy? Would everything have gone different if he had? Would Viktor still be breathing? Viktor wasn’t breathing. Viktor wasn’t breathing.

 

Jayce’s feet were hitting the floor hard as he ran, ran faster than he had in years. He could faintly feel his calves burning, but he ignored the tingling sensation and pushed through the pain with the racing ease of adrenaline sparking in his rib cage.

 

He slammed through the doors to their lab, Viktor’s frail body flopping down onto the table. (He was ever so gentle with laying down his head. He didn’t want to hurt him, never). Jayce’s heart thudded in his chest, the muscle trying to break free from his skin faster and faster. His hands shook as he flipped through papers and notebooks quickly, eyes fluttering over their messy notes and doodles. 

 

Then his eyes flickered up at the gentle spark of light infiltrating his vision, and they landed on- it. The hexcore. Flashes of tired conversations and trials and failures flooded his mind, and before he could think he was leaning over the table to grab it. He didn’t think to use his tongs, or a cloth, or anything that one would normally use in such a situation.

 

Jayce’s palm split open, skin cracking over the palm and spitting blood over the core. Flayed skin is torn away from his hand in thin sheets, clinging to the magic and sparking bright blue in excitement.

 

He cried out and dropped it, the hexcore dropping to the tile floor with a resounding clack and splattering dark red and bits of skin onto the tooth white. Jayce’s first reaction was to cling to his hand, but when he swiftly remembered Viktor, laying cold and completely helpless on the table beside him, he dropped to his knees and grabbed it again. 

 

Ignoring the sharp sting that spread up his arm, he tried to stand again. His shoes slid on the floor, making him fall back onto his knees and bloodying his pants. He ignored it, ignored all of it. Focusing all his thoughts onto his partner, he flung the hexcore onto the table and watched red and blue sparks fly.

 

After that everything was all hazy.
