Reaching out

One_kind_of_a_Witch

Summary:

What fools they had been. You don't think about it that way when you are young and bright and think you can tear down the world. Stars of their generation, the best Hogwarts had to offer, the Marauders- a bunch of fools. Now he is the last one and there is no-one left to care.

Work Text:

They were so foolish back then. His skin is sweaty, his feet feel cold. The flat, if you can call it that seems to be spinning, but Remus Lupin has been sick often enough to realize that it is just that. Sickness. Probably a fever, he summarizes from the cold sweat and the burning eyes. They were so foolish. Young and bright and thinking that the world was laying at their feet. Foolish enough and punk enough to think that they could tear down an entire system fueled by centuries of prejudice.

He remembers Sirius, shining as if he were the star he was named after, eyes stormy grey and grin crooked, always a little to wide. And he remembers Mary MacDonald sitting in the library with him, brown skin illuminated by a stray light of sunshine, eyes painted a reddish brown. Her curls had splayed around her head like a hallow. “Why go on?” she had asked, staring through the huge glass window. “Why hope Remus? This has been going on for seven years.” He had answers for her back then but it had not been what she needed, nor what she wanted to hear. He doesn’t remember now what he said. But he remembers her face, her cracking voice as she turned to him. “I just want to keep all of you safe.”

They are the only ones left now.

His muscles ache. It is a pain much duller than that of ripping skin and breaking bones and it’s almost familiar. His back has been hurting since he was thirteen, his fingers stinging. Maybe he has been dreaming, he muses as the fever wracks for his body. But when he opens his eyes, the ceiling above him is stained and his blanket is far from the fluffy duvets used in the Hogwarts hospital wing. The pain of loss is much sharper than anything the fever could produce.

He closes his eyes again. They were fools.

He can hear them, when he concentrates. His hands shake. They call him “Moony” and he is back to thinking that they were the best thing that has ever happened to him. Sirius rises from his mind, mischief in his eyes as he stands on the table in the common room and swinging a speech about quidditch and Remus thinking “Merlin, I adore him.” They never said “I love you,” to each other, having grown up in a house were “love” meant indifference and another one in which “love” meant cruelty. Instead, Remus adored his Sirius and he adored him in return. Because adoring was such a gentle word, so soft and all-encompassing and it was enough.

What fools they were.

Remus is so tiered.

James is there also and in his eyes the cosmos finds its home. Hidden behind these round glasses is a world of joy and a heart big enough to take them all. Out of everyone James was always the most loving one. He threw his heart into the world, no matter how fragile it was and he trusted people with it like no one else Remus ever met. He was free with his hugs and gentle with his loving words. He told them that he loved them. A life filled with “I love y’all”s and “I love you mate”s. One could scarcely believe in it. All that gentleness behind a force feared on the quidditch fields. Ultimately, Remus reflected, consciousness drifting in and out, James Potter had been a storm. And a better friend than he had deserved.

He was dead now and his body had been given the worms to eat, flesh and bone rotting in soft dark earth.

He hoped, oh Merlin he hoped, that he was embracing Lily in death, beautiful, bright Lily.

He had loved Lily, Remus that is. She was one of the first people in life who had trusted him. Even when he couldn’t do so himself and he had told her every single of his secrets, laid them bare at her feet. Because for all her temper and all her hurt she never judged him. Not even once.
He had to throw up, when he thought about Harry, about that tiny bundle of a child with eyes as green and bright as hers. She had loved him so much. They had been celebrating together, the both of them, when she had found out. And he did not have time then. Always of with the pack, trying to find answers. Later, he always thought, he could be the Uncle Moony that Harry deserved. And then she had died and his best mate along with her and Harry had been taken away from him, because he could not be trusted.

And he knew that. Deep down he had always known. But Lily had been good at maintaining the illusion and even only for a while he had wanted to give in. To feel loved. But now they were dead, gone so young. She would be forever red headed and bright-eyed Lily Potter, when she should have been white haired and dull eyed one day, long after he himself had left.

He had always imagined himself to be the first of them to go.

Never them. They had felt immortal. Fools they were.

His body is wrecked by shivers and he throws up into the bucket again. It burns. His tongue hurts. His head seems to burst. He wants to die.

Peter is dead. Shy and quiet Peter Pettigrew. His hair as sandy ashen as it was when they first had met, his pockets full of sweets and his eyes filled with nervous apprehension. He had related to these tense shoulders. The anxious fidgeting. He had loved him as he had loved the others. And where James and Sirius had been alive like lightning houses, like thunder storms and music festivals and parties in the common room, alive like filled quidditch pits and nights stayed alive and the biggest pranks pulled and food fights in the great hall, Peter had been more hesitant, but never less. His alive was a whispered word, and night time conversations and heads bent over a shared problem and shared sweets. He was sweet, Peter Pettigrew and Remus liked sweet people.

„I am afraid,” he had told Remus. “I am scared.” They all were scared, he thinks. They would have been stupid not do. Because they had seen people die and disappear and they knew, that people were out to hunt them. Their eyes were duller then already. They had all been scared, but Peter, quiet Peter, had been the first to speak it out loud. He cries now, eyes aching and throat dry. He was cold.

He was alone.

There was no one to care anymore.

Remus wanted to scream. The had been scared and yet they had still believed that they could get through this somehow. Sitting in that tiny kitchen, polaroid plastered around the walls and hot chocolates in chipped mugs Lily got from some muggle second hand shops. What had become of these mugs?

It hurts. It hurts so much. He needs a pain potion- something. Something to chase the fever away. “Sirius?” There is no answer. “Sirius!” He should be home now, there was no-

The pain is sudden and sharp.

There was no Sirius. Sirius Black was in Askaban, because he had betrayed their best friend, had killed James and Lily, had killed with his own hands, Peter Pettigrew.

Had left Remus Lupin behind. He hated himself, because that hurt worse than anything.

He curled into himself with a cry as if trying and failing to block out the pain. Sirius. Wonderful and sweet and adoring Sirius. So smart, so bright, so young. So alive that it hurt sometimes to look at him, with his ling hair and leather jacket and the black boots he so lazily threw over a chair. As if nothing could bother him.

He had adored him. And with an angry shout, he realized he still did. He adored Sirius Black and yet he never really knew him at all.

Remus Lupin was alone. Once he had thought, they could change the world if they just screamed loud enough, laughed long enough, danced into the night to David-Bowie songs on the recorder Sirius’ uncle had sent him. Once he had thought he could pretend for a little while longer.

But dreams shatter, end like lost loves. And the words of the young are spoken into the wind. Wild and passionate and blown away. Remus closed his eyes and cried.
