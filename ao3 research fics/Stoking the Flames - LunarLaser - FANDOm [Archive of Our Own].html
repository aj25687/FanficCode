Stoking the Flames

LunarLaser

Summary:

In this modern AU, Azula is living with her Uncle Iroh and brother Zuko after being disowned by her father after an accident causes her to be paralyzed from the neck down. It's been six months and Iroh suggests a tutor to help Azula catch up on her schoolwork. She reluctantly agrees only for the tutor be Katara, her rival and a victim of her past bullying.

Chapter 1: Spark of Ember

Chapter Text

“My dear niece, I beg you to reconsider,” Iroh pleads, his niece, Azula, glaring at him while his nephew, Zuko, sits wrongly in a nearby chair. “I’m telling you this is in your best interest.”

“And I’m telling you, dear Uncle,” Azula replies coldly. “That I am in no need of a tutor. My body might be useless now, but I assure you my mind is as sharp as ever. If I am just given the supplementary reading material, I will not only be caught up, but I will be light years ahead of the hollow headed commoners in my class.”

“I’d have to agree with her on this one,” Zuko says. Iroh turns his head to give Zuko a slightly chastising look. “I-I mean, the part about the being ahead part, not the… useless body part.”

Azula rolls her eyes.

“If we’re done here,” she says and puts her mouth on the straw connected to her motorized wheelchair. She blows a light amount of air into it, turning it left to go to her room.

“Please, wait!” Iroh calls and Azula reluctantly stops, turning a bored gaze back to her uncle.

“You get one more,” she says. “If you fail, you’re not allowed to talk to me for a week.”

“Can I get in on that, too?” Zuko chimes in.

“Listen,” Iroh says with a sigh, ignoring Zuko’s comment. “I know that you’d excel just studying by yourself, but you can not ignore reality forever. You will have to face your classmates again soon. We just want you to be accustomed to talking to people again.”

“Not like any of them would care to talk to me again anyway,” she mumbles. Her eyes drift past her uncle and land on Zuko. He’s giving her a pleading look to go with what Uncle Iroh wants. Even though every cell in her being wants to deny him, she supposes that she owes him this much.

“Alright, since you’re being so pushy, I’ll heed your wish. As long as you don’t get someone dim-witted, I’ll allow myself to be tutored by whoever you see fit.”

“We will search tirelessly for someone who can meet your standards,” Iroh says with a gentle smile that irks Azula.

“May I take my leave now?” she asks. “I have better things to be doing.”

“Of course! I’ll call you when it’s time for tea. We’re going to be having jasmine tonight.”

“Oh, joy,” Azula thinks to herself. She steers herself back into her room, leaving her family to do as they please.

Azula enters her room, immediately pushing the door closed and heading towards her computer.

“Computer,” she says. It gives a little ding to indicate it’s listening. “Search for ‘spinal damage procedure.’” The computer dings again and Azula looks at the search results flooding her screen.

“Innovative new technology for spinal injuries”

“The cure to broken spines doctors don’t want you to know about”

“Experimental new procedure in Thailand shows promise”

After quickly reading through her third article, she hears a knock on the door, followed by the sound of it opening.

“Azula?” Zuko says as he comes in. Azula quickly closes the tabs on her computer and turns to look at Zuko with what can only be described with a death inducing stare.

“Did Father pour boiling water on your brain, too?” she asks. “Don’t just barge in here whenever you feel like it. I didn’t even have the opportunity to respond!” Zuko puts his hands up defensively.

“I’m sorry! I didn’t think it would be a big deal.”

“Well, it is. Do you think just because I’m disabled, I’m not entitled to my privacy? I would have thought better of you, brother.”

“You know that’s not what I meant. Really, I’m sorry.”

Azula sighs dismissively.

“Whatever, but next time you come in without my express permission, I’ll make your face symmetrical.”

“Point taken,” Zuko says with a gulp. He knows she means it, too. To him, the wheelchair doesn’t make her any less ruthless or capable.

“What do you want, anyway?”

There’s a beat of silence.

“Come on, spit it out,” Azula pushes.

“What were you looking at before I came in?”

“What’s it to you? It was nothing incriminating.”

“If that were true, you wouldn’t have gotten rid of it before I came in.”

The two stare at each other like a couple cats about to fight with each other. Zuko leans against the door frame.

“You shouldn’t be chasing a cure, Azula. It’s only going to lead to heartache.”

“What would you know?”

“You think I wasn’t researching cosmetic surgery for weeks after what happened to me? That if I looked normal again, there would be a chance Father would take me back? You have to realize that he’s dropped you, Azula. You’re dead to him.”

“You don’t know anything!” Azula explodes suddenly. “You were always a failure. You were ousted because of who you are! I was his perfect daughter! I just came across… bad luck.”

Zuko tries to brush off her abusive words. He figured she would be like this, but it doesn’t make it easier.

“I’m just trying to help-”

“I don’t need your pity. You hate me. I hate you. Don’t treat me like a charity case just because you think you should. It’s beneath me.”

Zuko exhales deeply. It’s clear to him that he won’t get anywhere with her right now. He turns around.

“You’re wrong,” he says. “I don’t hate you, but I don’t really like you either. I still think you’re evil to your core, but… I do pity you, Azula, but not because of the chair. Because you’re father’s child.”

Azula says nothing in response.

“I’m not as good as Uncle, so sorry if I upset you. I’ll go now.”

Zuko leaves, closing the door behind him.

“Damn him,” she thinks to herself. “I let him get the last word.”

She directs herself to her painting station in the corner of her room. She’s been experimenting with trying to paint for a couple weeks. Having to use her mouth to hold the paint brush has been pretty tough and it’s been one of her more difficult tasks as she was never much of a painter before her accident, so she took this opportunity to show herself and others that she’s perfectly capable of excelling in anything she tries.

However, her attempts have been less than stellar. She’s managed to create the outline of some landscapes like mountains or the desert, but finer details were still lost on her. Admittedly, it has become quite relaxing for her to put all of her energy and thought into a single task. It has helped take her mind on the other things she has been rather obsessive over since living with her uncle and brother.

When Azula finishes, she looks upon the scene she’s created. She managed to create swirling storm clouds with a bright blue lightning bolt striking a blazing orange fire. Of course, the colors were pretty flat and if you were to look at the painting out of context, it would look as if a child drew it, but it was still progress for Azula as it’s been tough getting those zigzag lines right. Azula sighs as she puts the brush down.

“Azula!” Iroh calls. “Tea’s ready!”

As much as Azula hated to admit it, if there was one thing Iroh knew about, it was tea.

Zuko puts the teacup to Azula’s lips, letting her take a sip of the jasmine tea. He makes sure not to pour too much and to know when to take the cup away. He rarely helped Azula with any of her daily tasks which was fine with both of them, but Iroh insisted he be the one who helps her eat and drink when they are having tea. Azula didn’t particularly care for it, but she saw it as the perfect opportunity to tease Zuko.

“You know, ZuZu,” she says with a devilish grin. “This would be the perfect opportunity to give us matching scars.”

“You know I’d never do that,” Zuko replies instantly.

“I know. That’s what makes it a more fun game. If you ever decide to cross into the dark side, I win. If you don’t, I still win.”

“Always thinking three steps ahead, Azula,” Iroh says. “It’s one of your best qualities.”

“Please don’t encourage her,” Zuko says, causing a genuine chuckle from Azula.

“Looks like I’m already becoming the favorite again,” she says and Zuko simply rolls his eyes in response. “Now, is there any news about this tutor?”

“Yes,” Iroh says. “We’ve managed to find one through some complicated website.”

“It wasn’t that complicated,” Zuko says. “Your computer is just older than the internet.”

“Either way, with Zuko’s help, we’ve managed to find a most excellent tutor. She’s at the top of her class and has shown an excellent success rate with her tutoring. Her name’s-”

“Her name isn’t important,” Azula interrupts. “More than likely, she’ll be quitting the same day she starts.”

“Now, Azula-”

“Yes, yes. Do unto others and so forth. It was only a joke, Uncle. Now, I’ve had more than enough of your exquisite tea, I think it’s time I get to bed. I assume the twins are on their way.”

“Yes, Li and Lo should be here at their appointed times. I’ll make sure they knock before entering.”

Azula nods before making her exit. Lo and Li were one of the last ‘gifts’ her father had given her before disowning her. Iroh managed to stir up enough fuss for Ozai to pay for all of Azula’s medical needs such as her chair, computer, and part-time caretakers. Really it was pennies for a man as rich and powerful as Ozai who owns Pyro Industries while also being a powerful political figure. To him, it was probably worth more to not have his family members not cause a stink in the media, sullying his reputation. Azula has a small moment of gratitude towards Iroh as they both know they wouldn’t be able to afford everything she needs with only his tea shop. They can barely pay their rent, though he says it’s been easier since Zuko picked up a part time job.

Azula curses herself for having to think about her financial situation in the slightest. With a mental sigh, she waits for Li and Lo to show up to get her ready for bed before it all repeats the next day.

A couple days pass and the day of Azula’s tutoring is here. She awaits in her room, refusing to meet whatever poor fool they hired at the door. She just hopes that her abusive tongue is enough to get them to quit. While thinking up snarky comments to make at their expense, there’s a knock on the door.

“Azula,” Iroh says. “Your tutor is here.”

“Come in, Uncle,” Azula replies with a devilish smile.

“Right this way,” he says and opens the door. Azula’s smile quickly fades as her eyes land on her tutor. Familiar eyes that remind Azula of a serene lake land on hers.

“Katara,” Azula says without thinking.

“Azula,” Katara replies in an unreadable tone. Iroh’s eyes shift, looking back and forth between the two girls.

“Oh, you two know each other?” he asks.

“You could say that,” Katara answers, her gaze not leaving Azula’s.

“Well, in that case, I won’t hang around like a bad smell. Have a good session, girls.”

Iroh closes the door, leaving the two in an awkward, tense silence. Azula feels like she could choke on the tension, it’s so thick and Katara feels a bead of sweat forming on her forehead. They were like two cats, waiting on the other to make a move before pouncing. Azula wonders if they’ll spend the entire two hours like this, but Katara speaks.

“So,” she says softly. “This is what happened to you. There were rumors, but… I never would have thought any of them were true.” Azula clenches her teeth, trying her best to complete her composure.

“Well, go ahead then,” she says callously. “Take it in, laugh it up, and promptly leave.”

“No, no!” Katara puts her hands up in a defensive manner. “I wouldn’t do any of that. I mean, you were awful, probably still awful, but I’m not cruel enough to take enjoyment out of this.”

“Oh, Katara, goody-goody as always. I can’t say I’d be the same if our positions were reversed.”

Katara sighs and walks over to a nearby desk, sitting in a chair prepared for her by Iroh beforehand.

“Azula, I really don’t want to fight. I don’t like you, you don’t like me, but I still want to do my job here. Honestly, if there’s a silver lining to this, it’s that I know that you’re a fast learner.”

A light blush appears on Azula’s face from the compliment, but she wills the rush of blood out of her face as soon as it comes. She takes a deep inhale.

“Very well then,” Azula says. “Who am I to stop one’s masochistic tendencies?”

Katara rolls her eyes and takes a couple textbooks out of her backpack along with a notebook.

“This is for our history and math classes. I thought they would be a good place to start.”

“Mhm,” Azula replies lazily and Katara tries to brush off her cavalier attitude. They start off with math, problems and formulas that they have been doing in class the past few months, but it was all pretty elementary for Azula. Instead, she couldn’t help but let her mind wander to the last time she saw Katara. Azula’s tormenting was never limited to Zuko, it was relinquished onto anyone she had a problem with, and the biggest problem she had was with Katara. Despite her standing as a normal civilian born with far less than Azula, she seemed to have somehow gotten the idea that she could accomplish what Azula could.

Azula was obviously top of her class and the best at any after school activity she participated in, however, she had a great affinity as a swimmer and had been the school record multiple times. It was all well and good until Katara joined. She had always been right behind her in academics and now she was trying to steal her crown in swimming. Azula only barely managed to beat her. This rivalry caused her to set her sights on Katara for a long time, until that day.

“And that’s how you use the quadratic formula,” Katara finishes. Her eyes fall as she suddenly grows silent. Azula raises an eyebrow and suddenly Katara looks at her softly, tucking some loose strands of hair behind her ear.

“Mai and Ty Lee really miss you-”

“Don’t you dare say those cursed names in my presence!” Azula shouts. Katara’s eyes go wide.

“I’m sorry,” she replies. “It’s just they said they haven’t been able to get in contact with you.”

“They couldn’t make it up to me in a thousand lifetimes. Why would I want to talk to them? It seems they took no time buddying up with you.”

“That’s not it, either!” Katara says, a little more frustrated. “They’ve been distraught ever since the accident.”

“Well, I’m sorry my paralysis has been so hard on them.”

Katara opens her mouth to speak, her face full of anger she was ready to unleash, but she stops short, calming herself and shaking her head.

“You’re not worth it,” she says.

“Oh, so now I’m not worth it? I seemed to be pretty worth it before, always jumping down my throat every chance you got.”

“Because you were bullying people, Azula. I’m not the type of person to just let something like that slide.”

“How noble,” Azula says, bored. There’s a sudden knock at the door, causing them both to jolt.

“Uncle asked me to make you two some tea,” Zuko says from the other side. “May I come in.”

“Please,” Azula says. Zuko pushes the door open, carrying a tray with a teapot and two cups. He sets the tray on the desk next to Katara and begins pouring.

“It’s mint tea,” he says. “Supposed to help you relax.” After she pours two cups, he takes the cup over to Azula, kneeling down beside her to help her take a sip. Katara takes a sip and an unexpected hum of enjoyment escapes her throat.

“This is really good tea, Zuko,” she says. Before he can even say thanks, Azula interrupts.

“It’s slop compared to what our Uncle can make. But I suppose it’s passable.” Instead of being offended, Zuko offers up a smile.

“I think that’s the nicest thing she’s ever said to me,” he responds and Azula rolls her eyes.

“Good to know I’m not getting special treatment, then,” Katara says with a small laugh.

“Don’t take anything she says personally. She’s just like that.”

“Yes, don’t take it so personally, Katara,” Azula smirks and Katara narrows her eyes at her.

Katara glares at her, causing Zuko to raise his eyebrows in confusion. He feels like there’s some other subtext that he’s missing. His eyes wander to Katara’s necklace.

“That’s a beautiful necklace, Katara,” he says. Katara automatically brings her fingertips to the hand crafted jewel on her blue choker.

“Thank you,” she says shyly. “It was my mother’s. It was the last thing she gave me before she died.”

“O-Oh,” Zuko replies a little flustered. He felt so awkward, he found himself quickly asking “How did she die?” Azula quickly shot a glare at Zuko. Even she was offended by his lack of decency. However, Katara answers.

“She worked for a factory owned by Pyro Industries. She worked all day, the working conditions were awful, so one day she collapsed from a heart attack and… she didn’t make it to the hospital.” Silence followed Katara’s story, causing Zuko to swallow from his nerves.

“I’m sorry, Katara. Our father owns Pyro industries, so-”

“I hope you don’t plan on taking responsibility, Zuko,” Azula interrupts. “Neither one of us are heirs to Pyro Industries anymore. So it’s not like we can change anything, much less make an official apology.”

“You can’t be serious right now.”

“Hey, hey,” Katara says, putting her hands up to stop them. “It’s fine. I hate Pyro industries for a lot of reasons and it’s not like I’m not the only one, but I’m not going to put it on the two of you. Especially while I’m tutoring, that would kind of be unprofessional.”

“As if bringing up your dead mother isn’t,” Azula mumbles to herself. Zuko sighs and gets up, collecting the tea.

“You two have another hour, so I won’t keep you any longer. Azula, play nice.”

“Yes, Azula, play nice,” Katara says with a smirk. Azula narrows her eyes in response. Zuko exits, praying the room won’t be engulfed in flames by the time they’re done.

Now, the two are alone again. This time, Katara is trying to offer a kind smile in response to Azula’s glare. Katara takes out her history book.

“Maybe we should try history next,” Katara says.

“Ah, yes,” Azula thinks to herself. “History, the thing that’s put me in this situation.”

Azula briefly thinks back to that night. The pool party.

They were having it at Mai’s place, of course. She was rich and her parents were out of town, meaning they could throw a party without anyone knowing. They’re large backyard pool was perfect for it. It’s not like it was a blowout or anything, it was mostly people from the swim team and some boys they thought were cute. However, one person Azula didn’t expect to see there was Katara.

“What is she doing here?” Azula hisses at Ty Lee. She blinks at her, confused.

“You said to invite everyone,” she replies.

“Except her, obviously. A monkey could have figured that out.”

“Don’t be so harsh on her,” Mai says in her signature dull tone. “I’m sure she won’t cause any trouble. She looks uncomfortable as is.”

“Still too comfortable for me,” Azula says. An evil smile creeps across her face. “Mai, do you have any butter in your house?”

“Um, probably,” she replies. “Why.”

“Go get it. I’ll explain when you get back.”

“Okay…” Mai says, a little confused. She does as Azula commands and goes inside. Ty Lee cocks her head.

“What do we need butter for?”

“Just wait.”

A few moments later, Mai comes back, awkwardly holding an unwrapped stick of butter.

“Perfect,” Azula says, taking the stick of butter and handing it to Ty Lee. “Here, go to the top of the diving board and smear this on the top.

“Huh?” Ty Lee says in shock. “What for?”

“Simple, you butter up the diving board, do a fun little cannonball or something, then I challenge Katara to do a dive from top and the butter makes her slip, causing her to make a huge fool of herself.”

“No way!” Ty Lee shakes her head. “That’s way too dangerous! Someone can get really hurt.”

“Ugh, coward,” Azula says. “Mai, you do it.”

“I agree with Ty Lee,” Mai responds. “If Katara slips and hurts herself, it would be really bad. Especially since it’s my party.”

“How dare both of you disobey me,” Azula says, putting the stick of butter in her swimsuit, tucking it out of sight. “If you want something done right, do it yourself, I guess.” Azula begins walking away.

“Azula!” Ty Lee calls.

“This is a bad idea,” Mai says in as much of a high energy tone as she can muster.

Azula climbs up the diving board. It’s about as high as a regulation diving board, mostly so they can practice. She makes it to the top and as quickly as she can, she gets on her knees and takes out the butter and begins rubbing it up and down the board. She is going to be quick, stand up, and carefully take a dive into the pool, hiding the butter and chucking it into a nearby bush as soon as possible. That was the plan anyway.

Then, Azula looks over the board and sees Mai and Ty Lee talking to Katara. Then, Katara looks up at Azula, glaring at her intensely. Rage fills Azula and she quickly stands up.

“Traitors!” she yells, causing everyone at the pool to turn their eyes to her. “You’ll rue the day you-” Azula is cut off as she takes a step forward, already forgetting how precarious her position was. Her foot slides and in a panic, she over corrects and slips off the side of the diving board. She hears harmony of screams as she begins her fall.

Everything seems to become slow motion as she begins to fall. The wind slipping past her body feels like it’s cutting her, the diving board becoming smaller and smaller. At this moment, she’s sure that she’s going to die. She only has time to have a single thought before hitting the ground.

“There is no one to blame but yourself, Azula.”

“You got all that?” Katara asks, snapping Azula out of her memory.

“Yes,” she answers mindlessly.

“Are you okay?” Katara asks. “You seem to be spacing out.”

“Don’t act like you care,” Azula retorts immediately.

“It’s not that I don’t care, I just…” Katara drifts off.

“What? You think you need to feel bad for me now that I’m in a wheelchair? Trust me, I’d rather die than you do that?”

“It’s not pity, it’s basic decency,” Katara says. “Something you clearly have always lacked.”

“I lack nothing, I just don’t need it.”

“You really are… something. Listen, I really don’t want us to be at each other’s throat. I know I said I don’t like you but honestly, I don’t know how to feel about you. I don’t want to hate you.”

“May I ask why you’re so inclined to feel that way. Putting aside everything else, if that night had gone differently, there’s a good chance that you’d be the one in this chair.”

Katara looks away, there’s a pained look on her face.

“Because, I’m no better than you.”

“What?” Azula says, completely caught by surprise for the first, well, second time in her life.

“When I saw you fall, when I saw you hit the ground. A feeling of dark satisfaction entered my body. It only lasted for a few seconds, but afterward, I felt awful. I thought that you got what you deserved, but… Maybe part of me hopes that if there’s a twisted part of me that exists, there’s something good inside of you.”

Azula finds it hard to speak for a moment. For the first time, she’s seeing Katara in a different light. She can tell how genuine she’s being by the look on her face and she doesn’t have the desire to berate her. She sighs and gives a small smile, different from her more malicious smiles.

“Well, it is nice to hear you're not the goody-goody you portrayed yourself to be. Though I hate to crush your expectations, my soul is as dark as they come.”

“Oh…” Katara says in a disappointed tone.

“But you’re welcome to keep looking for it during our next tutoring session.”

“You want me to come back?”

“Want is a strong word. More like I need to keep my Uncle off my back. He won’t stand for it if I stop after a session. Besides, you’re more of a challenge to tease than Zuko. Really, the only thing I ever respected you for was your guts.”

“Uh, thanks, I guess.”

“Don’t be too happy,” Azula says. “Respect is nothing close to liking.”

“That’s true,” Katara says. “But I feel like getting your respect is harder than getting you to like me, so I’ll take it.”

“Why you…” Azula starts before stopping short. There’s a knock at the door once again.

“Katara,” Iroh says. “Your brother is here for you.”

“Oh, I guess it is that time,” Katara says, looking at her phone. “Be out in a second!” She looks at Azula. “Well, it’s been a pleasure working with you.”

“I won’t disagree,” Azula says. “Oh, and do not tell anyone else about this. Or else.”

“Of course,” Katara says with a smile. “Confidentiality is important in tutoring after all.”

“I’ll take your word for it.”

“Oh,” Katara says and scribbles something down in her notebook before tearing it out. She heads to Azula.

“This is my number if you need- Ah!” Katara suddenly trips on her bag that had tipped over. She does her best to get herself back up, but stumbles and ends up falling on her back across Azula’s lap.

“Ow!” Katara yelps as she lands on Azula’s armrests. As soon as her vision focuses, she’s looking up at Azula. It could be that she’s hallucinating from the shock, but for a split second, she thought she saw the faintest hint of worry on her face. However, any trace of it was quickly replaced by annoyance.

“And there goes any respect I had for you,” Azula teases, a playful smile on her face. Just the sight of it makes her heart skip a beat.

“You have a nice smile,” she says without thinking.

“Huh?” Azula lets out, her facing turning pink. Realizing what she had said, Katara stutters.

“Sorry! Sorry!” she says as she stands up. “I’ll just place it here.” She places the slip of paper on Azula’s desk. “I, um, I’ll go now.” Katara grabs her backpack and hurries towards the door.

“I’ll see you out,” Azula says in an attempt to regain her composure. “I don't want Uncle to think there’s a problem.”

“Yeah, yeah, sure.”

The two of them exit Azula’s room together. Azula is confused to see Zuko talking to not only a boy around Zuko’s age that seems to be Katara’s brother, but also two others that look to be freshmen. A young boy with a shaved head and a girl with gray, vacant eyes as well as a cane.

“Oh, you’re done,” the older boy says.

“What are all these miscreants doing in our home?” Azula asks Zuko.

“Wow, she’s just as harsh as you described,” the older boy responds.

“Uh, Azula,” Katara says. “This is my brother, Sokka. And these are our friends, Aang and Toph.”

“Actually,” Sokka says. “I’m friends with Zuko, too. We met at the same gym a while back. Had no idea he washer brother, though.”

“Ah, so my reputation precedes me,” Azula says.

“Yeah, you can say that.”

There’s an awkward silence as Sokka glares at Azula who is unphased.

“Wow, even I can see the tension,” the younger girl says.

“Oh, uh, yeah. I met Toph and Aang through tutoring as well. Toph is blind, by the way if the joke didn’t land with you.”

“Trust me, I’m laughing on the inside.”

“I like her,” Toph says.

“I have a feeling you shouldn’t,” Aang whispers.

“We were going to hang out after we picked up Katara,” Sokka says. “You wanna come with, Zuko?”
