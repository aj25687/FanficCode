Work Text:

Jayce Talis was a flame that would never burn out.

This was a lie, but one that Piltover’s Council was content to believe when he sat among them, preaching about his visions for a brighter future and trying to hide how he was nervously sweating in his too-tight jacket as he stared out into the glare of Piltover’s leading body. He thought in the back of his mind about Mel Medarda sitting across from him, and also about how nice it would be to go home alone and lock all the doors once this godforsaken meeting came to a close. Focus, Jayce. Mentally slapping himself back to the present, he refocused on the council’s discussion and continued the grating task of trying to decipher just enough through the anxious noise in his own head to convince them (and maybe himself as well) that he was paying attention. He sat still, and felt Mel’s eyes flicker to him periodically. He felt everyone glancing at him as if they didn’t know what to make of their new colleague yet; as if they were sizing up an ally or enemy.

It’s not that Jayce didn’t appreciate the opportunity. He was beyond grateful to finally be placed in a position of power- one can only handle lying in wait for as long as it feels temporary, and his new position was confirmation that he may still have the potential that Heimerdinger saw in him- the potential that Jayce himself tried to avoid contemplating the state of, for fear that he might not find it any longer. For years, he had been slaving away at his own inventions- for every breakthrough receiving another obstacle or setback that kept him treading water. Then, through a still unclear series of events, Jayce was blown from his old life and launched through the years into a position on the Piltover Council, of all things- with a lab, and a brilliant partner (who would eventually become a capital “P” Partner), and possibilities. And apparently really tight formal shoes, and- what had councillor Salo just said? Jayce nodded agreeably when he saw the rest of the council waiting for a response- was it very hot in the meeting hall? It felt very hot.

Jayce loosened his collar again. The council hall was a magnificent room- white walls reflected the burning of countless thoughtfully placed light fixtures, and shadows from the sunset through the window played over the gleaming wooden floor, casting everything else in a soft glow. It occurred to him that Viktor would appreciate the contrast, if he was stuck here with Jayce. Through the window, the sky was painted in waves of subtle oranges and reds- the comforting smoothness of hues melting into each other a stark contrast to the chilly, white marble of the councilroom, where Jayce pretended to be listening and pretended not to notice Mel Medarda’s heavy-lidded glances. In the back of his mind, Jayce wondered if Viktor was watching the sunset too.

-------

After the meeting was dismissed, Jayce hurriedly packed away his papers and walked back to the lab alone. Striding through the halls of the counsel building, he let his fingers drag along the polished banisters and marble surfaces that marked Piltover’s achievement: or at least the achievements of self-centeredness and misdirection that the city’s leaders seem to focus solely on, Jayce could almost hear Viktor complaining in his head. He huffed a laugh to himself. Jayce knew that he wasn’t any smarter or more well-equipped for a position on the council than Viktor would be, but he recognized that his partner had a tendency to be cold yet brutally forthright in debate. This treatment, which Jayce had been on the receiving end of many times and yet found strangely endearing, was… almost certainly not an attribute that would amuse the council. Especially when most of Viktor’s cold criticisms were directed at the members of Piltover’s high society sitting in the very seats that he and Jayce needed to win the trust of. There was a reason that Jayce was the one burning up in shiny suits on shiny chairs that had little felt feet protecting them from scratching the shiny floor- His fire and brashness was less threatening to the council than Viktor’s cool, calculated gaze. Jayce wiped sweat from his brow and hurried his walk to the lab.

-------

Contrary to popular belief, Jayce did not always enjoy his time in the public eye. Ever since Hextech had thrust him into the spotlight of Piltover’s aspirations towards greatness, the only times he was left alone were the days (and sleepless nights) spent in the lab working on harnessing Hextech’s potential. He might have enjoyed the attention at first; the new lifestyle and opportunities felt electrifying, and he’d found he had a natural talent for charming Piltover’s upper ranks. These days, though, he hardly had a moment without being surrounded by masses of people eager to catch a glimpse of the golden boy- he was passed around from party to party with the occasional speech or diplomatic foray squeezed into his schedule. Sometimes Jayce thought that if he melted himself down and left the husk of his body behind, standing straight up with a polite smile and champagne glass in hand, mid-conversation with yet another white-toothed diplomat, nobody would notice at all. Sometimes the idea was extremely tempting.

Jayce’s keyring jangled as he slipped his key into the Academy’s lock and pushed through the building’s outer doors. Piltover’s science academy was an architectural marvel- he supposed it would have to be, considering the brilliant minds that had passed through these same doors and come out the other side to shape many of the city’s most important infrastructural and technological marvels. The building contained many different laboratories, and the space Jayce was heading towards was all the way at the top of the 8 storey building- he normally took the staircase when unaccompanied, but after letting his foot fall heavily upon the first step, Jayce decided to take his risks with the brass elevator instead. He hadn’t realized how tired he felt or how late it had gotten- the building was deserted at this hour. Jayce pressed the button for his floor- sagged against the wall and laid his face to the cool metal as the lift creaked its way upwards.

-------

When Jayce opened the door to the lab, he did not turn on the lights. Closing the door silently behind him, he stepped over piles of notebooks and let himself drop carelessly into a squeaky desk chair. In the moonlight, the room was full of shadows. The lab’s large window drapes were permanently pulled open, casting a glow on countless half-assembled inventions softly glinting from where they sat awaiting completion. Jayce let out a rattling sigh, bent to lay his cheek against the cold wooden desktop, and squeezed his eyes shut. A chair creaked from across the room. Jayce knew who it was, and didn’t bother to lift his head from where it was buried in his arms. He was only momentarily surprised that Viktor was still there- the man was notorious for running himself ragged at his work and almost never returned home. Slow footsteps stopped in front of the desk where Jayce had collapsed and after a moment, he felt the light touch of a hand brushing his hair. Jayce looked up to meet Viktor’s eyes.

They did this sometimes. (They never talked about it). Jayce would come back, at the end of a day spent under the public’s eye: chest cavity burnt hollow and mind scorched out from the day, and would look up to meet Viktor’s eyes. Viktor would look back into him with a silent expression, cool and steadying. Jayce would let the feeling wash over and around and into him. He would search Viktor’s eyes and catch a glimpse of the mind moving behind golden glass, and Viktor would just hold his steady gaze until he could see that Jayce had felt his heart stop slowly smoking and become himself again.
“Exciting meeting?” Viktor asked quietly, breaking the silence of the dark lab. Jayce let out a long sigh, feeling some of the day’s tension disappear from his slumping shoulders.
“Hmmhhm,” he made a noncommittal noise and scrubbed a hand down his face.
Viktor cracked a hint of a smile in sympathy. “Well,” he tilted his head after a moment’s pause, “I suppose there’s always tomorrow.”
This was true- but uncharacteristic to hear Viktor, who left the lab even less than Jayce entered it these days, admit. Jayce leaned his head back to regard his partner, stretching out a leg to bump gently against Viktor’s shoe. Without Jayce having to ask, Viktor pulled over a chair and sat down opposite him, handing his cane over the desk to Jayce so he could lean it against the wall behind them.

This was another one of their unspoken traditions. Ever since Jayce’s promotion to the council, he had found himself able to return to the lab less and less often despite his best efforts. From the windows of Piltover’s council building, the science academy could just be seen on the horizon- sometimes Jayce would gaze out at it during a meeting running particularly late, and more often than not the window of their lab would still be lit- his partner up and working late into the night. Sometimes, Jayce would arrive back at the academy the next morning to find Viktor still scratching out equations at the chalkboard, leaning more heavily on his cane and with dark circles under his eyes, but still determined to prove his most recent theory. Jayce knew his partner, by disposition and upbringing, was accustomed to sacrificing many things for the sake of progress- and most of the time it worked. But this didn’t mean that Jayce liked seeing Viktor destroying himself for his work. Their work. This didn’t mean he didn’t miss Viktor more than ever, even if he didn’t say it out loud.

Those first months were difficult, but eventually they settled into a routine- Jayce managed to sneak away to the lab every few evenings, Viktor was always there, and a pattern was formed.

Sometimes they sat in silence and worked side by side, sometimes they left the lab and sometimes they didn’t. On the really bad days, when Jayce walked in and could tell that his partner was drowning in his own mind, Jayce would just open his arms for Viktor and hold him until his eyes dropped shut. The routine changed, but Viktor would always let his guard down when Jayce was there, and sleep till morning.

By nature of being a scientist, there were always plenty of unknowns in his life- but never let it be said that Jayce Talis didn’t know his partner.

Viktor was a sharp palette knife painting of a man, all geometric angles and shadows that Jayce wanted to tuck himself into when the rest of the world wasn’t looking. The rest of the world barely ever looked, when it came to Viktor- not where it counted. Jayce would never be foolish enough to think he knew all of Viktor, but what he did see was for Jayce Talis to keep- it would never be the property of Piltover or it’s Golden Boy. Despite every innovation and improvement that Jayce and Viktor had made together with Hextech, no matter how many different ways Jayce wanted to scream and force them all to recognize his partner’s brilliance, it was always him alone being lauded while Piltover’s elite pushed Viktor into the shadows again and again. No amount of expensive champagne could make Jayce ever forget it, and he hated them. He hated the feeling of the council’s hardback chairs, he hated the days wasted in courts while his hands itched to scribble equations and theorems across their perfect white tablecloths. He hated every member of the council and the way their eyes raked across his body without sparing a second for his mind. Jayce used to love the recognition from the city’s elite, but as time wore on, they all began to feel dull and tedious compared to the presence of the one person whose opinion mattered to him anymore (the only one who looked at him and always saw Jayce Talis, instead of the Man of Progress).

Every time Jayce stepped from the lab and back into his picture-perfect Piltover role, he knew he was living on borrowed time. Always a quick learner, the most important lesson Jayce had picked up was this- never let them know you. If he slipped up and showed any weakness, they could tear him apart in an instant- snatching away the dreams of Hextech’s future and ruining everything he and Viktor had fought for.

So, Jayce allowed himself to experience a regression of sorts. All the ego, all the brash confidence with none of the gentleness or thoughtfulness that made Jayce Talis. It was a shiny, burning, mask of youthful immortality- all of the traits he hated most, brought to the surface to appeal to Piltover’s rich and famous. It worked extremely well, and when the parties were over, Jayce sometimes found himself frozen with fear that he would reach to remove the mask and find it had become the only part left of him.

Far too much hung in the balance for Jayce to allow his mask to slip, and so he let Piltover make him their Golden Boy.

Jayce hated having to attend council meetings and every party that Viktor inevitably turned down his invitation to. Jayce didn’t like who he was when he couldn’t be Viktor’s partner. Jayce liked holding Viktor’s hands when they were covered in chalk, and liked the way Viktor would sometimes lean quietly against him when he stood very still in front of the lab blackboard at night. Jayce decided that it came down to this: the way Viktor’s accent twisted out of his mouth when his brain moved faster than his mouth could keep up with, the little pieces of hair that curled in front of his ears in the summer heat, the mole just under his eye which Jayce had memorized the exact angular location of.
“What are you thinking of?” Viktor’s voice rasped softly.
Now, tonight, Viktor sat across from Jayce with all the frantic energy drained out of him- leg stretched out under the desk, frowning with half-lidded eyes up from the book he hadn’t turned a page in for the past half-hour.
Jayce was sure the expression he couldn’t wipe off his face fast enough would have been sappy enough to inspire an eyeroll in a slightly more conscious Viktor.
“Stop talking, V,” Jayce grinned cheekily and scuffed the toe of Viktor’s boot under the desk.
His partner huffed in annoyance, ignored him, and went back to pretending to read.

-------

Like watching a sand-castle erode slowly in the wash of a tide, Jayce waits as Viktor slumps lower and lower in the seat, his partner- his lovely, clever, exhausted partner- finally giving in to who knows how many days worth of weariness, and sleeping with his head resting on his arms, splayed over the desk.
What are you thinking of?

Here is what Jayce was thinking:
Jayce was thinking that Viktor’s passion is a quiet flood, the kind it’s impossible to realize the depth of until it’s too late- until you think to take a breath but find that the feeling has pooled in to fill every empty space inside you when you weren’t paying attention. And you don’t even care. You swim deeper, and inside you is the same spark you’ve always felt burning away at your core- and the water welcomes you, and it doesn’t smother you, and the spark continues to trust the both of you and it stays lit, but now it doesn’t scorch your skin or leave the inside of your chest hollow and smoldering. Now it meets the water and is cooled and heats in return.
And you realize that you were right, and the two of you are always right, and maybe as long as you stay right here in the pale light and watch Viktor’s chest rising and falling gently from across the desk things will be alright.

Silently, Jayce reaches over papers of equations and diagrams to place his hand gently in Viktor’s outstretched palm. Jayce matches his breathing to his sleeping partner’s slow inhale-exhales and feels the last of the day’s tension slipping away from the point of contact where his fingers intertwine with Viktor’s. Together, they wait for the sunrise.
