Summary

The second that James saw Sirius sobbing on the ledge, his body wracking and his cries so loud they echoed, so loud that all James could hear was the sound of despair, James had known instantly.

No one had ever affected Sirius this way, had ever had the ability to hurt him the way his brother did.

Regulus.

James has many regrets. Hell, he could list them for hours and not get even halfway through. But out of everyone he's ever done, he doesn't think he's ever closed his eyes and wished please, please, please for something to be fake, or to somehow morph what he's seeing in front of him, and out of all his desperation; it didn't work, it never works.

When he had seen Sirius sobbing on that ledge, his body shaking from the intensity of his crying, James had known. Nothing had ever gotten Sirius upset like Regulus did, no one could ever affect him the same way, and James knew.

He had been such an idiot. He wishes he could go back in time and shake his past self for being so stupid, for not protecting him. Oh God, Regulus. James had really thought that he had him. He had been so close, just to have it all ripped from his fingertips again.

He'll never love anyone like this again. He doesn't want to. He just wants Regulus, but he can't have him, and it's all his fault.

He knew that he was being selfish, but he had fled straight to his home when he found out. He just couldn't bear it, to look at Sirius, when he was such a clear reminder of what James had lost.

Every person with silky black hair, or piercing grey eyes caught his eye and always made him do a double take, only for it to feel like his heart had got ripped straight out of his chest when he realized that it was just another stranger. Still, he searched for them in crowds, just to feel that sense of flickering hope for a brief moment before it turned into crippling despair.

Regulus eyes bore into him, staring wide at him suspiciously. "You sure you'll catch me?" He questioned, peering down at the steep slope he was standing before, with James at the bottom.

James isn't sure why this random memory is just coming to him now, but he remembers the day so clearly it could have been yesterday. They had been going to visit Sirius at the local medics, the time that he had gotten a concussion after scaling the side of a building for a dare. He had brought eleven year old Regulus along with him since he was constantly pestering everyone to go visit his brother. It irritated most people, how energetic Regulus was, but it only ever endeared James. He had volunteered to take Regulus to see Sirius, and the bright grin he had gotten in response was more than worth it.

James laughed. "Yes, I'm sure, Reg. It's only a little jump, and I wouldn't let you hurt yourself, would I?"

His eyes brightened and he pushed himself off the ledge, and James held out his arms quickly to catch Regulus when he tumbled off the ledge. Regulus's arms instantly looped around his neck to stable himself, and James grinned widely.

James didn't notice the tears forming in his eyes until they're rolling down his cheeks and he brings up his hand to roughly wipe them away. He's sitting on the rooftop of The Last Drop, the exact place he had sat with the other Regulus, even if that feels like lifetime ago at this point.

That's the most he ever got of Regulus, and even though it's not really him, James supposes, he would take that over not having him at all. He would take anything over not having him at all, because he can't even think about Regulus anymore with out it hurting so much that he thinks a better fate would be to die, but it's so painful to not think about him either, because he can feel himself forgetting him, details gradually slipping away at time passes. 

The exact shade of his eyes, James can describe, but he can't quite recall for all of his gazing into them and dreaming about them. the sound of his laugh, a childish giggle, and then a almost sadistic cackle.

It's getting hard to ignore all the piled up letters from his friends. Lily, Mary, Remus, Marlene. None from Sirius, but James can expect that. He wouldn't want to talk to him either. He's read the letters, sure, but he can't bring himself to reply just yet. He can't bring himself to do much more than sit on the rooftop alone, thinking of all his memories and what ifs.

Maybe he's wasting Regulus's sacrifice by spending his time not doing anything meaningful, but even he isn't sure what Regulus would've wanted anymore. He'd given Regulus a space on the mural, taking time to perfect every detail. 

He'd nearly given up multiple times, the sight too painful to even bear looking at when he knew he would never see him again, but he told himself that he do something for Regulus, to at least humour the idea that his death hadn't been a complete waste.

He stared down at the piece of paper in his hands, that he had been holding for so long he had almost memorized the pattern of the rough paper. He knew all too well what meaning it held.

It was an old tradition, to write a dead loved ones name on a piece of paper and send it into the sky, to let them go. Only problem is, James can't bring himself to let go of the paper, because that would mean letting go of Regulus, and evidently, James has never been good at that.

So now he's sitting on the rooftop, crying over a stupid piece of paper.

"I'm sorry, Reg." He sniffles, voice cracking.

He holds up the paper, and with one final look at it, lets it go. 

James feels a weight on his shoulder, and glances down to see Regulus's head on his shoulder, black hair tickling his neck. He hides a grin in the silky locks, breathing in the signature scent of his shampoo.

"Don't get a big head about it," Regulus mutters below him. "I can literally feel your already massive ego inflating."

James snorts, hesitating to even speak in case he ruins the moment. Regulus brings his head up to look James in the eyes, and James doesn't think that anything could ever top this moment.

He realizes what Reg is going for when he starts leaning in, and he knows that he shouldn't because this isn't his Regulus, but it's still Regulus, so he can't help but lean in and press his lips to his. 

Out of all the adjectives that could describe how James is feeling right now, he doesn't think he could pick one that would ever properly capture the fireworks he feels in his chest right now.

Regulus's lips are soft, and they're moving gently against James's, and he brings up his hands to gently cradle Regulus's face.

I love you.
