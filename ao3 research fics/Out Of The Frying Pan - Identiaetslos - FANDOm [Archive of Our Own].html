Out Of The Frying Pan

Identiaetslos

Summary:

After losing her arm again in another fight, Sevika finds herself having to be domestic. However, this new role comes with the penance of both she and Jinx realizing they're falling in love with each other.

Chapter 1

Chapter Text

The weeks following the Enforcer attack on Vander’s statue were some of the hardest Sevika had to endure. Which was saying a lot considering Sevika had endured much throughout the course of her life. Propaganda from Piltover, predictably, painted Zaunites as monstrous enemies. This also meant a slough of vigilantes coming down and “seeking justice” on their own, copycats, and a host of deep fakes to try and undermine Jinx.

And whatever it was she was trying to do.

What was she trying to do? At first, Sevika figured it was continuing Silco’s work, or maybe Vander’s work. Having had a space to finally get to know Silco’s daughter, it wasn’t something so clearly defined. She was her own story with her own mess. Maybe it was as simple as just trying to live out her days, have the freedom to be left alone...but, it was more than that: She did things for other people. Despite her unpredictable nature at times, her Shimmer-filled PTSD fits, Jinx had a good heart. It was worth dying for.

Not dying. Turning death into a fighting chance to live. That was closer to it.

However, this jar was going to be the death of Sevika.

Having lost her arm...again...in all the fighting, and waiting for a new one to be crafted, Sevika had the dubious distinction of playing maid to Jinx and Isha while they were out doing the Lord’s work.

With a sharp sigh of frustration, Sevika braced the jar against the wall to the skateboard countertop of the kitchen peninsula she worked in. Clutching a knife in her remaining arm she stabbed at the top of the jar, only to have it impact on the lid wrong and squirt away onto its side.

“Come on.” Sevika growled at the olives she wanted to throw in the dinner she wanted to surprise Jinx and Isha with: Something she learned to make while on her own in her youth. She righted the jar and braced it against the wall once more and stabbed at it.

Same thing.

“Uuuugh!” Sevika growled more loudly.

The front door opened and then slammed shut meaning Jinx and Isha were back and the surprise had been ruined.

“Honey, I’m home!” Jinx called from the other room in her usual mocking tone.

Sevika righted the jar and sighed in defeat. “Hi. Can you give me a hand?” She asked, glancing at the wall behind her.

Jinx and Isha rounded the corner and came into the kitchen.

Isha raised fingerguns at the jar and shot at it in support of Sevika’s plight. It was enough to elicit a partial smile from the latter.

Jinx’s face softened seeing Sevika’s predicament and the spoiled surprise. She grabbed the jar and popped the top off with a deft move.

“My hero.” Sevika said wryly.

“Shut up.” Jinx shot back with a smirk on her face and placed the jar back in front of Sevika. “What’s all this?”

“Goulash. My own take on it at least.” Sevika said. “I wouldn’t call it gourmet but it got me by when things got low.”

Sevika turned to the stove along the wall behind her, her back turned to Jinx. For a big, dumb, ogre, Sevika wasn’t as bad as Jinx made her out to be. At first, the relationship had been strained; Jinx incorrectly saw her as competition, but in reality, she was just a girl who was a firm believer in the Lanes and needed a family. Just like a lot of people who gravitated to Vander or Silco or Benzo or one of the other leaders who managed to make it down here long enough to have a semblance of a following.

Perhaps it was self-esteem issues that held Sevika back, perhaps it was a genuine desire to not be the center of attention, but she was worth more than she made herself out to be. Nobody listened to her, and Jinx wondered if that was partly her fault.

The food sizzled in the pan as Sevika worked, her short, black hair falling away from the back of her neck. She wore her typical black tanktop, revealing her broad, muscular shoulders, arm that was the size of a tree trunk and felt like one when colliding with it. Either harshly or softly.

In some ways, Sevika reminded Jinx of Vi: Always happier hitting something, but never too good to stop and smell the flowers. Sevika was pretty, too, in her own way. Not like Jinx was ever going to tell her that.

“When we were kids, Violet used to make macaroni with little hot dogs. We’d steal the stuff from somewhere, find a hole to cook it in and then ate while we bet on drunks coming and going from The Last Drop: Who would make it 3 steps before passing out, or 5 steps before puking.” Jinx reminisced and laughed. She came around the peninsula and leaned against the counter near the stove.

Sevika laughed softly at her pan and then glanced up at Jinx. The stove light made everything in this room soft and orange. Perhaps it was the smell of food or the gentleness of this moment, but it made Sevika pause and admire just exactly how pretty she actually was.

“I don’t remember betting on you.” Jinx said with an amused tone, her eyebrows lit artfully.

“Maybe if you’d known me when I was younger you would have.” Sevika replied, suddenly wishing she had. “I never drink more than I can handle, but...that was a hard lesson to learn.”

“The valuable ones generally are.” Jinx quipped. The thought of a younger Sevika danced through Jinx’s mind causing a small smile to stretch across her face. It was hard to imagine Sevika as a dumb kid. As much as Jinx made fun of her, she always seemed to be someone who had her shit together. Those must have been harsh lessons to learn, she mused, reminding herself that even the toughest people came from somewhere.

Not taking her eyes from Sevika, Jinx picked up a bottle of spices and popped the lid for her out of consideration before handing it to her. Sevika’s jaw moved almost imperceptibly and her gray eyes, lit like the left over embers in a cooling fire, flitted over Jinx’s jaw. To her lips.

No...no no no. Jinx thought to herself. Sevika was not the kind of person you kiss. She was the kind of person who dug you out of firefights and lifted you into high places because she’s extremely tall and muscular. Except those muscles were soft. As was the rest of her. Those lips would be soft, too, and slightly waxy from the lipstick she wore.

“You’re going to burn dinner.” Was the only thing Jinx could manage.

Sevika broke her gaze as if waking from a trance and resumed cooking.

The moment was gone, but what hung in the air forever changed the two of them.

“It shouldn’t be much longer.” Sevika said. Her words were brisk. She was nervous. “You should tell Isha to wash up.”

“Yeah. I should go do that, too. Don’t want to poison myself with Lane juice.” Jinx joked and pushed off the counter. It would be lying if she said she didn’t have a nervous shake of her own.

Over Sevika?

Jinx hazarded one last glance at her right hand woman as her back was turned once more, lost in her cooking. This time, she let herself admire the shape of Sevika’s body, even the curve of her ass. It was a nice one. She’d seen it without pants, too. It was even nicer then.

What was she even thinking?

That there could be worse. She answered herself. There were people out there that wouldn’t hesitate to betray her. Or worse, Isha. Yet Sevika didn’t. There could be worse people to play house with. Perhaps...it didn’t need to be playing.

Was it Jinx or Powder who reached out and placed a grateful hand on Sevika’s back? It didn’t matter. What mattered was the fact Sevika was here, had always been here, and would never leave. She let her hand linger for a few moments before retreating to get Isha.

Whatever was going on was something mutual. Lightness against the dark. If Violet was here, despite it being Sevika, she’d say it would be something to build on. And she was right.

Chapter 2: Sweet Scars and Pillow Talk

Chapter Text

Conversation over dinner didn’t amount to much. At least not what either Jinx or Sevika were hoping for. They sat across from each other, Isha at Jinx’s side like normal and scooped bites of food with the quiet scraping of utensils on cookware.

For Jinx it was Isha sitting there next to her. What would she think if she knew that she was having thoughts about SeViKA. The thought sent nausea into Jinx’s stomach, but it wasn’t the disgusted kind. More like the kind that wondered what it would be like to be happy. To be loved. To be loved the way she’d always envisioned in her darkest fantasies. It frightened Jinx more than anything.

For Sevika, it was Isha sitting next Jinx. A child shouldn’t watch the closest thing she have to parents be anything more than parents. No no...not parents. That was frightening. Guardians. That was it. Aunties. Even if it was an innocent form of intimacy. Being intimate with Jinx? Sevika shook off that thought and concentrated on the goulash. It came out nicely tonight, not over cooked, not under cooked, not too salty.

As she looked down at her food, Sevika felt a sharp pain from being kicked in the shin coming from Jinx’s side of the table. “Hey! What was that for?” She growled.

“You started it.” Jinx accused.

“Started what?” Sevika asked, raising both her thick eyebrows.

“You touched my leg.” Jinx shot. Her eyes narrowed at Sevika.

“I didn’t?” Sevika defended. She was even more confused. “I’ve just been here, eating my food.”

“Sevika, if you want to grope my footsies with yours, you don’t need to lie about it.” Jinx continued and slid off her chair to go wash her empty bowl.

“Oookay. Whatever.” Sevika said and rolled her eyes. She rose to take Isha’s empty bowl but Isha shook her head and tottered over to Jinx with a yawn to tell them both she was sleepy.

Jinx already had the water running to wash the dishes.

“I got this.” Sevika said instinctively.

“It’s my turn.” Jinx argued.

“No, it isn’t.” Sevika shot back. She was even more confused. The calendar was right in front of Jinx’s eyes with “SEVIKA” clearly circled.

“Yeah it is.” Jinx mumbled to a plate as she cleaned it.

“No...no it isn’t.” Sevika objected. Even though she was grateful for help, she hated when people did things for her.

“Ohmygod Sevika, will you just stop for once?!” Jinx shot and glanced up at her. The look on Jinx’s face told her she was being nice for once. The softness around Jinx’s eyes told Sevika not to push her.

Sevika relented and leaned against the counter with her arm across her chest. “You know, before you I could take care of myself just fine.”

Jinx slowed her dish washing and glanced back at Sevika, only making momentary eye contact. There was something more behind Jinx’s expression that slowed Sevika’s roll even more.

“Did Silco ever make you read the paper?” Jinx asked.

“Yeah, a few times.” Sevika replied.

“Even after his eye injections, they wouldn’t always work right, so he couldn’t always read the fine print. When I was little, I’d read the Want Ads to him so he would know who needed what, who was looking for a job, who was looking for workers.” Jinx reminisced. She dried the remaining dish and set it on the drying rack before turning around and leaning her backside against the sink.

She glanced up at Sevika with a face that was filled with a softness that reminded Sevika of the gentleness from earlier.

Jinx snorted in a sardonic amusement. “Violet would get that way too, sometimes. She’d take a hit too hard to the head—believe me she’s taken many--” the latter sentence was said with humorous sarcasm. “--but she couldn’t focus her eyes, so me or Claggor or Vander or Asshole McGee would have to help her move around the house so she wouldn’t fall over or bump into things and hurt herself.”

Sevika snorted, seeing the point to Jinx’s story. “Who’s Asshole McGee?” She asked.

“Milo. He’s the one who--” Jinx paused and a flicker of lavender filled her eyes.

Sevika placed a calming hand on Jinx’s. “You don’t need to tell me.” She reassured.

Jinx composed herself and, this time, looked Sevika in her eyes. “Yeah I do. He was the kid who gave me the name ‘Jinx.’ He always had something to say. Especially about me. And especially to Vi.” Jinx’s jaw tensed and the Shimmer returned to her eyes and contorted into rage.

“She. Always. Took. His. Side. Never defended me—no, that’s a lie—she did, but what did it mean? It was only when I was there, but behind closed doors!” Jinx’s voice was a snarl and she flipped her hand so that she gripped Sevika hard to the point it hurt. “They always betray! You will betray!”

Jinx’s voice was mangled rage. Flashbacks of Violet and her conversation the night before flickered through Jinx’s mind. Powder begged to come back, but she couldn’t she was dead. Or was she? In between gasps for some semblance of reality was Sevika’s face and Jinx as her hands as they clutched both sides of Sevika’s face until blood was drawn. The blood of someone she cared about...someone she l---

“NO!” Jinx tore herself away from her flashback and from Sevika.

Sevika breathed heavily, blood dripping from scratch marks left by Jinx’s nails onto the collar of her tanktop.

“They’re dead because of me.” Jinx whimpered and sank to the kitchen floor. “And I will kill you too, Sevika.”

Sevika sighed and sank to where Jinx was. She searched Jinx’s face for that spark of herself to guide her back from the chaos. At no time was Sevika ever angry with Jinx, just enraged for her. Between Vi, Silco, Chembarons, and the evilness of life, she’d borne the worst of it. Finding the trace of Jinx she’d been searching for, she gave her a soft smile before wrapping her arm around her and lifting her off the tile.

“What are you doing? I told you to stay away.” Jinx protested. “Put me down. I’m not potatoes.”

Sevika carried Jinx into the living room where Jinx liked to be with Isha and set her down on the tattered remains of the most comfortable and uncomfortable sofa ever imagined. “No. Potatoes don’t whine so much.” She quipped.

Jinx sat on a cushion beside Sevika and looked down at the front of her shirt and the bloodstains left behind. “This was my one clean shirt, Sevika.”

Sevika gestured apologetically. “That’s for accusing me of playing footsie with you when I didn’t.”

Jinx let out something between a scoff and a chuckle. “Okay fine.” She let up. “If it wasn’t you and it wasn’t me. I hope it wasn’t rats.”

A giggle echoed from the hallway leading from the kitchen to Isha’s room and the sound of footsteps along the hardwood thumped before either Jinx or Sevika could scold Isha for not being in bed.

“I see.” Jinx said and skewed her lips in a half smile.

“Successfully punked.” Sevika said with a chuckle which turned into a laugh.

Jinx laughed too. It felt good to. Jinx couldn’t remember the last time had. The fact that she was laughing with Sevika was even more ridiculous and devolved into even deeper laughter.

Sevika caught her breath and wiped the tears from her eyes before speaking her next thought. “You think Isha might have been trying to set us up?”

Jinx knew what Sevika was getting at. Isha had caught a few uncharacteristically tender moments as of late and it seemed to please her. Anything for a daughter? Well, not this thing. Jinx mused. Not with Sevika of all people. They’d known each other their whole lives, fought each other, damn near killed each other. Silco even purposely set them against each other just to see who would win.

Or maybe that was all in her head. Jinx mused. Maybe it was like with Vi: She needed to grow into herself first before she could play the big fat hero. Sevika was just the punching bag, the opening act that caught all of the crowd’s tomatoes before she got on.

That was cruel thought. Jinx wondered how that truly affected Sevika.

Sevika didn’t seem to complain. She took it in stride and still stood beside her when all seemed lost. Despite the yelling, the fighting, even murdering Silco, Sevika was still here like a big dumb idiot.

What was it? Love?

That word was scary. Jinx thought as she leaned over into Sevika. Her chest felt good. Solid. Warm. It was only right for Jinx to wrap her arms around Sevika’s waist. She was on Sevika’s bad side, but Sevika still manage to embrace her with her good arm.

The world had slipped away. All of Zaun, Piltover, the escalating chaos disintegrated into nothing. Sevika closed her eyes and buried her nose in the top of Jinx’s head. If the world knew of this one, simple gesture, this one simple feeling, perhaps it could get over itself.

Sevika kicked off her boots and sank down onto her back, not daring to let go of Jinx for one second.

This feeling is what she fought for, killed for, bled for. Who was she if there wasn’t someone in her heart to keep breathing for?

Jinx lifted her head and looked once more into Sevika’s eyes. This time her vision was clear even if Sevika’s wasn’t. Oh those gray irises were deep, soft, filled with something that scared Jinx down to the very pit of existence, but something she knew. Something she wanted. There Sevika was, in all her idiocy, with her one remaining arm wrapped around her, scratches all over her face with the purest love in her eyes and all Jinx could do was love her back.

There were worse people out there. Jinx mused as she moved up to Sevika’s face and cupped it, this time gently. Definitely worse people to get mixed up with, or in, in this case.

Jinx closed her eyes like she’d seen Vi do a hundred times with Caitlyn and pressed her lips to Sevika’s. Instead of rage and fear and hatred, she finally understood.

This is why she fought.
