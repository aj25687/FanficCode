Work Text:

The heavy door swung shut behind Sevika, locking her into the quiet of Silco's office. Outside, the rain drummed steadily against the windows, the sound filling the silence. The only other noise was the soft scratching of Silco’s pen as it moved across the paper. Sevika leaned back against the far wall, crossing her arms tightly over her chest, her muscles knotted from the frustration and exhaustion she was barely holding in check. Her eyes burned with irritation as she watched Silco, the calm he exuded only sharpening her own feelings of frustration.

 

She had reached the end of her patience with the cycle—the endless chaos, the constant damage control, and the never-ending reports on Jinx’s latest mess. She was done standing here, doing this same dance, day after day. Her restraint was wearing thin, a tight rope ready to snap between her desire to demand change and the reluctant respect she still had for Silco.

 

“Another disaster averted?” Silco’s voice came, smooth and unconcerned, without him looking up from his papers.

 

“For now,” Sevika replied sharply, her gaze narrowing. She watched him sign another document, his nonchalant demeanor only making her more irritable. Didn’t anything faze him? Didn’t he feel the same frustration, the same weariness from the constant fires they had to put out? She ground her teeth in silence, forcing herself to stay composed.

 

“You’re unhappy,” Silco noted, his tone quiet but observant, his eyes still fixed on the paper as he set down his pen, folding his fingers over it.

 

“Unhappy?” Sevika echoed bitterly, pushing herself off the wall with barely contained resentment. “I’m fed up, Silco. She’s not learning. You lecture her every damn day about patience, control, subtlety—and yet all Jinx seems to hear is noise. She does the exact opposite of everything you tell her.”

 

Silco’s lips curled into a faint, knowing smile, but Sevika saw nothing funny in the situation. His calm demeanor only intensified her frustration, making her feel like she was the unreasonable one. He always seemed to know something she didn’t, and it only made her angrier.

 

“Oh, she listens,” Silco said, his voice soft but certain. “Far more carefully than you realize.”

 

Sevika snorted, shaking her head, the memory of tonight’s chaos still fresh in her mind. “No, Silco, she doesn’t. She pretends. She nods along, even parrots your words back to you sometimes. But when it matters, she picks chaos every single time.”

 

Silco regarded her with a thoughtful, almost curious gaze. “She chooses chaos because it’s familiar. It’s her comfort zone, her safe place. To Jinx, order feels unnatural—unless she can see why it’s necessary.”

 

Sevika’s patience snapped a little further. Her voice was tight with frustration as she replied, “Necessary? How many times do you have to explain something before it sinks in? She set off an explosion just tonight—two hours after you told her to be quiet. She doesn’t listen, Silco. She hears only what she wants to.”

 

Silco tilted his head, considering her words. “It’s true. Jinx often appears to disregard what I say. Sometimes she defies me on purpose, deliberately going against my instructions. But that’s exactly how I know she’s listening.”

 

Sevika frowned, confusion spreading through her frustration. She hadn’t expected that response. “What?”

 

“Jinx resists instructions because she's testing them,” Silco explained carefully. “Chaos rejects structure unless it proves itself worthy. She pushes against every rule to see if it holds—if it truly matters. She needs to understand why a boundary exists before she’ll ever respect it.”

 

Sevika shook her head again, stubbornly resisting his calm reasoning. “You’re giving her too much credit. Vander tried the same approach. Trying to turn Vi into a leader by piling responsibility onto her without ever explaining the reasons. Vi didn’t listen either, just rebelled out of anger and resentment. The last thing Zaun needs is another angry teenager running rampant because someone thought patience alone would fix them.”

 

Silco’s expression darkened slightly at Vander’s name, but he kept his composure. “Vander didn’t practice patience. He was desperate. He didn’t have the luxury of carefully guiding Violet; he had to rely on her immediate strength, her capacity for violence. Violet learned how to fight, but never how to lead. She rebelled because she was overwhelmed and confused. She never understood the why behind her actions.”

 

The truth of his words hit Sevika harder than she expected. Memories of Vi flooded her mind—Vi’s unyielding defiance, her fists always ready to fly, never pausing to ask why. Sevika had resented that impulsiveness, the chaos that Vander had inadvertently fostered by not giving her the guidance she needed. Vi had burned out because no one had shown her how to control herself.

 

But Jinx wasn’t Vi, was she? Sevika pushed that thought away, refusing to draw parallels just yet.

 

“You really think your methods will work?” Sevika asked, her voice tight with skepticism. “That she’ll suddenly start listening, even after all the times she’s ignored you?”

 

Silco leaned forward, his eyes unwavering, his voice calm and sure. “She already listens. Every time Jinx ignores me, it’s a sign she heard every word. Every time she defies me, it’s evidence that she’s carefully considering my words, testing them. She challenges my rules to see if they hold up. And each time she challenges them, she comes closer to understanding why they exist.”

 

Sevika paused, her frustration ebbing slightly, replaced with reluctant recognition. “You seem awfully confident about all this.”

 

“Because I've seen it,” Silco said quietly. His voice was softer now, layered with quiet conviction. “Every time she defies me, she's also observing carefully to see what happens next. When her rebellions don't bring the satisfaction she expects, she internalizes the lesson. Her chaos is deliberate, Sevika—not mindless.”

 

Sevika stared at Silco, her irritation momentarily giving way to bewilderment. The careful confidence in his voice had always unsettled her, especially when it came to Jinx, whose actions seemed entirely erratic and unpredictable.

 

“You call her chaos deliberate?” Sevika echoed, incredulity evident in her voice. “You really believe that every explosion, every disruption, is calculated?”

 

“Calculated may not be the right word,” Silco admitted calmly, leaning back slightly, hands folded loosely before him. “But deliberate? Yes. Consider this—why does Jinx resist so fiercely?”

 

“Because she's uncontrollable,” Sevika answered without hesitation. “Because she’s reckless.”

 

“No,” Silco corrected patiently. “Because she's uncertain. She is constantly probing for limits, testing boundaries. Chaos despises arbitrary rules. She needs to know which of our rules are indispensable, and which are simply convenient. Until she learns this distinction, she’ll test them relentlessly.”

 

Sevika’s gaze narrowed, her doubts rising again. “So you’re saying she’s testing your authority?”

 

“No,” Silco said calmly. “Not my authority—my reasons. She respects strength, but not strength without purpose. Every command I give is a hypothesis, and she feels compelled to test it. If the reason behind it is solid, she’ll accept it. If it falters, she’ll dismiss it. But once she understands the why, she’ll respect the rule.”

 

Silco paused, watching Sevika’s expression shift from skepticism to something approaching understanding. “Have you ever noticed what Jinx does after these rebellious moments?”

 

Sevika frowned, considering it before answering. “She sulks. She disappears for hours, even days. Then she comes back, acting as if nothing happened.”

 

Silco nodded, his gaze intent. “Exactly. And during those disappearances, she reflects deeply. She isolates herself because she's processing the outcomes of her actions. She’s examining the consequences, determining if the disruption truly achieved her goals, or if it was simply empty defiance.”

 

“You think Jinx cares about consequences?” Sevika asked, unable to keep the harshness from her voice. “I've seen her ignore collateral damage without a second glance.”

 

Silco shook his head gently, a hint of patience in his faint smile. “Collateral damage isn't what weighs on her mind. Jinx measures success differently. She cares about effectiveness. Did her rebellion yield clarity? Did it achieve a meaningful result? When her chaos fails to give her purpose, she’s forced to reassess her methods.”

 

He leaned forward, eyes holding Sevika’s firmly. “I've explained the 'why' behind every decision I’ve ever asked her to make. Even if she resists, even if she rebels violently, that foundation remains. Eventually, she'll be faced with a situation that chaos alone cannot resolve. And when that happens, she'll know exactly where to turn.”

 

Sevika shook her head slowly, conflicted. Silco’s confidence was persuasive, yet she found herself still uncertain. “You're staking Zaun’s entire future on the hope that Jinx will eventually listen.”

 

Silco smiled faintly, meeting her eyes calmly. “No, Sevika. I'm staking Zaun’s future on the certainty that she already does. Every time Jinx rebels against an instruction I've given, she's signaling clearly that she heard every word. If she wasn’t listening, she'd simply disregard me entirely. But she reacts—defiantly, deliberately—precisely because she's weighing my words carefully.”

 

Sevika’s resistance wavered again, her jaw loosening slightly as his perspective sank in. It was a concept she hadn't truly considered before: that Jinx’s rebellions weren't mere impulsive acts, but strategic tests designed to uncover truth.

 

Finally, Sevika broke the silence, her voice low but still tinged with the skepticism that wouldn’t quite let go. “You really think Jinx can lead? That she can stop being the wrecking ball she’s always been and take charge of something more?”

 

Silco turned to face her, his eyes sharp but understanding. “I think she can. She has to, Sevika. Zaun needs a leader who isn’t afraid to break the rules and rewrite them. Jinx, for all her chaos, has that instinct. When she finally sees why those rules matter—when she realizes that the chaos can be controlled—she will lead in ways we can’t even predict yet.”

 

Sevika shook her head again, doubt lingering. "Vander tried teaching Vi restraint—control. He at least understood leadership requires careful thought. Jinx treats everything as a twisted game."

 

Silco’s jaw tightened, bitterness creeping subtly into his tone. "Vander prioritized hollow peace, compromising when strength was needed. His lessons to Violet lacked true conviction, based on what you've described. No wonder she rebelled—burdened with expectations she never fully grasped."

 

Sevika met his gaze directly, sensing his bitterness toward Vander. "Maybe Vander was flawed—but at least he recognized the importance of considering consequences before action. If you send Jinx out representing you, what's to stop her from destroying everything?"

 

Silco’s expression remained calm, but his voice hardened with resolve. "She thrives on chaos because Zaun has never offered her anything else. Vander preached caution, but Zaun requires decisive action, not hesitant compromise. Jinx knows this."

 

"You're certain," Sevika pressed. "Certain enough to let her lead?"

 

"Without hesitation," Silco replied firmly. "I explain the reasons behind my actions. Vander never gave Violet that clarity. He expected blind obedience; Jinx demands understanding."

 

Silence fell between them for a long moment, broken only by the faint tapping of rain against the window. Sevika’s gaze shifted thoughtfully, lingering on Silco’s calm confidence, recognizing for the first time how deeply he believed in the path he’d chosen for Jinx.

 

“You’re more patient than Vander ever was,” she finally acknowledged quietly, the bitterness fading slightly from her voice. “I'll give you that.”

 

"Patience isn't a luxury," Silco said quietly. "It's a necessity. Vander’s urgency cost him dearly. Violet suffered from that desperation; Jinx won't."

 

Sevika inhaled slowly, her shoulders relaxing marginally. She still harbored doubts, questions she couldn't yet fully silence, but the strength of Silco’s certainty had begun to wear down her resistance. She might never fully trust Jinx’s unpredictable nature, but perhaps she could trust Silco’s calculated confidence.

 

“Then we'll see,” Sevika said at last, her tone grudgingly respectful. “I suppose it's your gamble to take.”

 

Silco’s faint smile returned, one corner of his mouth lifting slightly as he turned his gaze back to the rain-soaked window. “Not a gamble, Sevika. A strategy. Chaos only becomes strength when it's fully understood. Jinx is learning one rebellion at a time.”

 

Sevika’s eyes lingered on Silco, frustration fading briefly into quiet thoughtfulness. Memories of Vander rose uncomfortably to the surface, tempered by her growing unease about Jinx’s apparent inability to move past impulsive, childish outbursts.

 

She'd never particularly cared how Vander chose to raise his adopted brood. They weren’t her problem, and she’d kept her distance—but even from afar, Vander’s shortcomings had been painfully evident. Vander had desperately clung to a fragile peace, always avoiding open conflict at all costs, hoping that Zaun could somehow stay together through sheer compromise. He'd placed heavy expectations on Vi’s young shoulders, grooming her for leadership without ever clearly explaining why those responsibilities mattered.

 

Sevika could vividly recall seeing Vander sitting alone after yet another failed attempt to get through to Vi. He never openly showed disappointment in front of her, never raised his voice. But when Vi inevitably stormed off after one of his lectures, Vander would quietly retreat to his bar, pouring himself a stiff drink, sighing heavily in frustration, clearly baffled why his careful advice never seemed to reach her.

 

In Sevika’s eyes, it had been obvious: Vander had tried giving Vi tools, yes, but he’d never explained why she needed them in the first place. Without clarity, Vi’s anger and stubbornness had only grown, leaving her resentful and impulsive, always fighting to carry burdens she'd never fully understood.

 

And now, Sevika thought bitterly, here they stood again, Silco placing his own hopes and expectations onto someone equally unpredictable. She stepped forward, breaking the silence with blunt skepticism.

 

“Say you’re right, and Jinx is listening,” Sevika said cautiously. “How can you honestly expect her to lead when she’s still so… childish? She acts first, thinks second. She makes jokes when things get tense, picks fights over petty insults. Vander at least tried teaching Vi restraint, even if it didn’t stick. But Jinx—she thrives on chaos, Silco. Chaos and impulsivity don’t inspire trust.”

 

He leaned forward, narrowing his eyes. “She’s childish, yes—but never foolish. You mistake her irreverence for ignorance, her defiance for carelessness. When I clearly explain why something matters, when she understands exactly what’s at stake, she thinks strategically. She sees moves others miss.”

 

Sevika’s expression remained openly doubtful as she folded her arms tighter. “You honestly believe she’s capable of leading strategically? That you could send her in your place—say, to a chem-baron meeting—and she’d hold her ground without making enemies or causing unnecessary chaos?”

 

Silco’s faint smile held a dark amusement, tinged with unwavering confidence. “She’d certainly annoy them deliberately,” he conceded calmly. “But she knows precisely how far she can push them. Jinx understands the balance of power—she knows exactly how to wield influence, to disrupt without destroying.”

 

Sevika shifted uneasily, still clearly unconvinced. Her memory flashed back again to Vi’s stubborn arguments with Vander, the girl stomping away angrily, too hurt or confused to internalize Vander’s lessons. Could Jinx truly be different just because Silco took time to explain himself clearly?

 

“You’re certain,” she pressed again, voice tense. “You trust Jinx to step into your shoes, to speak for you without acting out, without making decisions she'd regret later?”

 

“Without hesitation,” Silco repeated firmly, his eyes unflinching. “Because I have explained every choice I’ve ever made, every action I’ve asked of her. Vander never gave Violet that clarity, did he? He assumed obedience would follow naturally, that Violet would blindly accept responsibilities she never understood. Of course she lashed out—of course she rebelled.”

 

Sevika fell quiet again, her doubt still visible but tempered by Silco’s conviction. Perhaps Vander had truly misunderstood Vi, had burdened her without clarity or conviction, but was Jinx truly ready for leadership simply because Silco claimed she understood? It still felt like a dangerous gamble.

 

Silco watched her hesitation closely, leaning back with quiet confidence. “Jinx knows what Zaun needs,” he continued steadily. “She sees beyond meaningless compromises and fragile truces. Her childishness isn’t weakness—it’s her armor, her way of challenging anyone who might underestimate her. Once she fully grasps why order matters, why restraint matters, she’ll surpass anything Vander ever imagined.”

 

Sevika finally sighed deeply, reluctantly nodding once, her expression softened by reluctant acceptance. Silco’s bitterness toward Vander might be harsh, his judgment secondhand, but perhaps he'd identified the truth of Vander’s failure more clearly than she'd realized. Perhaps Zaun truly needed a leader who understood clearly why every battle mattered, why peace without purpose was meaningless.

 

She stepped back slightly, the tension easing from her shoulders. “Then let’s hope she figures that out soon,” Sevika murmured. “For all our sakes.”

 

Silco turned his gaze quietly toward the rain-slicked windows, bitterness fading slightly into calm resolution. “She will. Because unlike Vander, I never assumed blind obedience would be enough. Jinx knows exactly why she fights, why Zaun needs her strength. And that clarity is something Vander never gave Violet.”

 

Sevika could only give pragmatic hope that Silco was right. 

 

 

Sevika stood quietly in the doorway of Jinx’s workshop, arms crossed loosely as she watched Jinx work. The room hummed gently with machinery, punctuated by occasional murmurs as Jinx carefully assembled the prototype water treatment plant. Isha hovered quietly at her side, small hands gripping tools, passing them over at the slightest nod or gesture.

 

Jinx’s movements were deliberate, careful, a stark contrast to her usual chaotic energy. Sevika studied her silently, noting the precision with which Jinx tightened screws, adjusted valves, and tested connections. It was strange, almost surprising, to see Jinx so thoroughly absorbed in a task that didn’t involve fire or explosives.

 

Jinx paused briefly, squinting at a component as she reached toward Isha. “Wrench.”

 

Isha handed it to her quickly, watching with quiet, earnest attention. Sevika noticed how smoothly they worked together, how seamlessly Jinx had adapted to working carefully rather than recklessly.

 

As if sensing Sevika’s thoughtful scrutiny, Jinx glanced over her shoulder, eyes bright with mischief, her familiar smirk breaking through her serious expression. “Ya look surprised back there, Sev. Shocked I haven't destroyed anything yet?”

 

Sevika snorted quietly, stepping forward to study the prototype more closely. “Something like that.”

 

Jinx grinned triumphantly, tossing the wrench lightly into the air before catching it again. “Told you I could pull it off.”

 

Sevika sighed softly, unable to hide the faint amusement in her tone. “You don’t always have to rub it in. Besides, half the time you think you’re proving me wrong, I didn’t doubt you in the first place.”

 

For a brief second, Jinx hesitated, fingers tightening subtly around the wrench, clearly caught off guard by Sevika’s casual admission. But she quickly covered her surprise, shrugging nonchalantly, her usual bravado effortlessly reasserting itself. “Well, duh. You’d be pretty stupid not to.”

 

Sevika raised an eyebrow, the corner of her mouth twitching slightly. “That so?”

 

“Obviously,” Jinx shot back smoothly, turning fully back to her work, deliberately keeping her eyes away from Sevika’s gaze. “I always know exactly what I'm doing, Sev. Maybe you’re finally starting to catch on.”

 

Sevika shook her head faintly, a low hum of acknowledgment her only response. Jinx’s reaction wasn’t unexpected, she always brushed aside Sevika’s quiet admissions of trust as though it was perfectly natural for her to have absolute faith in her abilities, despite the occasional flashes of genuine surprise Sevika still glimpsed beneath the surface.

 

Jinx adjusted another valve, making minute changes as she murmured instructions to Isha, her tone confident again, steady as ever. After a moment, she glanced sideways again, expression deliberately casual. “You know, when the chem-barons agreed to back this, they didn’t think I’d actually manage it.”

 

Sevika studied her carefully. “Did you?”

 

“Of course,” Jinx scoffed, though her gaze flicked away quickly. “They underestimate me. Everyone always does. But you?” She tilted her head slightly, smirking again, hiding whatever else might’ve been beneath her bravado. “You’ve seen enough to know better by now.”

 

Sevika allowed a faint huff, refusing to rise to the bait. “Guess I have.”

 

Jinx nodded firmly, clearly satisfied with herself, passing another tool back to Isha and stepping away briefly to look at the nearly finished prototype.

 

Sevika watched quietly as Jinx studied the machine with a critical eye, fingers tracing the metal edges as if searching for imperfections. It was almost surreal to see Jinx like this—focused, attentive, methodical. If someone had asked Sevika a few years ago whether Jinx was capable of building something other than explosives or tools of destruction, she'd have laughed in their face.

 

Jinx had always thrived in chaos. She took joy in explosions, found comfort in the aftermath of destruction. Sevika had accepted that as an immutable fact, just part of who Jinx was. But seeing her now, meticulously adjusting tubing and carefully aligning valves, Sevika found herself acknowledging just how narrow her view had been.

 

Jinx was still chaos, certainly, but somewhere along the way, she'd learned to control it. The impulsive, unpredictable girl who’d blown off Sevika’s arm was now standing calmly in front of a device meant to provide Zaun something it had never truly had—clean water, a step toward a real future. It was something Sevika had honestly never imagined Jinx would even care about, let alone attempt to build.

 

Jinx stepped back again, head tilted thoughtfully as she murmured quietly to herself, double-checking connections with a seriousness Sevika hadn't fully anticipated. Isha watched her attentively, her admiration clear in wide, quiet eyes, carefully handing Jinx whatever she needed without question.

 

After a moment, Jinx glanced back again, as if sensing the weight of Sevika’s gaze. Her lips twitched, a knowing smirk slipping easily back into place. "If you keep staring like that, I might think you’re actually impressed."

 

Sevika’s expression didn’t shift, but amusement softened her eyes slightly. "Never said I wasn't."

 

For the briefest second, Jinx seemed genuinely surprised again, her fingers pausing briefly against the prototype’s surface. But just as quickly, she covered it, rolling her eyes dramatically as she returned to inspecting her work. "Well, of course you should be. I'm a genius."

 

Sevika gave a quiet huff, stepping forward to glance more closely at the neatly connected pipes, wires, and filtration systems. It was genuinely impressive, even she could admit that. "Never thought I'd see the day you’d put your genius to use building something other than a bomb."

 

Jinx shrugged casually, as if it had never been in doubt. "What can I say? Blowing things up gets boring eventually. Gotta keep things interesting, Sev."

 

Sevika shook her head slightly. "Right. I’ll keep that in mind next time you get bored."

 

Jinx shot her a quick look, eyes brightening with mischievous glee. "You do that."

 

Sevika stepped back again, letting silence settle comfortably between them. Jinx's playful bravado masked any deeper feelings, as usual, but Sevika knew better than to push. Instead, she allowed herself a quiet moment of recognition, acknowledging to herself, if no one else, just how wrong she'd been about Jinx’s potential. The girl wasn't simply capable of destruction. She could build—maybe even rebuild—if she chose to.

 

Jinx gestured toward a tangle of wires and tubing nearby, her voice breaking Sevika from her thoughts. "You just gonna stand there watching, or are you gonna help me finish this thing?"

 

Sevika pushed off the wall, moving toward Jinx with a faint sigh. "What do you need?"

 

"Start untangling," Jinx replied cheerfully, already turning her attention back to her prototype. "Think you can manage that without supervision?"

 

"Watch yourself," Sevika warned lightly, beginning to carefully separate wires, noticing once more how quickly Jinx slipped back into confident ease.

 

Maybe Jinx still thrived in chaos, Sevika thought, sorting quietly through the wires. But she was starting to realize that chaos didn't always mean destruction—sometimes, it meant change.

 

Jinx finally stepped back from the prototype, exhaling quietly and stretching her arms high above her head, a satisfied noise escaping her lips. “Alright, kiddo,” she announced, glancing down at Isha, who still hovered attentively nearby. “Think that’s enough for tonight. Go get your pajamas on and brush your teeth.”

 

Isha nodded obediently, setting down the tool she’d been holding and quietly slipping out of the room. Sevika watched her go before shifting her gaze back to Jinx, who was still studying the water treatment prototype with careful scrutiny, eyes narrowed thoughtfully.

 

“You’ve been at this for hours,” Sevika pointed out mildly. “Surprised you’re stopping now.”

 

Jinx shrugged casually, eyes never leaving the prototype. “If I don’t, she won’t sleep. The kid’s stubborn. She’ll wait me out if I try staying down here.”

 

Sevika hummed quietly in acknowledgment, recognizing the unspoken care beneath Jinx’s casual words. It was another surprising shift, one Sevika had witnessed slowly unfolding but was still adjusting to.

 

The quiet padding of small feet interrupted Sevika’s thoughts as Isha returned, dressed neatly in her pajamas, her eyes wide and innocent as she approached Jinx, stopping at her side.

 

Jinx looked down at her with a skeptical expression, eyebrows raised knowingly. “Did you brush your teeth?”

 

Isha nodded earnestly, her expression serious and solemn.

 

Jinx raised one eyebrow higher, crouching down to the girl’s level, giving her a smirk. “You sure about that, kiddo? Lemme smell your breath.”

 

Isha hesitated just a second, glancing nervously toward Sevika, but quickly stepped forward and obediently opened her mouth. Jinx leaned forward, inhaled exaggeratedly, and immediately let out a playful, dramatic groan.

 

“Ohhh, you liar!” she accused, shaking her head in mock disappointment. “I can smell it from here! You definitely didn’t brush those teeth!”

 

Isha squealed with laughter, trying to step backward, but Jinx was quicker. She scooped the girl up swiftly, dangling her upside down and making Isha burst into uncontrollable giggles. “You think you can fool me, huh?”

 

I did! Isha protested between fits of laughter, face flushing as she swung gently upside down in Jinx’s grip.

 

“Nice try, kid, but I’m not buying it!” Jinx declared dramatically, marching toward the doorway with Isha still hanging upside down, limbs flailing and laughter echoing down the hall. “Time for a supervised brushing session. Clearly, you can’t be trusted.”

 

Sevika watched them go, listening as the girl’s laughter faded gently into the distance. She shook her head slightly, allowing a quiet breath as she turned her eyes back toward the nearly finished prototype, its polished surfaces glinting under the workshop lights.

 

Sevika was still quietly untangling the wires when Jinx returned, Isha tucked securely in one arm, the girl’s face pressed sleepily into Jinx’s shoulder. Isha’s eyes were heavy, half-lidded as she struggled to stay awake, though she offered Sevika a small, tired smile as they passed.

 

“We’re hitting the hay, Sev,” Jinx announced quietly, shifting her grip on Isha. “Lock up behind you when you’re done.”

 

Sevika gave a short nod, glancing up briefly. “Got it.”

 

Isha lifted her hands slowly, sleepily signing a gentle goodnight toward Sevika. Sevika nodded back softly, voice quiet. “Night, kid.”

 

She watched as Jinx carried Isha toward the makeshift nest of blankets and pillows tucked into the corner. Sevika had witnessed Jinx’s softer side around the girl many times—the careful gestures, the protective glances, the patience she rarely showed anyone else—but she’d still expected bedtime to be more of a chaotic struggle, something messy and loud, closer to Jinx’s own childhood routines. Memories of Jinx as a child surfaced briefly: pillow fights that erupted without warning, endless rounds of jumping on the bed, and every conceivable excuse to delay sleep just a bit longer.

 

But this scene was surprisingly different. Jinx set Isha down gently, adjusting the blankets around her shoulders and quietly brushing back the girl’s hair with a tenderness Sevika had come to recognize but still found somewhat unexpected. She could hear their quiet exchange, Jinx’s voice soft yet playful as she negotiated bedtime terms.

 

“Alright, pick one story,” Jinx was murmuring firmly, clearly in response to Isha’s quiet signing. There was a brief pause before Jinx’s voice rose again, tinged with exaggerated exasperation. “Oh come on, that’s way too long to be a bedtime story. You’ll be asleep halfway through anyway.”

 

Another moment of quiet, and Sevika imagined Isha’s earnest protests, hands rapidly moving to argue her case.

 

“Fine, two stories, but they’re gonna be short ones,” Jinx relented grudgingly. Sevika could practically hear the playful pout in Jinx’s tone, even from across the room. “No way I’m falling asleep halfway through your favorite again.”

 

Another brief silence, filled only by Isha’s stubborn signing, made Jinx sigh dramatically.

 

“Fine. But if you pass out halfway, don’t blame me.”

 

Sevika continued quietly working, the wires in her hands momentarily forgotten as she listened to Jinx’s voice soften further, easing into the gentle, rhythmic cadence of storytelling. It wasn’t what she'd anticipated. No bouncing on beds, no pillow wars, no long list of stalling tactics—just a calm, quiet routine she hadn't realized Jinx was capable of.

 

She allowed herself another glance toward the corner, finding Isha already curled close to Jinx’s side, eyes closed, breathing steady, lulled quickly by the soft murmur of the story.

 

Sevika finished untangling the wires in silence, letting the unexpected calm settle around her, quietly recognizing that the chaotic child she remembered was now someone entirely different—a strange blend of chaos and calm that still managed to surprise her.

 

She quietly locked the door behind her, leaving Jinx’s gentle murmurs and Isha’s sleepy breathing behind.

 

She had to admit, parenthood changed Jinx for the better.

 

 

Sevika sat quietly behind the desk at the Last Drop, her gaze flicking occasionally toward Isha, who was perched comfortably in the corner of the office, crayons scattered around her as she meticulously colored on sheets of paper. The girl’s face was calm, absorbed fully in her task, completely unaware of the tension Sevika herself felt quietly building beneath her outward calm.

 

The low drone of conversation downstairs barely filtered through the heavy door, leaving the room strangely quiet. Jinx had been gone for nearly two hours, presenting her carefully assembled prototype to the chem-barons. Sevika had never particularly worried about Jinx’s ability to handle herself, but she’d still caught herself glancing toward the door more often than she’d admit.

 

Isha shifted slightly, carefully choosing another crayon, her small fingers gently shading in vibrant colors as Sevika watched. It was oddly soothing, the rhythmic scratch of wax on paper filling the silence, making Sevika’s own impatience more bearable.

 

Then, without warning, the calm shattered as the office door slammed open, nearly kicked from its hinges by an exuberant whirlwind of blue braids and manic energy. Jinx burst through the doorway, arms thrown triumphantly upward, eyes wide and shining with triumph.

 

“I did it!” she declared gleefully, practically vibrating with excitement. Her voice echoed through the room, loud enough to startle Isha, who immediately perked up from her drawing, wide eyes fixed eagerly on Jinx.

 

Sevika raised an eyebrow calmly, leaning back slightly in her chair. “That good, huh?”

 

Jinx smirked smugly, arms dropping to her sides as she sauntered further into the office, chin lifted proudly. “Oh, better than good. Those stuffy old chem-barons ate it up—hook, line, and sinker. We’re getting a full-scale water treatment plant built, Sev. Clean water for Zaun—officially approved.”

 

Sevika couldn’t help the faint huff of amusement that escaped her, shaking her head slightly at Jinx’s enthusiasm. “You don’t say.”

 

Jinx grinned even wider, eyes bright with barely-contained manic joy as she turned toward Isha, who was now standing, crayons abandoned as she eagerly moved toward Jinx, signing rapidly in excitement.

 

“You bet I did!” Jinx laughed, scooping Isha up and spinning her around, making the girl burst into delighted giggles. “Told you they’d listen! Who knew, right?”

 

Sevika remained seated, watching silently as Jinx set Isha down again, the girl immediately launching into a flurry of questions signed with enthusiastic gestures. Jinx nodded along, answering with exaggerated smugness, clearly basking in the triumph of proving everyone wrong.

 

It was a familiar sight, Jinx bursting with pride and chaotic excitement. Yet beneath it all, Sevika recognized something different—a genuine, purposeful confidence she hadn’t fully expected. She tilted her head slightly, regarding Jinx with a faint, amused expression.

 

“Guess you weren’t kidding about proving them wrong,” Sevika remarked dryly.

 

Jinx shot Sevika a playful glare, though her smug grin didn’t waver. “C’mon, Sev. You should know better by now. I always get my way eventually.”

 

Sevika shook her head slightly, settling back comfortably into the chair. “Never doubted it.”

 

Jinx rolled her eyes dramatically, scoffing as if Sevika’s belief was inevitable. “Obviously.”

 

But Sevika caught the fleeting moment of warmth beneath Jinx’s bravado—there and gone again almost immediately—as Jinx turned back toward Isha, eagerly answering another stream of signed questions.

 

Sevika leaned back quietly, letting Jinx have her moment, feeling something close to relief quietly settle into place. Chaos or not, Jinx was getting things done, and for the first time in a long while, it felt like Zaun might genuinely change for the better.
